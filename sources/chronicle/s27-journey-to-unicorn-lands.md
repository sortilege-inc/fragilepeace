## Narrative

!lede The arbitration ended while [[Doji Setsuna]] was away from the table, and it ended her way. The Imperial argument reached the post first — the [[Scorpion Clan|Scorpion]] were close enough behind that one more solid roll would have carried it — and the diamond mines pass to the Empire rather than to either clan. [[Kitsuki Kaage]] cited her reasoning as the most persuasive before him, and then did something better than citing it: he gave her the privilege of helping him draft the judgment itself, so the legal justification that goes to the capital is partly hers. It has already been dispatched, and the decision is public.

!note The table's own gloss on the alternative: one more Scorpion success and the most powerful clan in Rokugan would have been handed a strategic resource, which is a considerable way not to preserve a fragile peace.

[[Kitsuki Kaage]] leaves in the morning for [[Shiro Yogasha]] to report. He has no case for them at present and expects that to change.

They also settled what becomes of the man they brought into that castle in ropes. Sent back to a grandfather who might do something for him, he goes to the [[Castle of the Centipede]] to try for peace, and if he cannot find it there he goes to [[The Wall]] and takes his chances against the first enemy — which is hardly dishonourable, and is not what was wanted for him. The prisoner is to be deposited at the Centipede, which is one more errand on a list that now runs in three directions.

The thing she actually asked for is still unanswered. Her request was to have [[Shinjo Harunobu]]'s custody moved somewhere closer, and it has not been refused — *there has been no response yet* — and nobody knows how long a message takes to reach the front, let alone how long an answer takes to come back.

She knows where to write to him, which means she knows where he is, and it is the same place the question about the [[Lion Clan|Lion]] leads. The Lion army was last known to be besieging [[Gatherer of Wind's Castle]], one of the [[Ide Family|Ide]]'s principal fortresses — which they would not want at their backs while pushing deeper into [[Unicorn Clan|Unicorn]] country, and which may have fallen already. Whether that siege was sanctioned is a question an Emerald Magistrate is entitled to ask, and asking it puts her in the camp where her husband is held. The [[City of the Rich Frog]] is the border and the convenient crossing; the road runs northwest from it, which is almost certainly the way the Lion went.

Then a very tired [[Unicorn Messenger|Unicorn messenger]] found her leaving court. He had been sent to her old posting, then to [[Shiro Yogasha]], then told she was not there either; he had been riding for something like a month, and this was the last of many letters he had failed to deliver. He handed over a package wrapped in purple. *Your husband sends you a gift.* It had been sent weeks ago.

It is a book by [[Hana no Ame]] — a pen name, nobody knows who writes them, and the fiction is salacious in exactly the ways [[Doji Setsuna]] most appreciates. It is the most fashionable thing in the capital and it makes a serviceable pillow book. [[Shinjo Harunobu]] would never sit down and read a book himself; he is literate enough for military orders and does not enjoy it. He knows she reads, and he has been paying attention, and he is a great deal more sentimental than she is. He almost certainly bought it before he went to war. She accepted it in front of [[Bayushi Monban]] and opened it alone.

Their conversation afterwards was not comfortable. [[Bayushi Monban]] asked her to return the letter from the [[Lion Clan|Lion]] — [[Akodo Akihito]]'s answer on her husband, which had not quite refused the request so much as gently observed that it was not a good look. She gave it back. He is still sore: *if he was looking out for me, he would have given me the yoriki I requested.*

[[Shiba Midori]] petitioned [[Kitsu Takeko]] to take [[Kitsu Ayoko]] as her yoriki, and made a poor job of the asking. She stumbled over her words, did not project the confidence she wanted, and her naturally soft voice would not carry across a court; the daimyō had to ask her to repeat herself more than once. It was a wretched few minutes. And then she got everything she wanted anyway — *of course, you are an Emerald Magistrate, and you may choose your assistants as you see fit* — with [[Kitsu Ayoko]] directed to serve her until she says otherwise. What [[Shiba Midori]] took away from it was a note about Kitsu Takeko's court: the courtiers who get their way do it with flattery and flowery words, and [[Kitsu Takeko]] is far more easily moved by wit and charm than by a correct petition. [[Kitsu Ayoko]]'s own face lit up when the request was made, which is not what a samurai's face is supposed to do.

[[Shiba Midori]] then performed the tea ceremony for [[Kitsu Takeko]], and did that beautifully, and everyone who attended left lighter for it.

During that ceremony [[Bayushi Monban]] watched the daimyō take delivery of a letter containing a hastily drawn portrait, and recognised it: the mahōtsukai who escaped them at [[Slowtide Harbor]], which is most of a country away, on the far side of [[Lion Clan|Lion]] lands. What he actually noticed was her face. It was horror and recognition together. She knows who that is.

His own post was worse. The missive he had received in [[Lion Clan|Lion]] lands about his betrothed was not from her at all — the [[Crab Clan|Crab]] who came north to recruit could place her at [[The Wall]] on a date that makes it impossible, and the letter carries only a Crab chop, the mark of a military post rather than any person's signature. Somebody wrote to him in his betrothed's name to draw him south.

The real letter from her arrived that same evening, and it is worse again. She wants to know what happened at the Topaz Championship — *aside from the fact that you didn't win and some lame Crane won* — and then she tells him why she has been sent over [[The Wall]]: the [[Sword of Hiruma]] has been stolen from the Hiruma's new castle, and the clan is nearly certain it was taken by servants of the first enemy, and nobody knows what for.

The whole wing heard the roar and the fist hitting the floor. [[Doji Setsuna]], a few pages into her new book, sat up, registered it, and went back to reading on the understanding that she would hear all about it shortly. [[Bayushi Monban]] told [[Ryu]] and [[So]] that unless it was a meal, or the Lady Doji leaving, or one of the magistrates, nobody was to disturb him — and they caught a glimpse of the green snowflakes behind the mask and were frightened of him for the first time. He is normally the one person they are not afraid of.

The sword matters to him for reasons that are not about the sword. It is one of the very few things recovered from the destruction of the Castle of Hiruma. A Hiruma physician cared for him through a long illness; the Hiruma are the ones who take new arrivals at [[The Wall]] in hand, show them around, introduce them, and treat them — and they did that for a sickly boy who would not have survived without it. *I would have married one if I hadn't met my betrothed.* The Hiruma have been kicked enough, and they had finally been given one good thing back. He went to meditate instead of leaving the room, on the grounds that if anyone spoke to him he would take their head off, and set himself to learning a ritual called the Wayfarer's Path.

He has given his word they go west first. He wants to go south.

There is something else he is carrying and has decided to carry quietly. He is angry with [[Shiba Midori]] — his big sister, as he calls her — for putting the sealed letter into evidence rather than handing it to him, and angry with [[Akodo Akihito]] for sending it to her in the first place. He also thinks she is doing her best, and that she has earned the chance to explain herself, and he intends to say his piece somewhere on the road west and not wreck the thing between them doing it.

They left the next morning with the [[Three Hida Brothers]], who had come north to recruit for [[The Wall]] and found an empty castle, the whole army being away west. Departure was marked by [[Kitsu Ayoko]] chasing a badly dressed servant out of [[Shiba Midori]]'s quarters with a broom, it being the handiest thing available — *they're a servant, I shouldn't beat them with a katana, that's not fair.* The servant had been going through her things. [[Shiba Midori]] followed and found an empty corridor that smelled faintly of sulphur and held a little fog. Whatever they were looking for, they did not find it — [[Kitsu Ayoko]] had not so much as glanced at the things she had been told to guard.

On the road [[Bayushi Monban]] set the [[Crab Clan|Crab]] to watch the horizon for anyone following, and to treat whoever they found as adversaries rather than enemies. Then he put himself between [[Kitsu Ayoko]] and [[Shiba Midori]] and delivered a lecture aimed at both of them. The girl had lit up like a lantern festival when she was chosen, in a room full of people who read faces for a living. He forgave her, generously, for accusing him during the hearing — and had to be told that she had done no such thing, that it was [[Ide Tsubame]] who called him a forger and a thief, and that [[Kitsu Ayoko]]'s distress on the stand had been his own doing.

The rest of it was better than it started. She should be mindful of how she behaves, he told her, because even he finds that difficult; if her conduct embarrasses the people who have put their necks out for her she will have a serious problem, and he will do everything in his power to halt the blade. She has a duty to learn as much as she can and become a renowned samurai and shugenja, and there are not many in her position. If she wants counsel she may have it, in confidence — the same confidence he would give [[Shiba Midori]]. And if he is the one starting to slip she may give him the eye, because they are travelling together now and will have to trust one another.

[[Doji Setsuna]] did a fine job of concealing her disdain for the jumped-up peasant.

Then he told them everything: the forged letter meant to draw him toward [[The Wall]], their history with the Blue Flame and with mahōtsukai, the theft of the [[Sword of Hiruma]], the increase in the first enemy's minions in the [[Shinomen Forest]] — *you remember the opening, the one uncle failed to close; I believe we have found where it opened* — and [[Shiba Midori]]'s outstanding journey to the Elemental Masters, which he promised would be next with no further detours.

What is known of the sword is this. The Hiruma lands are on the far side of the Kaiu Wall now, wholly swallowed by the Shadowlands. The Hiruma made their last stand at the old castle, and the enemy has never been able to fully occupy it — a shugenja is said to have laid something on the place that denied them the fortress, and denied them the ability to drain or corrupt the souls of the Hiruma dying to hold it. Their flesh was taken; their souls were not. The sword is one of the blades forged for the clans before the [[Unicorn Clan|Unicorn]] brought meishodo back to Rokugan, made so well that it carried supernatural properties without a shugenja's hand in it, and it stayed pure in the middle of a hell. The last person known to carry it is rumoured to be that same shugenja — who was not born Hiruma, but married into the family.

And they made a pact. [[Bayushi Monban]] asked what they were going to do about their little incident, and they agreed to keep it quiet until he can speak to his champion: not the fact that they died, and not the part where they lived through the Battle of the [[Snow Plain]] from inside their own ancestors. With one carve-out he made explicit — a lord asking a vassal what happened to them is a reasonable question, and nobody is being asked to lie to their own lord. The rule is that it is not offered to everyone who asks.

He also changed how he travels. He has been pleasant to [[Doji Setsuna]]'s retinue, and engaging, letting them lead the conversation about places and people and their own interests. She treats her people extremely well and does not fraternise with them; he does, on the grounds that they all serve the Emperor in their own way, and [[Ryu]] has had to remind him more than once that he is not supposed to speak to them like that. He trains with the [[Crab Clan|Crab]] daily and they are giving him work. The man they were told about was a sickly boy who coughed and needed his tea; the man in front of them is nearly as large as the youngest brother and keeps up all day. They have noticed. *The situation has changed* is all he will say, which is not the truth and is not a lie.

Passing through [[Ikoma Family|Ikoma]] country they learned that [[Ikoma Ichigo]] will present the true story of the [[Snow Plain]] at the next winter court, that he is looking forward to it, and that he understands some Emerald Magistrates had a hand in recovering it.

The [[City of the Rich Frog]] sits at the junction of the Three Sides River and the [[Drowned Merchant River]], and is one of Rokugan's great river ports — the trade between [[Unicorn Clan|Unicorn]], [[Dragon Clan|Dragon]] and [[Lion Clan|Lion]] passes through it. It is three cities. The [[Unicorn Clan|Unicorn]] hold the western bank with a wharf walled in polished black granite, violet roofs behind it, and a tower at the southern end said to be tall enough to put an arrow across the river. The [[Dragon Clan|Dragon]] have the north bank and no wharf to speak of — a line of shrines along the water, and a modest neglected jetty the [[Phoenix Clan|Phoenix]] sometimes use, each side of it standing for a month of the year with a lantern moved around it to mark the date. The [[Lion Clan|Lion]] have one of their largest trade hubs and no riverbank at all: the bank is a defensive wall, and cargo comes and goes through a canal with half a dozen gates. It is ingenious and it is extremely defensible.

[[Doji Setsuna]] remembered a teahouse there, memorable for the tea and the company and for having no name — it has stood since before there was a village here and changed hands so often the original name was lost, so that when anyone says *the* teahouse everyone knows which one is meant.

Six samurai were already in the sitting room, in three pairs. A young man in [[Dragon Clan|Dragon]] colours with a monk, and the young man is unmistakably [[Kitsuki Wataru]]'s son. Two young women in a corner who look a great deal like [[Ide Tsubame]] — Ide merchants, cousins perhaps. And a pair of [[Lion Clan|Lion]]: a young woman who is not local, and an older man dressed more like a town guardsman than a samurai who nonetheless wears two swords, and whose face [[Doji Setsuna]] pointed out to [[Shiba Midori]] because the resemblance to Uncle is uncanny.

[[Ikoma Kotone]], the tea mistress, welcomed them all and remembered [[Doji Setsuna]] on sight. *And where is that rakish young man you were with last time? I certainly hope he is staying out of harm's way in the current circumstances.* — *He is doing his duty as he ought.*

[[Ikoma Kotone]] knows everything about everyone and will not simply tell it. She treats it as a game, and to get anything out of her one has to play, and generally to bring something of one's own.

The older [[Lion Clan|Lion]] is Karu Akiara, formerly Lion himself, now of the Karu — a family of ronin who provide security in the [[City of the Rich Frog]]. They belong to none of the three clans here and answer to the Miya governor directly, which has been true so long that they now function as a samurai family: insular, tight, looking after their own, jealous of their interests, aligned with the governor's wishes and with the peace of the city, and not at all opposed to doing the work roughly. [[Kitsu Ayoko]] identified the young woman with them as a [[Matsu Family|Matsu]] on sight. [[Doji Setsuna]] noticed something else about her — a quality that put her in mind of [[Bayushi Monban]]'s inability to keep his mouth shut when he is trying to lie. Either bluntness, or lying does not come naturally.

Every group in the room was working [[Ikoma Kotone]], and all of them had the look. It is a strange position for an Emerald Magistrate: watching other people play the investigative game. [[Shiba Midori]] read their lips and sorted it out. The [[Unicorn Clan|Unicorn]] woman, talking past a companion who was not listening: *we are going to find her.* The [[Lion Clan|Lion]] woman: *my lord will not be pleased if we do not find this scurrilous person and persuade them not to slander our clan anymore.* And the [[Dragon Clan|Dragon]] monk to his young magistrate: *this was where I met the person you were inquiring about.* Three teams, all hunting, all at the most connected woman in the city. She passed it along in cadence, without much subtlety.

Then [[Ikoma Kotone]] let something slip to the Ide woman: [[Hana no Ame]] has been in this teahouse recently, and was meeting [[Tonbo Kuma]], the [[Dragonfly Clan]] shrine keeper from the [[Dragon Clan|Dragon]] district, and asking a great many questions. Research, presumably, for the next book.

[[Kitsuki Wataru]]'s son came over rather than be stared at any longer. [[Bayushi Monban]] told him he had been under his father's tutelage recently, and asked whether he had come home — he has not, and the last anyone heard he was travelling through [[Phoenix Clan|Phoenix]] lands with some young Emerald Magistrates, and then nothing. Write to his mother, [[Bayushi Monban]] said, and perhaps to the [[Dragonfly Clan|Dragonfly]]; the man is on his way; they had parted a little south of here. *Do not be surprised at his change. Do not ask him about it.* The son said his father had been called back by his lord the Ruby Champion, who needed his expertise, and had been thoroughly bored in retirement anyway. Told his father is quite a character, he rolled his eyes: *you don't need to tell me, he raised me.*

He serves as a [[Dragon Clan|Dragon]] magistrate and hopes to be an Emerald Magistrate one day. And he is here on business: there is a trade on the black market in this city in [[Meishodo Talismans]], the [[Unicorn Clan|Unicorn]]'s own magic, which the Unicorn guard so closely that its appearance for sale is itself the anomaly. [[Tonbo Kuma]] brought him because the shrine keeper had met a woman asking after such a talisman and wanting to understand how it worked — the novelist, apparently, researching. There are also fox in the city, and they may be in it somewhere. The concern is both that the things should not be loose where they can be misused, and that anyone is trading them at all.

[[Bayushi Monban]] warned him plainly, and let his Emerald Magistrate badge show at his collar while he did it: if the [[Unicorn Clan|Unicorn]] learn that someone is copying their art, or that one of their own is selling it, this becomes dangerous. He should attach himself to people he trusts, and call on them if he needs to.

[[Shiba Midori]] said nothing at all, with visible effort. [[Doji Setsuna]] watched her do it. Some of what killed her the last time runs through exactly this — a council so occupied with policing other clans' magic that it did not stop what it should have stopped — and binding a spirit into a sheet of paper offends her twice over, once for the spirit and once for the idea that each clan should hoard its own art when knowledge ought to be shared. If shugenja are selling magic for money, and if people who are not shugenja are buying talismans, that is a short road to mahōtsukai, and on that much she and [[Bayushi Monban]] agree entirely.

[[Doji Setsuna]] took the [[Lion Clan|Lion]] pair with [[Bayushi Monban]] as her foil, deflecting to him, letting him be unpleasant and then softening it herself. It made the woman more guarded rather than less — and it worked anyway, not by beating her caution but by breaking her concentration, until she gave up the whole of it. [[Hana no Ame]]'s next book concerns an illicit affair between a [[Lion Clan|Lion]] and a spirit, and her lord wants it not to exist. *Why did you have to pick us? Leave us out of it.* She thinks the assignment is nonsense and is a samurai, so she is doing it.

*You mean there is basis in fact for the story?* — which was cruel and effective.

The advice [[Doji Setsuna]] gave her was real. A book already written and printed is one problem; a book not yet written is another and a better one, and the answer there is to offer an alternative subject rather than a prohibition, because it is easier to redirect a moving thing than to stop it. Perhaps the character could be a [[Dragon Clan|Dragon]]. And the offer of help was genuine, for her own reasons: she would like there to be more books, and she would like the next one not to be about ghosts, which is not her favourite subject at present.

Then she asked what that famous Lion book was called, and was looked at as though she were the least informed person in the Empire. *Leadership.* Is that not just a book? — *It is not a book. It is the book.* As for [[Bayushi]]'s *Lies*, the Lion consensus was that it is better suited to a use they were too polite to name until [[Doji Setsuna]] named it for them.

*The funny thing,* said [[Bayushi Monban]], *is that when Bayushi wrote Lies, he was telling the truth. Lies is full of truth.* It had been a school assignment — read and comment on Lord [[Bayushi]]'s most famous work — and that was his commentary, and he was well praised for it.

Before they left he told [[Kitsuki Wataru]]'s son to keep himself safe, being important to both his parents. The young man observed that he has no yojimbo yet and might have to requisition one, though [[Bayushi Monban]] is surely too highly ranked to ask — *indeed, and also currently taken*. He will be careful, he said, and he will still get his results, because if these things are being sold here the window needs closing.

*Believe me, I feel like the only Scorpion in this city.* — *You are by far not the only one in the city.* — *I am sure I will have to pay one for information before this is over.*

*You are so like your father.* — *I will take that as a compliment, and accept that it is how it was intended.*

## Setsuna

**She won it, and she got to write it.** The mines are the Empire's, her reasoning is cited in the ruling, and [[Kitsuki Kaage]] handed her the drafting of the judgment — which is worth more than the citation, because a judgment she helped write is a precedent shaped in her own hand and going to the capital under a senior Kitsuki's seal. Six sessions of argument produced the finding she was aiming at from the moment she found the edict on dormant strategic resources.

**And the thing she actually wanted is still nowhere.** No refusal on her husband's custody, no acceptance, no answer at all — and a messenger who spent a month failing to find her is the whole explanation. Her office moves rulings; it does not move gaolers, and it does not move roads. What has actually moved [[Shinjo Harunobu]] before was obligation rather than authority, and the [[Unicorn Clan|Unicorn]] lands are where obligation lives. That is the real reason to be going west, and the [[Lion Clan|Lion]] siege is the reason she can say out loud.

The book tells her more than she has let herself notice. He is well enough to send something, allowed to send it, and handled by people who let a parcel through. He bought it before the war and it took a month to arrive — so the silence on her request is probably distance and not refusal. That is genuinely good news and she should let it be good news.

**What she should watch in herself is the disdain.** [[Kitsu Ayoko]] is a peasant-born girl who summoned an ancestor in open court while compromised, and gave that hearing its one clean moral finding, and [[Doji Setsuna]] is expending effort on hiding what she thinks of her. Meanwhile [[Bayushi Monban]] — a [[Scorpion Clan|Scorpion]] with a disdain for courtesy and no patience for anyone — pulled the girl aside, corrected her without humiliating her, offered her counsel in confidence, and promised to halt the blade if it came for her. He was wrong about the facts and right about the person. That comparison does not flatter the [[Crane Clan|Crane]].

**The pact is more defensible than it looks, and still leaky.** Not volunteering that they died, or that they lived the [[Snow Plain]] from inside their ancestors, is reasonable — and [[Bayushi Monban]] carved out the obvious exception himself, so nobody is being asked to lie to their own lord. The leak is that they already testified to all of it, in open court, in front of [[Kitsuki Kaage]], both advocates, the [[Kitsu Family|Kitsu]] daimyō and a room of witnesses, and [[Ikoma Ichigo]] is about to read the recovered history at winter court. The secret is not a secret. What the pact can still do is control the telling, and that is worth having — but it should be held as a discipline about emphasis, not mistaken for concealment.

Three things are converging on this city and she should treat them as one thing until proven otherwise. A mahōtsukai [[Kitsu Takeko]] recognises on sight. A forged letter designed to draw [[Bayushi Monban]] toward [[The Wall]]. And an illicit trade in [[Meishodo Talismans]] — bound spirits, sold to people who are not shugenja — in a river port where three clans meet. She has seen a bound spirit in an object once before, on a black talisman [[Matsu Morozane]] cut from a [[Unicorn Clan|Unicorn]] shugenja's neck three centuries ago. That is the second time bound spirits in objects have come near her.

And there is a novelist at the centre of it, asking a shrine keeper how the talismans work, which is either the least sinister explanation available or the most.

Two smaller notes. [[Bayushi Monban]] is going to go south whatever anyone says, and has given his word to go west first; she should spend that word carefully, because he will keep it and it will cost him. And the older [[Lion Clan|Lion]] at the teahouse has Uncle's face. [[Shiba Midori]] saw it too.
