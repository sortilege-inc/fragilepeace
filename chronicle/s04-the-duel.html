The Duel

Session 4·2025-04-01

Neither lord had sanctioned the duel, so Kakita Kazumi set terms that could not produce a corpse — until one man taps out, any weapon or armour a man owns, no fatal blow.

Shinjō Mono carried word to the Ide at Otosan Uchi that the first Matsu incursion into Unicorn land had been thrown back. What he was told in return is the thing that matters: the Emperor knows what is happening on that border and has made no declaration either way. Until he speaks, or until the two clans settle it between themselves, the war continues by default, and nobody is obliged to answer for a war that continues by default.

He came back with an Emerald Magistrate's badge newly around his neck, and instead of going the long way round through Dragon lands and up the river he cut straight across for Loyalty Castle. Lion outriders met him on the road. They were ready to take him prisoner; the badge turned it into an escort, and the journey was the same either way. At the castle he found Doji Setsuna already there, and the two of them talked while Lion kept a distance and watched without pretending otherwise.

The courtyard had been busy that afternoon. Bayushi Monban had insulted the daimyō's kitchen and a number of Lion bushi had set about him for it — more of them, and faster, than the offence seemed to warrant. A monk stepped in with the Dragon teaching, neither shall I, and the offer that goes with it — if every blow is given to him, give one to me — and the bushi tried to game it, laying a solid blow on Bayushi Monban and a light tap on the monk. The monk declined to accept that as an answer. The arithmetic never recovered, and the commotion of Shinjō Mono's arrival gave Bayushi Monban the chance to walk out of it. He kept the bruises. He also kept the belief that the insult had been offered to him first.

That evening Matsu Koda challenged Kakita Kazumi, with a warning that cowardly terms — first blow, say — would not stop him having satisfaction one way or another. Kakita Kazumi had no idea who the man was and could not place him; he searched his memory for the family and came up with nothing. He set the terms anyway, and set them carefully: until one man taps out, any weapon or armour a man owns, no fatal blow. Neither lord had sanctioned this, and a corpse would have made it a matter for magistrates rather than for the two of them. Matsu Koda accepted without argument.

Then Kakita Kazumi arrived a minute short of the hour at which he would have forfeited, hair deliberately wrecked, yawning and stretching — I apologise, my Lion friend. Where are we supposed to have a duel today? — and put on Topaz Champion armour in front of the crowd with a Topaz Champion katana at his hip. The provocation did not land the way he wanted, though it cost Matsu Koda his composure a little. The armour was for the crowd, and it went on before the first exchange.

None of it worked. Kakita Kazumi opened well — Crossing Blade, a clean hit, six damage against three of armour, and an opportunity spent to draw the yoroi-dōshi from his hip sheath, the armour-piercing knife his grandfather gave him. Matsu Koda swung and missed, and Kakita Kazumi spun the knife in his hand and knocked the katana aside. Then Matsu Koda took the miss back, and answered with Striking as Water, which strips a man's resistance so that the next blow does more work than it should. He pressed and kept pressing. A critical strike came through and left Kakita Kazumi with damaged armour and a close call. One of the watching Lion stepped closer as Kakita Kazumi gave ground.

The end was not a misjudged attack so much as a lost temper. Kakita Kazumi went for Flashing Steel Strike, all spun blades and a stumble made to look like an attack that was not one — and ran out of composure in the middle of it, which handed Matsu Koda a finishing blow. It put him flat, and Matsu Koda kicked him over onto his back and set a blade at his throat. He yielded. Doji Setsuna and Shinjō Mono watched from among three Lion minders and a number of the bushi who had beaten Bayushi Monban a few hours earlier.

Then my problem with you is settled, Matsu Koda said. But I do not speak for the other four. They can take their own flesh once I have rested.

Doji Setsuna closed that on the spot, from custom: a duel resolved answers for the whole of the offended party, not for one man of it. This is not your business, Doji. You were not the one who lamed my brother. — It is my business to ensure that duels are conducted according to tradition.

Kakita Kazumi, flat on his back and in no hurry to get up, supplied the other half. He asked Shiba Midori, across the yard, what the Lion at the Castle of the Emerald Magistrates had actually said to her that started all this — something about an insult to Shiba Midori's ancestors, and a very vulgar pass at her. How unlike a Lion to forget words so carelessly given when the belly is full of sake. There is a whole host of people at the Castle of the Emerald Magistrates, the Ruby Champion among them, who can attest to what was spoken.

Matsu Koda went red. It was plainly new to him. He bowed awkwardly to Kakita Kazumi — I was not informed of the circumstances in which you wounded my brother. I regret that I have humbled you without cause — and then bowed to Doji Setsuna as well: I hope you will overlook the fact that this was unwarranted. I was misinformed. Kakita Kazumi told him he was aware of the history of the Matsu and of the particular ways that family likes to see things through where a Kakita is concerned, and then, if it makes you feel all the better, Kakita Kazumi said, I have suffered worse for less. Doji Setsuna closed it with if Kakita Kazumi is satisfied, then so am I.

It was too late to set out, so there was dinner, and the party was seated at the daimyō's own table among the senior courtiers — not as a courtesy to rank but because they were the best gossip in the building and everyone wanted to be near them.

Shiba Midori went to work on two junior Kitsu shrine maidens at the next table, who were talking behind fans to signal that it was private and doing nothing to make it so. With a theology reading ready she could tell their aura was clean: idle rather than corrupt, layabouts and not criminals. They asked where she had come from, and were delighted by the answer — the far north of the Phoenix Lands, almost as far north as Rokugan goes — how horrible, there must be no men out there. She allowed there had been one or two worth looking at while she was studying, and that he was doubtless married with three children by now. They were sorry about the Kakita, on the grounds that Matsu Koda is big and strong and rather thick. When she suggested they might also help patch up the Scorpion, both fans went up at once: certainly not, they had no wish to offend their lord, and a man bruised for offending him deserves it — your friend the Kakita did not deserve what he got. The other one did. Then they wanted to know about Shinjō Mono, and whether he really smells of horse hair and saddle sweat. Shiba Midori said she had only known him a few hours and could hardly be an expert, and left them with the observation that he does have nice shoulders.

Doji Setsuna spent the dinner on her husband. She went to the Ikoma Bard she had dealt with two days before, on the reasoning that a well-connected man on good terms is worth more than the right man on none, and asked for something narrower than a release: a letter of support for an exchange, which she could carry closer to the front, and which she was careful to frame as not conflicting with her duties as a magistrate. She was asked, drily, what her function had been before the badge. The answer was the sort of middling city bureaucrat that the Empire runs on.

The refusal was polite and total. Requests for the release of hostages belong after hostilities conclude, and these have barely begun — and it is rumoured the Emerald Championship itself may be delayed, because the champions of both clans are committed to the fighting. Doji Setsuna noted that a man does not mention how long a war will run unless he means the remark to be heard, and let it pass. She clarified instead: she was not asking after a hostage so much as a husband, if the distinction meant anything. I have every sympathy. But your husband could not possibly be in a safer place than the custody of the Lion army. He will not be harmed, and he is in no further danger of dying in a field, until the matter resolves. — You do not know the Unicorn. He will suffocate without the sky above him. The Unicorn needs open sky as the Lion needs the din of battle.

There is a second road, and the table found it while looking for the first. Hostages are ransomed against status, and Shinjō Harunobu's status is thirty. Between them, Kakita Kazumi and Bayushi Monban are carrying most of what that would take.

Kakita Kazumi paid for the seating by performing, and gave the courtiers the Phoenix Lands, the fight in the rain and the ninja, with Bayushi Monban's heroics embellished well past what happened — outnumbered, outmatched, standing between half a dozen of them. Nobody in that room had heard a word about a plague. They very much wanted to hear more about the blue flame ninja.

Then a plate came to Bayushi Monban with a conspicuously phallic cut of meat on it and a second note under it. It was a love poem: the writer had not expected to see him back so soon, hoped he had enjoyed the treat sent with breakfast, and was sending the choicest cut the kitchen could manage so that he would be ready to take care of her all night — and would be waiting under the Cherry Tree. Signed Yushi Sama. He took out the first note, smelled cherry blossom on it, and understood two things at once. The testicles in his soup had been a gift. He had insulted his hosts for nothing.

That cost him six honour on the spot, for a breach of courtesy, and it put him back under thirty — which means he has his contempt for courtesy back, having only recently got rid of it. If you had just let me take my beating, everything would have been fine.

Doji Setsuna had already caught the scent on him earlier, standing over Kakita Kazumi in the yard, and asked whether he was wearing perfume. It is a day scent. She observed that it was an odd thing on a Scorpion pretending to be a Crab. He admitted to having received a few missives.

He told her he was going, and why — that the advances had to stop, that he is betrothed, and that whoever it is either does not know or is testing him. She offered him Kakita Kazumi. He declined, and went alone. Kakita Kazumi followed anyway, at a distance, in case it was a trap, and was never noticed.

It was not a trap. A falsetto came out of the dark under the tree — my dear Bayushi, I had not expected to see you back so soon; did you enjoy the presents I sent you? — and would not come out of the shadows, and claimed to have shared his bed a fortnight ago. He could not read the speaker at all. What he could tell, by other means, was that nothing about it was supernatural and everything about it was wrong: the timing did not fit, and whoever this was, they were not talking to him. Does the moonlight deceive me, or have you changed your mask? Have you mistaken me for another? — I am not who you think. I am his younger brother, the third, the cursed one, sent south.

She stepped into the light and was a catamite, powdered and painted, in a woman's kimono, who had got all dressed up and sent his favourites. On learning which brother he had, the admirer fled, and the scent of cherry blossom hung on after. The earlier note was from the same hand: saccharine love poetry about his brother, and about his brother's tantō. Bayushi Monban took it about as well as could be expected and resolved to beat his brother nearly to death for leading people on. Kakita Kazumi, satisfied that no one was going to be killed, went back to bed with a new pillow book to write and something to hold over a Scorpion for years.

So: rude to his host, beaten for it, over a misunderstanding and a case of mistaken identity with his brother.

The letter of passage reached Shinjō Mono in the morning, clearing the party onto the Lion Roads toward the Castle of the Swift Sword — and Shinjō Mono argued for speed and against sleeping in another Lion fortress, while Doji Setsuna argued for the castles. Kakita Kazumi conceded to her on the grounds that it is what magistrates do. The plan they settled on was Crossroads Fortress the first day, past Rugashi the second, and the castle on the third, arriving rested rather than arriving early and wrecked. There is no hurry that cannot absorb a day: Kitsuki Kaage is held up at least a week waiting on ambassadors, and what he wants from her before he arrives is a preliminary report.

They travelled in ordinary clothes rather than armour, because riding through another clan's land in war gear announces a doubt that they can protect their guests. Lion escorts went with them through several daimyō's domains of Lion lands. At every postal station the papers came out and Lion Clan officials read Shinjō Mono more carefully than they read the papers. Columns went north past them and gave way to the chrysanthemum. There was no banditry, because the Lion would treat banditry as target practice.

Kakita Kazumi foraged and found what he was looking for, which was not a killing poison but a concentrate that would take a man out of a room rather than out of the world — for anyone who might prove an inconvenience to the group. Nobody could close Bayushi Monban's wounds: he has light wounds in his three highest rings, he would only be treated by Shiba Midori to begin with, and when it turned out Kakita Kazumi is the better physician of the two, he failed at it too. Bayushi Monban will carry them until they reach someone who can. He also spent a night failing to compose a formal letter and then managed it on the road, wound and all, having quietly studied Kakita Kazumi's paperwork to get the signs and seals in the right places.

Shiba Midori asked the shrine maidens what the kami are like where they are going. Well tended, and they should be very responsive; a predominance of fire, with plenty of water and earth beneath. The Castle of the Swift Sword stands in Kitsu country. The road was good and nothing happened on it. The investigation would begin when they arrived.

Setsuna

Two things came out of Loyalty Castle and neither cost her anything.

The first was the custom. Matsu Koda had settled with Kakita Kazumi and reserved four more grievances; she closed all four by naming the rule that a duel answers for the whole offended party. It held — Koda bowed to her as well as to Kakita Kazumi. The Lion at this castle will now have a harder time reopening the matter piecemeal, and she has established in front of Lion witnesses that she argues from custom rather than from clan.

The second was a refusal, which is still information. She asked the Ikoma Bard for a letter supporting Shinjō Harunobu's release — pressed as her own suit, deliberately narrower than a hostage exchange — and was told to wait, because such requests belong after hostilities and her husband is safest where he is. That is the standing answer, and anyone below the rank that can overrule it will give her the same one. What came with it was a rumour that the Emerald Championship may be delayed because the clan champions are committed to the war, which is a remark about duration and was probably meant to be heard as one. She answered that Shinjō Harunobu would suffocate without the sky, and kept the conversation warm.

The thing worth acting on is the one nobody said out loud. Hostages are ransomed against status, and his is thirty. Kakita Kazumi and Bayushi Monban are carrying most of that sum between them right now. The courteous road is closed for the duration of a war nobody will date; the commercial one is open and costed, and it does not require anyone's permission. It does require her to ask two colleagues for their money, and it still ends in a man being bought back rather than winning his way out — which is the same objection as the letter, wearing different clothes.

And she should be honest about what she is doing here. This was a political marriage. What she feels for Shinjō Harunobu is duty with real warmth in it and no fire — a long way above pure instrumentality and a long way short of a woman who will not rest until he is free. The trouble is that at a Lion table those two things look identical, and she has now been seen making the attempt. It cannot be said she was not trying to be a good wife. It also cannot be said she was, and she is the only person at that table who knows the difference.

She did not learn what happened under the Cherry Tree. She knew he was going, and why, and offered him Kakita Kazumi for it; he went alone, and Kakita Kazumi followed anyway. What she has is a Scorpion who suddenly smells of cherry blossom and admits to correspondence.
