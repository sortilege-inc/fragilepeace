Winter's Wrath on the Battlefield

Session 13·2025-06-17

A path to workings that were on no map, and the snow over it swept deliberately flat. Somebody had been up there, and had taken the trouble not to be followed.

The blizzard came down at dawn and made the whole field one flat white sheet. That cut both ways and nobody could agree which way it cut hardest: the Unicorn Clan know what is under that snow and the Lion Clan do not, but the fresh fall also buried the markers the Unicorn had set to give themselves the edge. What it certainly did was hide every hazard on the ground from both armies at once.

Ikoma Akuyaku came into the General’s Tent on a stretcher with his feet ruined by frostbite and handed over a map. The Unicorn were undaunted, in good spirits and ready; their cavalry was late or lost somewhere in the storm; their strength was about equal to the Lion army's. And the cold out there was severe enough to take men without anyone fighting them.

Akodo Toronoko — his wife, and a commander in her own right — went for balms and came back with boiling sage water and a mortar paste, and worked it into his feet while the medic Akodo Kayamayako called for gentle fire, carefully controlled, to bring the limb back. He got some feeling into it. He was confined to camp and told what a second exposure would cost him. Akodo Kagekatsu chose that moment to tell Akodo Toronoko that her husband's intelligence work had been inadequate, and she did not give an inch of it back to him.

Then Ikoma Akuyaku left his tent anyway, on those feet, to watch the battle.

The general's plan was a flanking force through the mountains to come at the Unicorn from behind — no cavalry, not in that country, but mountain-born ashigaru: archers under Kitsu Somalia and spearmen under Matsu Morozane. Akodo Toronoko's archers held the left below, Akodo Kagekatsu the right.

The flanking force went up along the old mine paths. At the Frozen River the ice took Kitsu Somalia's archers — it broke, one of them died in it and four more went into the water. Matsu Morozane's spearmen crossed dry.

And then, higher up, Matsu Morozane found a path he had not been told about, running to workings that were on no map anyone in that army carried. The snow over it had been disturbed and then deliberately swept flat again. Somebody had used that path recently and had gone to the trouble of covering it. He sent scouts.

Below, Akodo Toronoko's archers put a volley into the fortified hill, pinned the enemy leader where he stood and put panic through his people. Then the flanking force came down off the mountain into the counter-attack: several Unicorn cohorts, and among them a shugenja everybody recognised, juggling fire on horseback. Kitsu Somalia threw a volley at that cohort before it could close and the forest ate most of it. Three cohorts hit Matsu Morozane's spearmen at once, killed a great many of them, and broke the rest into panic.

He could not rally them. The command techniques would not take. So he went forward at the head of what was left instead, which is the other way of doing it. Kitsu Somalia told her archers to put the bows down and draw swords, and they went in and did real damage. Matsu Morozane took a Lightning Raid into the shugenja's cohort and opened it.

Word came up from below that Matsu Sakura had taken the Central Hill and driven the Unicorn off it — and that the Unicorn on that hill had been thin, because their units had gone to answer the flank. The hill was bought with the mountain force's casualties. Both armies were still on the field.

Setsuna

This is the moment the mines enter the record, and they enter it as a secret somebody was keeping.

Matsu Morozane came on a path to workings that were on no map, and the snow over that path had been walked and then swept. Not weather — work. Somebody had been up there in the days before two armies met below, and had taken the trouble not to be followed. Neither the Lion nor the Unicorn knew what was on that hill. Somebody did.

Hold that against Shoshuro Amane at the parley. A Scorpion who knows what the hill is worth, who wants the fighting stopped before either army wanders up it, who will not be read, and who leaves quietly when refused — and a covered path on the same hill in the same week. She is the reading. The memory does not prove she made those tracks, and the memory is not evidence anyone will accept anyway.

What she can use is the shape of it, and the shape is that the Scorpion's claim to the mines is older than the battle. Their case is not that they won the ground. It is that they already knew, and were already using it, and said nothing while two clans bled at the foot of the hill. That is a strong claim in a deed and an unspeakable one in a courtroom, which is why Shoshuro Aishi will never put it that way.

The other thing to carry: the whole flanking manoeuvre went up along the old mine paths. Those workings were the road. That is a fact about the terrain that no account of this battle records, and it is checkable — a road leaves marks that outlast a war.
