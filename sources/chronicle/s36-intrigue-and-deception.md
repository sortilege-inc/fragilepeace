## Narrative

!lede [[Bayushi Monban]] spent the night at the [[Geisha House]] arranging a murder. He found a [[Scorpion Cousin]] at a game board, dismissed the man's geisha to talk — *how is this not forcing yourself on me* — and bought him three more, and then said it in Cadence, plainly. A servant to carry food into the guest rooms. An ingredient served to a woman. *It would have to be quick. I do not need her to explode. I need her to expire.* He had a Western flower with him already and thought it would take too much of it.

The cousin is an informant and said so twice — *I do not do that, I never have, my job was to get information* — and then said he was well versed in the means anyway. Quick he could manage; silent would be harder. [[Bayushi Monban]] would take quick. The only condition was that nothing trace back to either of them. They are leaving in the morning, so it was set for the noon meal.

The price was future favours, and the cousin named what that means: the family will one day have a target it needs removed, and a man who has just bought poison can be assumed willing. He gave the rest of his advice unasked. *If you are the legendary Bayushi Monban, you may find your reputation is getting in the way of your actual work.* And: *your family is going to use you, abuse you and exploit you until you are useless. Get out while you can.* [[Bayushi Monban]] told him he is the [[Scorpion Clan|Scorpion]] that serves, that he does not run from his responsibilities, does not hide, and does not lie.

Who the woman is was never said.

While he was out, [[Kakita Kazumi]] went over the roof into [[Shiba Midori]]'s quarters, opened a hole in the tatami, shimmied down the wall and took [[The Box]] on eight successes. It was empty, and he was certain of it. He was on his way to search [[Bayushi Monban]]'s room next when a scream came out of the guest quarters — from the Imperial Envoy's room, diagonally across from [[Doji Setsuna]]'s.

[[Shiba Midori]] was at the daimyō's table with her breakfast bowl. [[Doji Setsuna]] was in the worst night of her life — exhaustion on top of the medicine on top of everything else — past vomiting into dry heaving, convulsing, her attendants rotating buckets — and through the delirium she caught the scream as a separate thing from her own noise: a young woman's voice, and terror. The medicine had not reduced the damage by much.

[[Kakita Kazumi]] was on the roof and closest. He looked through the window rather than dropping in, because coming through a window would have finished the girl off, and went round through the building to knock on the door like a person. [[Miya Misato]] was crouched in the corner of her own room clutching herself. [[Miya Misato]]'s room had been searched exhaustively and left in complete disarray. She pointed, terrified, at the wall — where a throwing star pinned a scrap of paper, two characters crudely scrawled in dark blue ink. *Turn back.* Two characters, and no more than that — nobody signed it.

She threw herself at him. *Someone was here. They could have killed me, and they did not. Why did they not kill me?*

He went over the room and got the answer. They were after one specific thing and did not find it. It was done fast and in the dark. There were footprint impressions in the tatami and dust scuffed about. The window was not merely unlocked but standing open — *I closed it last night, it was chilly* — on a bright moonlit night. And they had time. Anyone with that long in the room could have killed her twenty times over.

So he told her, in front of the governor and half the household: good news, they were not there to kill her; bad news, they wanted her message. Then he explained to her, hypothetically, that a seal can be lifted off one document and pressed onto a forged one, and that a forged Imperial order would set the [[Lion Clan|Lion]] and the [[Unicorn Clan|Unicorn]] further against each other than they already are. Asked where she keeps the writ, [[Miya Misato]] tapped her kimono — in the open, in front of everyone.

[[Kakita Kazumi]] tossed the empty box to [[Shiba Midori]] on his way past. *You should keep an eye on this better.*

[[Shiba Midori]] went to [[Doji Setsuna]] instead, with blanket consent and a patient too far gone to give any other kind. She could not tell whether she had measured too little or whether the body was simply taking it badly — whether there was enough poison in her to make her this ill and not enough to finish what it was for. Knowing would take an examination more intrusive than the moment allowed. [[Doji Setsuna]] brought the chamomile straight back up. What [[Shiba Midori]] did find, looking round the room, was a scrap of almost-black fabric hanging off a nail in the rafters — not cloth from any samurai's clothing, and near enough to invisible held against a shadow. She did not recognise it and could not place the colour.

Two rooms entered in one night. Only one of them had anything in it worth wanting.

[[Doji Setsuna]] got herself upright on nothing but will, dressed over her ladies' objections, and washed a face that was already most of the way to her own funeral paint. She gave herself an hour. In the hallway there were three times the usual guards and [[Governor Miya Tetsuya]] holding his niece while she sobbed on him, which in any other week would have been unthinkable.

She went into the ransacked room and drew the shuriken out of the wall herself, carefully, and wrapped it in cloth, because that is a favourite way to deliver poison. The blade was clean and unmarked. She handed it to [[Kakita Kazumi]]. *This shuriken needs to be returned to its rightful owner.* — *I will see that it is done, Lady Doji. With extreme diligence.*

The ink on the note is the same dark blue as the cloth. There was nothing hidden in the paper; [[Kakita Kazumi]] passed it over a flame and got nothing, and could not place the ink either. [[Shiba Midori]] slipped him the fabric — *someone you have a professional interest in?* — and his eye twitched. Between the two rooms it is more than one intruder, with more than one errand.

[[Bayushi Monban]] woke in the arms of one of three women, an empty sake bottle beside him and half his clothes somewhere else, with the sun already high. He tried Cleansing Spirit on his own intoxication and failed it, and stayed dazed, disoriented and drunk. Four plates of breakfast were waiting outside the door. The serving girl mentioned that the young lord he had been drinking with left early, in a hurry. He tipped a koku, took his leave of the matron — *we know what sort of discretion your family looks for* — and went out into the street walking in no particular line.

[[Ryu]] found him there, having been sent across the water by [[Kakita Kazumi]] to fetch his master back by the hair if necessary, and put him in a hired cart. The man pushing it wanted paying. Told his passenger was an Emerald Magistrate, he said he was His Majesty the Emperor and still had rent to find.

There were people waiting in the boat who [[Bayushi Monban]] did not know — *there are three of them to your eyes*, which is the sort of thing a man is told when he has drunk a bottle of sake and cannot clear the condition. Plain black shirts, brown trousers, and a bundle that looked as though it had come from the market. One of them leaned over, said his lord sent his regards, put the bundle into [[Bayushi Monban]]'s hands, and they stepped off.

He arrived at the mansion held up by [[Ryu]], bloodshot and unable to hold a straight line, into a house full of guards and a crying envoy. [[Kakita Kazumi]] asked whether he had trained at the school of the drunken fist. Asked why [[Doji Setsuna]] looked the way she did, and told it was because her bodyguard had not been there, he had an answer ready. He had asked, the night before, what was going on. He had been told by [[Doji Setsuna]] that it was none of his business and to go away, and by [[Shiba Midori]] the same, and he had gone away. Now he was back. So what was the problem?

[[Doji Setsuna]] gave him a look he will remember and went to lie down.

[[Governor Miya Tetsuya]] offered him the post of his niece's yojimbo. [[Bayushi Monban]] declined it — he is not certain [[Doji Setsuna]] is even able to dismiss him, given who assigned him — and agreed to keep an eye on the girl anyway. The governor will send some of his own men on the journey. He also pointed out what the palanquin costs: six men carrying a box, and no speed at all. [[Shiba Midori]] suggested a covered wagon and [[Kakita Kazumi]] said no samurai can be seen travelling in one.

[[Kakita Kazumi]] measured [[Miya Misato]] by eye against the two women. She is midway between them, and [[Shiba Midori]] can make up the inches in her sandals, which makes [[Shiba Midori]] the decoy in the Imperial Envoy's place.

Then [[Bayushi Monban]] went to bathe, and [[Ryu]] slid his door open to fetch clean clothes, and [[Kakita Kazumi]] saw a box in the room out of the corner of his eye. Not the empty one — [[Shiba Midori]] had already given that to [[Kitsu Ayoko]]. A different one. It had been in [[Bayushi Monban]]'s quarters before he ever got home.

He told [[Shiba Midori]] to lock the bathhouse door and tossed the room. *We should have sealed the damn box up when we had the chance. I should have just stabbed him.*

[[Bayushi Monban]] does not remember taking the box. He does not remember most of his night. And when he undressed for the bath there was a warm golden chain around his neck that had not been there the day before. He knows it is there. He does not want it off. It is comfortable.

[[Ryu]] went for [[Shiba Midori]], apologetically, with the assurance that his lord would be wearing trousers. [[Kakita Kazumi]] shouted the actual question across — was he wearing anything strange — and both of them said *amulet* at the same moment.

In [[Bayushi Monban]]'s room there were three of the odd little boxes. The protective seals had been deliberately scratched off. The magical locks [[Shiba Midori]] set were broken and defaced. And over every one of her warnings, scratched deep: *fuck you, I do what I want.*

She will need a clean vessel and will have to start again.

## Setsuna

**Her yojimbo is compromised and the record should say so plainly.** He bought a poisoning last night, for a woman nobody named, to be served at the noon meal by a hired servant, and paid for it with a promise to kill for his family later. He does not remember taking the vessel out of [[Shiba Midori]]'s room. He does not remember most of the night. There is a warm gold chain around his neck he did not put on, and he does not want it taken off.

Everything he did between the geisha house and the bath was done by a man wearing it. That is the explanation and it is not an excuse, because she cannot tell where he stops and it starts, and neither can he. The scratched-out warnings in his quarters are not the language of a man having a bad night. *I do what I want.*

**The target of the theft was never her, and never the envoy's life.** [[Miya Misato]] was left alive by people who had hours in her room. They wanted the writ, or more likely a clean impression of the [[Imperial Seal]] to make a better one — and [[Kakita Kazumi]] said that out loud, in a room with the governor, the household and a sobbing sixteen-year-old in it, and then watched her pat the front of her kimono to show where she keeps it. Three Emerald Magistrates were standing there and not one of them stopped her. If anybody in that room was working for the other side, the second attempt will not need to search.

*Turn back.* Two characters, unsigned, in the same dark blue ink as the cloth left hanging in **her own** rafters. Nobody threatens an Imperial envoy and then leaves; this is a warning-off, written by somebody who would rather this did not become a killing, and who took care to leave nothing in it but the instruction. She should stop treating those two rooms as one event. One intruder wanted a document. The other stood over a woman who was convulsing and took nothing at all.

And [[Shiba Midori]] did not recognise the cloth. Not from the road in session six, not from anywhere. It is not a clan colour, it is shinobi work, cut to be invisible against a shadow — which means the party has no prior sighting to match it to, and the comfort of a familiar enemy is not available.

**She gave an order today that she has not admitted to giving.** She drew a shuriken out of a wall, wrapped it because she knows what shuriken carry, and put it in the hands of the one of her fellow magistrates who kills people, with the instruction that it be returned to its owner. He said he would see it done with extreme diligence. There is no version of that exchange in which she did not ask for a death, and she asked for it in the state she was in, an hour out of her own sickbed, from a man she has never once acknowledged is what he is.

Which is the pattern of the whole day. She is being poisoned by her own choice and it may not have worked; [[Shiba Midori]] cannot tell whether the dose was short or the body is simply failing at it, and finding out means an examination nobody has time for. She got up, put her face on over a face that was already the colour of the paint, and gave herself an hour of usable life.

She spent it well. But she should notice what she spent it on. Not the envoy, not the seal, not the decoy plan — she spent it handing a weapon to an assassin, and then she went back to bed.
