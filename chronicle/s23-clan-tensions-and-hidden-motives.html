Clan Tensions and Hidden Motives

Session 23·2025-09-29

Setsuna read the secret treaty and heard her own yojimbo's voice in the phrasing. So did everybody else in the room, and one of them was a Kitsuki.

Four Lion bushi dragged Mirumoto Isshin into the castle in restraints. He was shouting misconduct the whole way, and he spat on a menpō, and Bayushi Monban met him with the flat authority of an Emerald Magistrate and had him taken to his quarters to be questioned properly rather than handled in a corridor. Whatever the Dragon came to say, it arrived tied up.

In the shrine that is now a courtroom, Ide Subane asked Kitsuki Kaage for a recess to read the newly entered evidence, got it, and spent it going through the government documents line by line.

Then Bayushi Monban produced his case: three versions of Ikoma Akuyaku's diary, and a secret treaty between Shinjo Kamo and Shoshuro Amane, carrying Shoshuro Amane's chop and a clause transferring the diamond mines to the Scorpion.

Doji Setsuna read it and heard something wrong in it. The phrasing had a particular flavour — his flavour, the turn of speech a man is known for — and once she had noticed it she could not stop noticing it. A document three centuries old should not sound like the person handing it over.

Ide Subane went straight at it: the treaty's legitimacy, and the inconsistencies running between the three diaries. Kitsuki Kaage called the room to order and told all of them to bring substantiated claims instead of accusations. Then he ruled: the treaty is withheld from evidence pending verification, and he sent an assistant to the Lion library for the records that would verify it.

Shiba Midori said nothing at all. She had decided to speak in the morning, when the room was cooler.

Doji Setsuna moved instead — for summary dismissal of the Scorpion claim, with prejudice, on the ground that the Scorpion had sat on this evidence for three hundred years and forfeited the right to raise it now. It stopped the room. Kitsuki Kaage tabled the motion: not until the evidence is in and authenticated.

The court rose. Bayushi Monban went to the shrine and stayed there a while.

Setsuna

She recognised the hand. The secret treaty transferring the mines reads like Bayushi Monban talks. She noticed the phrasing herself, unprompted, and the thing to understand is that noticing was not clever — it was available. Ide Subane went for the treaty within minutes. Kitsuki Kaage withheld it and sent for the Lion library's records to check it against. When those records come back, the comparison will be made by a Kitsuki against a chop he can hold in his hand.

This is the forgery from Soshi Yamako, laundered through her own yojimbo, produced in open court under an Emerald Magistrate's authority. There is no version of the coming week in which it survives.

Her dismissal motion is clean, and it is worth being clear why. What she offered Shosuro Imako was conditional: support for the Scorpion claim in exchange for a pledge of non-intervention in the Crane–Lion treaty. The Scorpion deferred on the agreement. An offer made against a condition that is never met does not bind the person who made it, and she is right to treat herself as free.

So the motion is hers to aim. Summary dismissal with prejudice on the ground of three centuries' silence is good law, and it does useful work: it kills the Scorpion claim, leaves the Unicorn holding only the Treaty of White Shores Lake, and makes the Imperial settlement the tidiest thing left on the table.

She should still expect the Scorpion to describe it as a betrayal, because that is what they will get out of saying so. The answer is short and she should have it ready: they were offered terms, they declined to close, and the offer went with the condition.

And Shiba Midori is waiting for morning. She withdrew her own testimony last session on principle, has held her tongue through this one, and has something to say when the room is cool. That is worth being present for.

One loose thread with a rope on it: a Dragon bushi was dragged into a Lion castle in restraints, shouting misconduct, in the middle of an Imperial arbitration. Nobody has asked yet whose misconduct he meant.
