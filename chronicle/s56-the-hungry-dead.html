The Hungry Dead

Session 56·2026-09-14

Every sunrise the ship runs aground somewhere desolate. Every sunset it comes down the river out of Unicorn country and enters Gaki-dō again, for the first time, as though it had never happened before.

Bayushi Monban closed the water. Nobody on the river by order of an Emerald Magistrate — which as a practical matter only enforced what the city was already doing, because the docks were eerily empty and stayed that way. Then he needed to cross to the Lion district, and discovered what he had built. He asked a Kaeru to row him and was refused: I am not a peasant, sir. He asked to be taken where peasants could be found, was pointed with great courtesy at the pleasure district, and stood being politely sassed by a rōnin he could not do anything about without making it worse. He wrote the man's name in his notebook — Kaeru Jinku — and went to the sake houses instead, where the proprietor told him the same thing in fewer words. The Emerald Magistrates closed the water. Nobody will take him across.

The Kaeru have not forgiven the insult he gave their lord. Every one of them should have duelled him for it, and the commander forbade it and said she would give him a proper lesson herself.

Doji Setsuna crossed back from the island and the Drowned Merchant River was smooth as glass, which it never is. The boat skimmed. Out of the corner of her eye, in the water, there were skeletal faces in white robes; in her ear an obscene guttural howl like a starving animal; and every hair on her stood up exactly as it does when the ghost is near. But the ghost was not there. Whatever this is, it is not hers.

Shiba Midori was where they had put her to rest, half spent from the road, and could not get her severely wounded fire ring to close. What she did notice, from the window, was lights over the river upstream — low ground, toward Unicorn lands. There were several gourds of water standing by her bed, which Bayushi Monban had been filling out of the river for her, because what he can do with it is scratch the surface and what she can do is find out why the water screams.

In an alley on the Unicorn side he found the rest of it. The wall had been whitewashed — except it had not; it was scorched pale, and left on it in darker shadow was the after-image of a person cowering, burned into the plaster the way a bright light leaves an image behind the eye. The dust was disturbed where something had been dragged away. His demon wound itched around his neck the whole time, which is confirmation enough on its own that something ghostly stood here, and it is emphatically not what he felt in the water. These things hunt in packs, they come to water, and this one had hunted, found, and moved on. There were no more shadows on the other walls and no trail to follow.

The fox rode in his kimono through all of it, as she has since the desert — hidden, inquisitive, occasionally biting him, and the reason he got out of Unicorn lands with nobody seeing. She wanted to know why there was boiled leaf juice by the bed and what all the water was for and why the woman was lying down instead of doing things.

Then the storm came in properly, sheets of rain across the window, and the ship came down the river toward the Unicorn shore and began to turn for the docks.

Bayushi Monban stopped being emotional and became something else entirely. He called to arms. He wanted every scrap of jade in the district and he wanted it now, he told people to get on their knees and pray to the sun and to any benevolent thing they had ever heard of, and he looked at his own Emerald Magistrate's badge and worked out that he could break it into powder if it came to that. He asked the fox whether there were others like her in the city who could be reached and asked for help, and told her she did not have to go. Ryu and So arrived drenched with the rest of the luggage and were told to stay close. Shiba Midori put fire on Kitsu Ayoko until she was a walking bonfire, dangerous to stand near and impossible for anything on the river to ignore.

What was on the ship was Kitsuki Wataru, and he had been gone a long time.

He followed Tonbo Kuma aboard two days ago because Tonbo Kuma had gone after the strange ship three days ago and not come back. He found him. He also found that the ship sails itself, that there is no crew and nobody aboard to find, and that he has not been in the world since. Every sunrise it runs aground somewhere desolate. Every sunset it comes down the river out of Unicorn country and enters Gaki-dō again as though for the first time. He can steer it. He can choose where to land. Sometimes people come aboard.

The place on the other side is the City Of The Rich Frog, and is not. Governor Miya Tetsuya's palace is simply absent. The gigantic willow it is built in the roots of — the oldest living tree anyone knows of in Rokugan — stands withered, with small black fires erupting all round it. Tonbo Kuma read that as the corruption of Jigoku reaching into Gaki-dō, and what frightens him is what happens if it becomes manifest on this side. The portals have to be closed.

Tonbo Kuma had to fight the dead samurai who were aboard when he arrived and put them off the ship, because they had become gaki themselves, as everyone who dies in that place does. Kitsuki Wataru has spent two days praying for the hungry dead morning and evening — let there be ease of your suffering; we will share with you our water — capping his meridians daily to keep them both spiritually clean, fighting off a swarm every single time either of them brought out anything edible, and hoping that whoever came aboard next would have jade.

They had jade. Part of the deck had burned away, and under it were crates of teardrop-sized jade. We will need that at the tree.

And Kitsuki Wataru is not the man who left. He was a Kitsuki investigator in a scholar's hat, correct to a fault and expecting to be obeyed. What came up off that deck was a Togashi monk — bald, tattooed, in a travel-dirty green robe with prayer beads and a monk's bag, beard kept, entirely at ease. It brings me joy to see you all again. I did know that karma would eventually bring me back to you, and it looks like we are going to be having another grand adventure in the land of the dead. Bayushi Monban did not find the phrasing as cheering as it was meant.

They are uncle and nephew, and were before. He asked after the twin-tailed girl, Shiba Midori's bodyguard, and the answer was that their missing sister had a different call. Bayushi Monban went quiet at the mention of the Moon Maiden and shrank a little, the way a man does at a personal failure, and then opened his kimono and showed him the phoenix, which was thanked for as the personal thing it was.

Doji Setsuna did not get on the ship. She had already done the thing she came to do, and she said so: she sent support, she wished them well, and she went back to the bath. From a warm room with clean clothes waiting and rain on the window she could hear her yōjimbō out in the dark shaking the district down for jade.

Shiba Midori did get on. She was standing on the deck talking when Kitsu Ayoko looked up at her and asked why she was fading away, and a crack of thunder took the City Of The Rich Frog out from under her.

The fox put a black paw out over the water and asked what those were. About a dozen of the hungry dead, swimming across the river toward the ship. Kitsuki Wataru took a piece of fruit he had been given at a shrine and threw it to one of them, because compassion is owed to the hungry dead for a hunger that is not of their own making, and told Bayushi Monban he was building merit. That is where it stopped.

Setsuna

She was right, and she stayed out of it. Shiba Midori is the one the governor sent for. Bayushi Monban can put his hand in the river and hear what is on the other side of it. She can read a room, and there is no room. So when the ship came in she sent what she had and went back to the bath, and that is the correct call, and it is worth writing down that she made it deliberately rather than being left behind.

It will not feel correct later. Her colleague went aboard mid-sentence and the city came out from under her; Shiba Midori is now somewhere that turns everyone who dies in it into one of the things that killed them. If that goes wrong, the last thing Doji Setsuna did about it was decline.

The river told her something and she should not file it with the rest. The water was glass. There were faces in it. The howl was in her ear and not on the air, and every hair on her stood up exactly the way it does when the ghost is close — and the ghost was not there. She has spent this campaign learning the shape of one haunting. This is a second thing wearing the same signature, which is either a very large coincidence or the reason her own haunting has always felt like weather rather than a person.

The jade was already aboard. Bayushi Monban tore up a district for it and was ready to break his own badge, and the ship arrived with crates of it under a burned deck. Somebody provisioned that vessel for exactly this, or the tree did, or Gaki-dō did. Nothing about a derelict with no crew and a hold full of the one substance that matters here is accidental, and nobody on board has asked the question yet because they are busy being grateful.

And the old man came back a happy one. Kitsuki Wataru left as her mentor's sort of magistrate — precise, correct, expecting to be obeyed — and has returned bald and tattooed and delighted, having spent two days in the realm of hungry ghosts praying for them by name and sharing his water. He is the only person in this campaign who has gone into something worse than he left and come out lighter. Bayushi Monban finds him unbearable, which is the surest sign he is worth listening to.

What he asked, first, was after the Moon Maiden. Nobody has had to say it out loud to a newcomer before. Our missing sister had a different call is a kind sentence and it is not a true one, and Bayushi Monban flinched when it was said for him.
