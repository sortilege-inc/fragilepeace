## Narrative

!lede The gaki came over the side while a second ship came down the river to ram them. It was steered by a funa-yūrei — a hungry ghost that captains a ghost ship, ringed about with flaming lights, which anyone with any theology at all can name on sight — and crewed by gaki punting it through the muck. Two of the things aboard had been samurai once and had not lost the knack. One opened its mouth and let a forked tongue slide out over sharp yellowed teeth, and shook a battle-damaged katana across the water. The other stood at the bow holding the forestay, armour black and stained, a bloodied bandage over both eyes and a massive nodachi over its shoulder — and when the ships closed it made the leap without being able to see it.

These are not the mindless kind. [[Shiba Midori]] could read off the shape of one what sins had sent it to [[Gaki-dō]] in the first place.

[[Kitsuki Wataru]] drew the blind one and met a nodachi with a quarterstaff, parried it, put a crit back into it and shouted for the bushi. Then a second came at him with a naginata and put him on the deck unconscious, and the hungry ones crawled over to eat him where he lay. He went on defending himself while he was out, which is the only reason he kept his throat; his robes did not survive it.

[[Bayushi Monban]] spent the fight being burned by his own salvation. He had gone down into the burned-out hold where the jade was, and jade does not care that a man means well — he had touched it impure, and the question the night left him with is whether that is now permanent. He punched the blind one back with a fist of air so [[Shiba Midori]] could duck under it, then rode the clouds back down into the hold, where there was enough jade to make a man wealthy for several lifetimes. Compromised, a turn away from unmasking, he scooped it up and flung it out onto the deck instead. *Use the jade.* A lump of it landed under a gaki's foot; it hissed and went over the side in pain.

[[Tonbo Kuma]] petitioned the water kami and clean water came up out of the muddy river and wrapped itself round [[Bayushi Monban]] like a second set of armour, shedding his fatigue a little each round. Then he called the wind and flew.

And then the thing he had called went wrong. His manifested water kami hit one of the samurai gaki with a smashing torrent, took strife it could not carry, and stopped being his. What it did next it did on its own: it struck at every living thing it could reach, and the whole deck went over the side. Nobody saw what happened to [[Shiba Midori]] or to [[Kitsuki Wataru]], who was unconscious when the water took him.

[[Tonbo Kuma]] went down after him and brought him up, and they surfaced into a river with gaki swimming toward them and a long hole torn in the hull above. The ship was sinking, and there was an island dead ahead.

[[Bayushi Monban]] hauled a second crate of jade up out of the flooding hold with [[Tonbo Kuma]] lending his weight, which was the last chance anything down there had. A third crate stayed in the water. Then the wreck: [[Tonbo Kuma]] shoved [[Kitsuki Wataru]] into the shallows and stayed airborne, [[Bayushi Monban]] rode the clouds off the deck with one crate under his arm, [[Shiba Midori]] went over the side, and the ship put its bow into the tree.

The tree is the other one. In the world it is the great willow, the oldest living thing anybody knows of, with [[Governor Miya Tetsuya]]'s palace built down among its roots. Here it stands black and gnarled and dead, lit in flashes by the lightning, and its branches are strung with ancient rotting prayer cords — binders, wooden blocks on strings, the whole apparatus of calling the kami down to hold something in place. The loops along them once held something. Almost all are empty now. One still carries a tiny decaying finger of jade.

That is what told them. This is not a tree that has been cursed; this is a tree somebody warded, a long time ago, to keep what is in it from getting out. The barrier is weakened and it is still standing. The jade is not treasure. The jade is the ward.

There was another way to do it. The source could have been destroyed instead of contained, and nobody could say what that would do — and what it would have done, as it turns out, is open the way into [[Gaki-dō]] in the middle of the city.

[[Bayushi Monban]] looked at three crates of it and said what he was thinking, which was that there was a great deal of money here. Then he said the other thing. If they did the right thing together they could fix this and go home, and they could always pocket a couple afterwards.

They split the tree between them. [[Bayushi Monban]] and [[Tonbo Kuma]] took the high branches from the air, replacing every rotted and corrupted finger they found with a fresh one; [[Shiba Midori]] and [[Kitsuki Wataru]] worked the low ones, and [[Kitsuki Wataru]] salvaged what he could from the crate that had gone into the roots.

The cleansing would not take. [[Shiba Midori]] tried to lift the corruption off the whole area and failed it; the taint in the tree is stubborn and old. So she prayed instead — that Amaterasu show favour on her children even when they are very far from her — and in the grey realm where it does not happen, the clouds parted. Light came down and lay on the dead black tree, and for a moment they all saw it as it is on the other side: green, and new, and entirely alive. After that the work was possible.

[[Bayushi Monban]] stood out of the actual cleansing and said why. The demon wound makes him the wrong instrument for this, and he knew it. [[Tonbo Kuma]] worked the rite with the others assisting and widened it to take in the whole ground, and the river of [[Gaki-dō]] ran clean over the roots of the tree.

Then they blinked, and were watching the sun come up from the bank of the governor's island, standing among the willow's roots at more or less the spot where they had been standing a moment before.

The ghost ship will not come back. What is under that tree is sealed for something like another hundred years, which means somebody will have to do this again, and the record should say so plainly rather than let it read as finished.

The jade went home without them. Three crates of it, left on the wrong side. *I am supposed to be a monk anyway. I suppose I should be used to being broke — but I would have loved to see my lord's face when I emptied a pocketful of it at his feet.*

## Learned

Its branches in Gaki-dō carry ancient rotting prayer cords and binders, and the loops along them held fingers of jade — almost all now gone, one still in place. The ward was raised to contain what is in the tree, it had decayed almost to nothing, and it has now been restrung. Sealed for roughly another century; someone will have to do it again. Destroying the source instead would have opened the way into Gaki-dō inside the city.

## Setsuna

**She was not there for any of it.** She stayed in the bath while the ship came in, which was the right call on the night and is now a night she will only ever have secondhand. What her colleagues did on the other side of the water is the largest thing that has happened in this city since she arrived in it, and her entire part in it was declining to go.

The company pools what it learns, so she will have the account. She should notice what the account costs her anyway: [[Shiba Midori]] went into [[Gaki-dō]] mid-sentence and came back having prayed the sun open over a dead realm, and [[Doji Setsuna]] heard about it at breakfast.

**The jade was the point, and nobody knew that until they were standing under the tree.** [[Bayushi Monban]] tore a district apart for it on a hunch and [[Tonbo Kuma]] said only that it would be needed. It turned out to be the ward itself — fingers of jade strung on prayer cords round a thing somebody bound there long enough ago that almost every one of them had rotted out. Three crates of it went over with the wreck and stayed on the wrong side, which she will find an amusing detail and which is also the whole of the campaign's luck in miniature: the price was paid by people who did not know what they were buying.

**What was sealed is not gone.** A century, near enough, and then somebody else does this. Whoever built that ward built it to be renewed and nobody renewed it, which is why the cords were bare and the ship was loose on the river in the first place. The useful question is not what is under the tree. It is who was supposed to be maintaining it, and when they stopped.

And there is a thing worth holding onto about her yōjimbō. He was handed a fortune, on a night when he had nothing, in a place where nobody would ever know — and he put it into the tree and made a joke about pocketing a couple afterwards. Then he stood out of the cleansing, unprompted, because he understood that what is in him would have spoiled it. Neither of those is the man who shouted at her in a garden.
