Tea and Tensions in Transit

Session 35·2026-01-06

The Imperial Envoy she is sworn to see succeed is sixteen, has no retinue, no experience and no actual authority, and does not know where either army is. Her instructions were delivered to her out loud, by a servant.

The envoy had already been told. Between sessions the magistrates worked out what she is — a cat's paw sent somewhere she is likely to be killed — and then told her so, and she has spent the days since too frightened to leave her room. I'm dead. I'm dead. I'm dead. The reading was right. Saying it aloud to a sixteen-year-old was not.

Miya Misato is Governor Miya Tetsuya's niece. She carries the Emperor's written order and nothing else: her instructions from the Imperial Advisor were given to her orally, by one of the Advisor's servants. She is to go into Unicorn lands, find the Lion armies, order them to cease operations, then find the Unicorn Champion and bring both sides to terms. She was told not to bring a retinue because it would be faster. Her uncle refused to let her go unescorted; she had thought being an Imperial was protection enough.

Kakita Kazumi invited her to tea at the Governor's Tea House to settle her, and Shiba Midori insisted on preparing it herself and hosted. Bayushi Monban did not come, and Kakita Kazumi did not like the shape of that absence. Doji Setsuna arrived late from being ill that morning, was announced properly, and found the teacup shaking violently in the envoy's hand and a stammer she could not get through a sentence without. She read her at a glance and answered as though none of it were happening. Of course we will go. This is an Imperial order, after all. Are we not Emerald Magistrates?

Her uncle had recommended them, Miya Misato said, because they found someone who had been lost.

Then the thing Doji Setsuna said about her own husband, which nobody in that room can have expected. Asked whether the object was to find the Lion and arrange Shinjō Harunobu's ransom: it is possible that my goals are not limited to my husband, and so it depends what other concessions are available as to whether we choose to ransom him or not. A prisoner whose value is left deliberately unstated is a prisoner nobody can price.

Underneath all of it, the first problem: Miya Misato does not know where either army is. The only lead anyone has is Akodo Akihito's letter, which placed him in a cave near where the Battle of the Snow Plains was fought — a week ago, without a return address, with an army that has certainly moved since. The Snow Plain lies more or less due east of the City Of The Rich Frog. And the army they would be walking up to is Matsu Tsuko's: she has the Lion host in hand, and if the clan champion takes the field it passes technically to him, but she will be there — she would not miss a fight her own family started. And there is a second problem nobody solved: four people in magistrate robes walking into a battlefield have no military escort, no standard and no mon, and no way to raise any. Nobody will stop to ask who they are.

So they went to ask the Unicorn in the city, and took the envoy with them — how can we protect you if you are here and we are there — and told Governor Miya Tetsuya plainly why they were going. He warned them they might still spend a month riding around after a moving army.

They crossed by the governor's boat to the Unicorn District on the western bank, which is a merchant quarter thick with samurai and Kaeru rōnin. Kakita Kazumi's new servant was asked for the best broker of news about the fighting and came away with three names: Ide Chiharu, the old man at the curio shop, who is an Ide and will not be pleased to see any of them; Irei Sakube, who deals in fine art and antiquities from all over Rokugan and, some say, beyond; and Hideyoshi Aki, a well-connected trader who is always on the move. At the counting house Hideyoshi Aki was out taking a shipment, so the answer came from a Yoriki — and from his own reputation, because the servants worked out who he was. Every action I take endangers myself. Have you not seen the play? There is a play about him. It opens in a month, as one of the first showings at the city's new opera house.

What the Yoriki gave him is the only hard fact in the session. Shipments up the river have been going due northeast for the last week or two, having gone south before that — diverging a few days out from the city. Goods follow armies.

Bayushi Monban had a fruitless afternoon. He found one Scorpion, a young man in a cat mask who wanted directions to the Tea House With No Name and turned out to be on holiday from Slowtide Harbor, who knew nothing about the armies and knew exactly who Bayushi Monban was — I do not think anybody in the Emerald Empire does not know who you are. He left him one thing worth having: there are servants of the family about, and he sees their marks from time to time. Bayushi Monban spent Lord Bayushi's Whispers looking for those marks and found nothing.

And then walked, by accident, into the ruin of the Burnt Theater — and found on a destroyed section of wall a Shosuro mark, or at least the style the Shosuro use.

The Crane bushi guarding the reconstruction are Daidoji Shin's, posted there because there has already been one sabotage. The temple has completed the cleansing rites, and the place is purified — but a Dragon priest has said the aftershock of a fire spirit breaking loose will leave the ground unnaturally dark for a long time, and that matches what Bayushi Monban can feel standing in it. The play that night was a scandalous one and not the grand reopening, which will be The Famous Mediocrity, all the rage in the capital.

The audience is the finding. Daidoji Shin was in it. Governor Miya Tetsuya was in it. So were a dozen more names — mostly Unicorn, a couple of Lion, the occasional Dragon — about half the city's government, and a fair cross-section of who matters in the City Of The Rich Frog. Nobody of consequence died. A couple of servants are unaccounted for and no bodies were ever found.

Shiba Midori has the Ifrit's box in her own quarters, in a long receptacle, out of the way and never against her skin. Nobody has seen it and nobody knows she has it, though somebody with the right knowledge might work it out. She has asked the Kitsu she trusts to stand guard over the room, which is the best she can do short of a unit of guards.

Back at the palace, the other thing. Doji Setsuna told Shiba Midori she had decided on the Terminating Tea, said how far along she believed herself to be, and asked whether it could wait until after the envoy was delivered. It could. Shiba Midori told her the recovery would be neither pleasant nor swift. Then the time is now.

What it is: several poisons combined at titrated doses, each with a medicinal use at a concentration far below this one. The calculation is how much poison a body can be given before it gives up a pregnancy, without being given enough to die. There is no way to be certain of a dose except by administering it.

Shiba Midori sent servants, because samurai do not do their own buying, and gave them the list out loud. Ishika and Aika came back with two of the four things — substitutions for the rest that will be useless at best, including an expensive exotic from abroad which is a relative of the nightshade they were actually sent for. They did not come back with anything more dangerous than they were asked for, which is the one mercy; the cost is that the dose of the poisons she does have must now be increased.

Bayushi Monban met them coming off the boat and asked to see the bundles, and they would not show him. The lady's words are her responsibility. Her life is mine. They still would not. He failed to get a look at the contents and then worked it out anyway on his own herbalism — there are things in there that are medicine titrated one way and poison titrated another. Kakita Kazumi worked it out at the same moment and would not tell him.

So Bayushi Monban went to Doji Setsuna and asked outright whether she was ill. In a way. I have been ill. — In what way? — I would prefer you did not have to lie. — Then do not lie. She would not tell him, said Shiba Midori was ensuring her safety in the matter, and gave him one concession: he may go to Shiba Midori, and if she believes he can help, he has her blessing to help. Otherwise he is to leave it alone. You are my responsibility. Kakita Kazumi put an arm round his shoulders and steered him away — there are some issues in which a woman is allowed her privacy.

He read her anyway. She has been sick in the mornings repeatedly over the last weeks and has complained of nothing else, and she is going to a shugenja for medicine. He does not have confirmation. He does not need it.

Kakita Kazumi found Doji Setsuna alone and told her quietly to look for nightshade tea in very diluted doses, and then brought Shiba Midori the leaves himself, in a tightly wrapped paper packet with no name written on it — he tells his poisons apart by the colour of the tie — and instructions: one or two leaves, never with bare hands, never lick anything afterward, wash thoroughly.

For the last ingredient Doji Setsuna sent her own scribe, who is literate, with a written list. The scribe read it and said the substance is of questionable legality, that only certain apothecaries may carry it, and that none of them would dispense it in that quantity. So she went herself, on her own authority as an Emerald Magistrate, showed her chop and told the man he would sell it to her servant in the amount requested, and left Ishika to settle the price, because a samurai does not haggle. It is legal because a magistrate said so.

She also went to Governor Miya Tetsuya for a palanquin. He gave it and told her what she was buying: you are going to be a walking peacock across the Unicorn lands. The Lion will see you coming from miles away. That is the point. Envoys want to be seen.

Bayushi Monban came to Shiba Midori's door while she was brewing and was stopped by Aika, who told him the lady was not to be disturbed and did not care who he was. Shiba Midori answered over her shoulder without turning round. Please respect the woman's privacy, Monban, even though it taxes your patience. This is my patient, after all. He turned to go and Kakita Kazumi — who had been in the room the whole time and whom nobody had noticed — leaned in beside his ear and asked what he was doing. He went to his rooms, said and I am the Scorpion here, I am the untrustworthy one, and read the Tao Of Shinsei.

Kakita Kazumi then offered to help with the brewing, and the two of them settled it in four sentences. Do you not want to know what we are doing? — I have my suspicions. — You do not need to state them openly. — If you do not state them openly, I do not need to lie about how I know.

Shiba Midori erred deliberately toward too little, so that the dose is likelier to fail than to kill. Doji Setsuna drank it and met it in the void, sacrificing rather than standing, and spent everything she had on the roll. It left her severely wounded in her void ring, with gut sickness and exhaustion on top.

They leave for a war in the morning.

Setsuna

Her giri just walked into the room, and it is sixteen years old. She is sworn to see Miya Misato succeed, and until now that was a sentence in a letter. The envoy has no retinue, no experience, no authority, and no idea where she is going — and her orders from the Imperial Advisor were never written down. Only the Emperor's demand is on paper. Whoever sent her can deny every word of what they told her to do, and the one person who could contradict them is a girl they expect not to come back.

It also puts her exactly where she wants to be, which is the part worth watching. The writ orders the Lion to stop fighting and treat with the Unicorn Champion. Prisoner exchanges happen when armies stop. Shinjō Harunobu is with the Lion host. Serving the envoy honestly and getting her husband back are, for the first time in thirty-five sessions, the same road — and that is precisely when a person stops noticing which one they are actually on.

And she said the quiet thing out loud. It is possible that my goals are not limited to my husband. Said in a tea house, to an Imperial envoy, in front of two magistrates. It is true, it is well judged, and it prices him at nothing in advance so that nobody can price him against her. It is also the coldest sentence she has said about him in thirty-five sessions, and she said it in a room where it will be remembered.

Northeast, for a week or two, having been south before that. That is the only hard fact anyone acquired today, and it came from a trader's Yoriki reciting a shipping pattern. Same lesson as the quartermasters' books at the Snow Plain: the people who count things know first. What nobody has solved is the rest of it — no escort, no standard, no mon, and a battlefield at the end of the road where four people in magistrate robes will be killed before anyone asks who they are.

The private matter is being handled by her friends, and badly, and that is nobody's fault but the method's. Servants sent for poisons without being told what they were for brought back half a list and a substitute that is a cousin of the thing they were sent for. The dose had to be raised on what did arrive. Kakita Kazumi fixed it without being asked and without remark. Shiba Midori measured deliberately low, so that failure was the likelier error than death — which is the right call and means this may not be finished.

She then went to an apothecary herself and used a magistrate's chop to compel an illegal sale. That is a real exposure and it is hers alone: a merchant who will remember which magistrate, for a substance he knows the law on better than she does, in a quantity the law does not permit.

And Bayushi Monban knows. Not because anyone told him — because he was refused twice, in identical language, by her servants and by her, and then read her: sick in the mornings for weeks, complaining of nothing else, going to a shugenja for medicine. The record should stop saying he does not know. He has not been told, which is a different thing and a thinner protection. What she actually gave him was a rule: go to Shiba Midori, and help only if Shiba Midori says so. He has not gone.

Kakita Kazumi, who also knows, settled it with her the way it should have been settled with him. You do not need to state them openly. If you do not state them openly, I do not need to lie about how I know. That is the arrangement on offer and Bayushi Monban has not been offered it, because he cannot stop asking. He said it himself in the corridor afterward: and I am the Scorpion here.

She is severely wounded in the void, sick and exhausted, leaving at dawn, in a palanquin she asked for knowing it makes the party visible for miles. The Lion will see them coming. That is deliberate, and it is correct for an envoy, and it also means she will be carried across a war in a box that announces itself, at the worst physical moment of the campaign so far, with two people who know and one who has guessed.
