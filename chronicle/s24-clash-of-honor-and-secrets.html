Clash of Honor and Secrets

Session 24·2025-10-06

The letter had been sealed for three hundred years and it was addressed to the least of the Bayushi. Monban demanded it in open court, got it read aloud, and walked out of the room it destroyed him in.

The Head Librarian answered Kitsuki Kaage's order from the day before, and the answer was fatal. The castle holds four second-hand copies of letters that passed through Shinjō Kamo's hands, and a small number authenticated as his own writing — every one of them predating the Snow Plain, because he died there. They are travel correspondence from the years of the return, descriptions of lands beyond Rokugan's borders, kept as curiosities. None of them touches the battle. And of the secret treaty Bayushi Monban swore he found on the shelves of the Akodo Libraries, the librarians found no record of it ever being there at all — a thing that would have stood out, they noted, when the castle was assembling everything it had on the Snow Plain in anticipation of the magistrates. It would be quite a find if such a thing were there.

Ide Tsubame also found a watermark in the corner of the librarian's own letter — a stain placed to look spilled. Held to a flame it gave up a line of hidden ink: speak to the Commandant of the Academy.

Shosuro Aishi caught Bayushi Monban in a corridor with her fan up. She was not grateful, she was annoyed. There is no use lying to me, Bayushi-san. If they are after the same thing there was no reason to keep something that vital from her when she could have used it far earlier. His answer was that she now has deniability — Kitsuki Kaage already believes forgeries have been presented, this one proves to be another, and she is free and clear. They watch me. I am the Scorpion that stands out. She asked for a signal next time he does something arguably brilliant, and it visibly cost her to say it.

She also told him what she has arranged: Lady Kitsu will call up the ancestral spirits of Ikoma Akuyaku and a Shosuro who was at the battle, and Shosuro Aishi will ask the questions. He objected on grounds only a Scorpion would reach for — spirits can be controlled by whoever summons them, and it was their own clan's work to make sure everyone knows it. As for the Unicorn calling the treaty too long and too precise: that only means they have never read a Crane treaty or a Lion one.

In the library Bayushi Monban handed Doji Setsuna two letters. Shinjō Harunobu's said he is well, detained, injuries tended as the Lion would tend their own — and that the man who took him means to keep his horse, and that he had overheard talk outside his tent of an Emerald Magistrate pulling strings to get him out. The second was Akodo Akihito's refusal: requesting a prisoner of war as a personal servant, when that prisoner's spouse is the requesting magistrate's superior, looks a great deal like a hostage being arranged, and the answer is no. He wrote it subtly and it was still no.

She could not tell which thing she was looking at. I cannot for the life of me tell if this is good-intentioned and inept, or if this is some game of Scorpion subterfuge I cannot fathom. Whatever your intentions, I ask that you leave the situation with my husband alone. Then she thanked him. He agreed to write to Akodo Akihito withdrawing the request, and did.

Hida Kasudo reached the castle from the east at mid-morning with his two brothers — they are triplets, and he is the youngest. Hida Nozomu carried the banner, longest hair, a scar down the left of his face with the eye still in it; Hida Kenji bald and bearded with the matching scar on the other side. All three in lacquered armour, war helms tied at their hips, and all three calmer than a Crab is expected to be. His errand is two errands: his uncle, who is his lord, wants men for the Wall if any can be got, and his mother wants him to look in on things here. The guard thought the first would be difficult, and said the Commandant would be told in case cadets could be volunteered.

Court opened without Doji Setsuna, who had sent word she was in the libraries on case law. Kitsuki Kaage announced he had summoned expert testimony from the castle library to speak to the provenance of Bayushi Monban's document, and that they would join later.

Kakita Kazumi entered the battle journals, and with them something he could not document: Kitsu Ayoko's word that information about her own ancestor had been suppressed, and that the Scorpion had a hand in suppressing it. Shosuro Aishi met it by naming the wrong secret — if you are speaking of the legend of ninja, even among us that is a fairy tale — and was told no, the suppressed thing is the summoning of ancestors. That she conceded, with a condition and a half-bow: it is the Kitsu's alone, it would normally be barred because a shugenja governs what they summon, but if an honourable Kitsu does the summoning and the judge permits it, she holds no objection.

Bayushi Monban objected to the imputation itself. A clan's reputation is not evidence, nor are second- and third-hand accounts — and it surprised him that Kakita Kazumi had found anything, when the two of them had searched together and found nothing.

Kitsuki Kaage ruled, and the ruling will outlast the session. He will not admit the testimony of the dead unattested. He will entertain a motion to summon an ancestor, but such testimony is explicatory and not definitive, because spirits cannot testify in the court of the living. Kitsu Ayoko would be summoned as a witness and could enter documentary evidence on her ancestor. And he would much rather the documents were proven by other means.

Then Ide Tsubame made the move the case actually needed. The castle holds letters authenticated as being in Shinjō Kamo's own hand; let them be entered, not to argue her case but as exemplars, so that an outside expert with no interest in the dispute can set them beside the secret treaty and say whether one hand wrote both. She had the precedent for the outside expert ready. Kitsuki Kaage allowed it, and named the problem in the same breath: where are you going to find an expert historian to examine the provenance of these documents when every historian in this castle has probably already studied them ad nauseam in preparation for this case?

Bayushi Monban lost his temper, loudly, and the walls of the temple are paper. He had been told his documentation alone was suspect while the Lion produced nothing from anyone of note and the Unicorn produced nothing at all; nowhere had he heard such prejudice against one side of an argument; his clan's honour was being besmirched on rumour. And then the part everyone in the corridor heard through the screen: without the Scorpion, the Crane could not write poetry and stay clean and tidy, the Unicorn could not ride off into the unknown for three centuries and be welcomed home, the Phoenix could not sit reading in their libraries, the Dragon could not sit in their mountains thinking about thinking, and the Lion could not go out and do the things the Lion do. We do what we must. We are the villain.

It was a performance and it failed as one. Shosuro Aishi was invited to back him and declined, having been told plainly that he was being discourteous to everyone in the room including the judge; what she thought instead was why again did I choose to trust him. Kakita Kazumi brought him a cup of tea and told him to sit down, that he was doing his own argument more harm than good. Kitsuki Kaage waited for him to draw breath: you will be seated and you will be silent, or you will be expelled from this hearing.

The corridor outside had become a crossroads — Hida Kasudo and his brothers arriving, Shiba Midori coming from the guest rooms with a long box under her arm, Doji Setsuna down from the library on a few hours' sleep and immaculate with it, Kitsu Ayoko out of the stacks — all of them listening to a Scorpion magistrate shout through a screen.

Shiba Midori had the cache, and had to beg permission to present it, and was made to explain why it was germane. She began with the covering letter, which meant Kitsuki Kaage read her private correspondence aloud into the record.

Akodo Akihito had dreamed it. He woke in camp with memories that were not his own — a long-ago battle, a Lion historian, and what became of that historian's work — and saw, before the dream ended violently, someone burying it. Something told him the ancestors meant him to look, so he asked for leave and went and dug, and it was exactly where the dream put it. A sealed jar in a hidden cave. Inside: the historian's complete and intact field record of the Snow Plain, never edited into proper form, carrying the historian's own chop and seal. A draft agreement between Unicorn and Scorpion pledging the diamond mines as payment for assistance — assistance whose particulars are nowhere specified — bearing no signature, seal or chop, and so, he assumed, never ratified. A skeleton in a shallow grave nearby, which he did not know what to do with and hoped a spirit-speaker would. And one sealed letter, which he did not open, addressed to the least of the Bayushi — a title, he noted, that he had heard used of Bayushi Monban, which made him ask why it was on a letter that had lain sealed for three centuries.

He explained his choice of recipient with a candour that survived being read in public. Not Bayushi Monban, for reasons she would understand. Not Kakita Kazumi, who would take the glory for solving the Snow Plain, and that he could not allow. Not Doji Setsuna, whom he does not know, and who is in any case a Crane, and the Crane need no further encouragement. And then the reason under the reason: this history was lost to treachery and betrayal, he knows that personally, the battle is a hole in the Lion's records that shames a historian who died before the work could be written — they did not fail in their duties, and that truth must be known.

A second, smaller note was folded inside, meant for Shiba Midori alone. She steered Kitsuki Kaage off it by offering him the sealed letter instead.

Bayushi Monban claimed it in open court. That letter is addressed to me. Everyone here knows I am the least of the Bayushi. Even Akodo Akihito confirms it. I want my letter. Kakita Kazumi proposed Imperial custody and was told the ranking magistrate present is Imperial custody. Kitsuki Kaage observed there was no proof the phrase meant him, only a common appellation applied to him for some reason, and that a letter three centuries in its envelope could not be addressed to a living man. Unless we were there — which we were. Ide Tsubame was asked to help Shiba Midori hold the letter and wanted no part of it. Kitsuki Kaage struck the seal.

I do not know you. I do not know why our agent within the Lion Clan army insisted upon you being the beneficiary of the diamond mines as part of their payment for their involvement. Anyone called the least of the Bayushi deserves no consideration in the division of spoils; anyone known by that name is a shame and a failure to the clan, and should go to Lord Bayushi and grovel, beg forgiveness for every failure, and offer to end his life if the clan would be served by it — it most likely would not, though depending on what earned him the distinction, it might. And if he wants the mines his associate is so determined he should have, he may find a way to seize them himself, and he will be facing the writer and the writer's heirs for spoils already taken. So, good luck. In any case, cease being an embarrassment to the clan. It was signed with nothing but a stylised drawing of the Shosuro mon.

On second thought, Kaage-sensei, said Kakita Kazumi, I believe this might actually be addressed to Monban.

He was compromised on the spot. He stood, bowed, walked to the door, turned, and said it evenly: I am the only person here who has protected the divine couple. The rest of you wish you had such an opportunity. Then he went out without looking at anyone, and went over the three Crab blocking the corridor rather than around them. Kitsuki Kaage folded the letter away. I do not see anyone here to whom this letter could possibly be addressed. It is a strange artifact of history.

Hida Kasudo heard all of it through the screen, and could not see who was speaking. He knows the name Bayushi Monban: a friend of the Hida daimyō, and a man the Crab hold in some esteem.

Kitsuki Kaage read on. The field records were what they claimed — raw, unedited, and far too long to read out entire; he would have a Lion authenticate the documents and seals of that era. The last scroll was a second secret treaty, close to Bayushi Monban's but not identical, and with one difference that mattered: no mention of the least of the Bayushi anywhere. These mines go to the Shosuro Family, who had so aided the effort. Which was excellent news for Shosuro Aishi until he reached the end of it and read out what was not there: there are no signatures and seals as Magistrate Akihito described; none of the formalities attached to it that would support its evidence as being ratified or agreed to.

Shosuro Aishi moved to withdraw the whole matter to diplomacy between the two clans, and was told she had come too far to go back down. Diplomatically resolving this was the step you should have attempted before you brought it to the Emerald Magistrates. Kakita Kazumi proposed the mines be consigned to Imperial custody given how clouded the ownership had become.

Doji Setsuna then spent her sleepless night. Shinjō v. Asawa: the Unicorn failed to assert rights to a bridge's tax revenue for over three generations, and the court found the inaction constituted constructive abandonment under the doctrine of latches. The rights reverted to Imperial control at the Emperor's sole discretion — and twenty years later he gave them back to the Unicorn as a new grant, not as recognition of the old claim. And, to her clan's shame, Daidoji v. Yokoto: the Crane sanctioned for vexatious litigation despite using formally correct procedure, having summoned Lion to Otosan Uchi at staggered intervals over trivia, and ordered to pay damages to the Imperial Treasury and to the Lion for the abuse. Mines to Imperial control; Scorpion sanctioned for weaponising the court. Both entered as germane, circumstances not being identical. She also apologised to Kakita Kazumi for the Daidoji, and noted she had been unable to find any record of the Kakita doing likewise, which got the room and very nearly got Kitsuki Kaage.

Ide Tsubame answered that there is no precedent because there is nothing to reverse: the Unicorn were granted the whole of that land undivided by the Emperor himself, to end the war, and the mines were not carved out of the gift. Why undo an Emperor's settlement in another time of upheaval, for a claim the Scorpion's own advocate has called a farce? Kitsuki Kaage took the point and narrowed it: the Unicorn's original claim to the Snow Plain is not in dispute and never was. What is in dispute is whether there is legitimate cause to transfer these mines for services allegedly rendered.

Kakita Kazumi had put a laxative in the tea. He had not expected anyone to drink it. Ide Tsubame and Shiba Midori both had. Ide Tsubame got herself out of the chamber on courtesy and burned it out of her system by main force; Shiba Midori went shades of purple and held. Shosuro Aishi had asked a servant for sake instead and was spared, Bayushi Monban had refused the cup, and Kakita Kazumi was drinking from a different one. With her opponent out of the room, Shosuro Aishi took the floor unanswered and argued that the new evidence showed plainly that the Scorpion had rendered service, and that the Unicorn were therefore welching on a debt. It went well for her.

Kitsuki Kaage recessed until he could speak to experts on authentication, asked that they return with more focus on the facts and less of whatever that had been, and said that if anyone found Bayushi Monban he was expected back.

In the recess Doji Setsuna had Shosuro Aishi to a quiet, beautiful room beside her quarters, tea served, waited on — and said it plainly: she had made overtures to others of the Scorpion who lacked the broader vision, Kitsuki Kaage has not yet ruled, and so the offer comes to her first. If the Scorpion pledge non-interference in the coming Crane treaty, Doji Setsuna will throw her weight behind the Scorpion claim to the mines.

Shosuro Aishi checked the room for poison before she sat. She gave a preliminary yes. She cannot get an answer from her superiors in the time available and both of them knew it, so she chose to commit and beg forgiveness afterwards, with the request for written authority as cover. Doji Setsuna wrote it up with the Crane ritual for the drafting of treaties, verified it clean, and each of them staked ten honour on it.

Ide Tsubame went to find the Commandant. Shiba Midori took the cache scrolls away to read what she had loosed on the court, and then went looking for Ikoma Ichigo — who is the descendant of the very historian who died recording those pages — asking Kitsu Ayoko to guide her there. They found him in the library, working, with a box of scrolls beside him. Hida Kasudo was already in that library, having heard the word and gone straight to it.

Setsuna

She traded her case away in a private room and called it a pact. The offer she made Shosuro Aishi over tea is the one she made Shosuro Imako and never closed: non-interference in the Crane treaty, and in exchange Doji Setsuna throws her weight behind the Scorpion claim. Hours earlier she stood in front of Kitsuki Kaage and argued, with two cases at her back, that the mines belong to the Emperor and the Scorpion should be sanctioned for bringing the suit at all. Both of those cannot be true. She has agreed, in writing and under ten staked honour, to argue tomorrow against the thing she argued today, in a proceeding where she sits as an Emerald Magistrate.

There is a word for buying a magistrate's position with a treaty concession, and the fact that she is the magistrate does not change it. Shosuro Aishi knows exactly what she has bought — she committed her own clan without authority to buy it, and she will present it at home as an achievement.

What makes it worse is that she did not need to. The Head Librarian had already reported that the treaty was never on those shelves, which makes Bayushi Monban's sworn provenance a lie with a document behind it. Ide Tsubame had already moved to put Shinjō Kamo's authenticated hand beside it for an outside expert. The cache treaty came out of the ground unsigned and naming the wrong family. And the sealed letter told the whole room that three hundred years ago a Shosuro thought the mines were something the Scorpion would have to seize, because they were not owed. She was winning on the merits and in the documents, and she bought a result she was already going to get.

The cache is still the first clean evidence in this case and that has not changed. Everything else in that room has passed through somebody's hands — the Hall of Scribes accounts, the genealogies, three versions of one diary, and now two versions of a secret treaty. These were in the ground before anyone began editing. They carry the historian's own chop, and they are now waiting on authentication — which Kitsuki Kaage has correctly handed to the Lion, who are the only people qualified and also the people with the most to lose. Shiba Midori has gone to Ikoma Ichigo, whose ancestor died writing those pages, which is the right instinct and also the most interested witness imaginable. Somebody should say so before it becomes a problem.

And the letter did something nobody has drawn out yet. It confirms from a Shosuro hand, three centuries old, that the Scorpion had an agent within the Lion Clan army — which is Ikoma Akuyaku, said by the other side. It also proves the phrase the least of the Bayushi was already in use then, so it is not the modern coinage the forgery argument assumed. That cuts both ways and it should be thought about rather than celebrated. What it does not do is help the Scorpion: a clan told to seize what it wants is a clan that was never granted it.

One thing she should not lose in all of this. Bayushi Monban stood up in an Imperial court and claimed, by name and in front of witnesses, to be the man a dead Shosuro called a shame and a failure. He did it to get a letter he thought was his. Then he was told, in the Empire's hearing, to grovel and offer his life. Whatever he is doing in this case, that happened to him in public, and the last thing he said before he left was that he is the only person in the room who ever protected the divine couple. He may even be right.
