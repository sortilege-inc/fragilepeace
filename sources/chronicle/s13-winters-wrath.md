## Narrative

!lede The blizzard came down at dawn and made the whole field one flat white sheet. That cut both ways and nobody could agree which way it cut hardest: the [[Unicorn Clan]] know what is under that snow and the [[Lion Clan]] do not, but the fresh fall also buried the markers the Unicorn had set to give themselves the edge. What it certainly did was hide every hazard on the ground from both armies at once.

[[Ikoma Akuyaku]] came into the [[General’s Tent]] on a stretcher with his feet ruined by frostbite and handed over a map. The Unicorn were undaunted, in good spirits and ready; their cavalry was late or lost somewhere in the storm; their strength was about equal to the Lion army's. And the cold out there was severe enough to take men without anyone fighting them.

[[Akodo Toronoko]] — his wife, and a commander in her own right — went for balms and came back with boiling sage water and a mortar paste, and worked it into his feet while the medic [[Akodo Kayamayako]] called for gentle fire, carefully controlled, to bring the limb back. He got some feeling into it. He was confined to camp and told what a second exposure would cost him. [[Akodo Kagekatsu]] chose that moment to tell [[Akodo Toronoko]] that her husband's intelligence work had been inadequate, and she did not give an inch of it back to him.

Then [[Ikoma Akuyaku]] left his tent anyway, on those feet, to watch the battle.

The general's plan was a flanking force through the mountains to come at the Unicorn from behind — no cavalry, not in that country, but mountain-born ashigaru: archers under [[Kitsu Somalia]] and spearmen under [[Matsu Morozane]]. [[Akodo Toronoko]]'s archers held the left below, [[Akodo Kagekatsu]] the right.

The flanking force went up along the old mine paths. At the [[Frozen River]] the ice took [[Kitsu Somalia]]'s archers — it broke, one of them died in it and four more went into the water. [[Matsu Morozane]]'s spearmen crossed dry.

And then, higher up, [[Matsu Morozane]] found a path he had not been told about, running to [[Abandoned Mines|workings]] that were on no map anyone in that army carried. The snow over it had been disturbed and then deliberately swept flat again. Somebody had used that path recently and had gone to the trouble of covering it. He sent scouts.

Below, [[Akodo Toronoko]]'s archers put a volley into the fortified hill, pinned the enemy leader where he stood and put panic through his people. Then the flanking force came down off the mountain into the counter-attack: several Unicorn cohorts, and among them a shugenja everybody recognised, juggling fire on horseback. [[Kitsu Somalia]] threw a volley at that cohort before it could close and the forest ate most of it. Three cohorts hit [[Matsu Morozane]]'s spearmen at once, killed a great many of them, and broke the rest into panic.

He could not rally them. The command techniques would not take, so he went forward at the head of what was left instead. [[Kitsu Somalia]] told her archers to put the bows down and draw swords, and they went in and did real damage. [[Matsu Morozane]] took a Lightning Raid into the shugenja's cohort and opened it.

Word came up from below that [[Matsu Sakura]] had taken the [[Central Hill]] and driven the Unicorn off it — and that the Unicorn on that hill had been thin, because their units had gone to answer the flank. The hill was bought with the mountain force's casualties. Both armies were still on the field.

## Learned

- Akodo Toronoko: Ikoma Akuyaku's wife, and a commander of the Lion left. She treated his frostbite herself with sage water and paste, and when Akodo Kagekatsu blamed her for her husband's intelligence work in front of others, she gave him nothing.
- Akodo Kagekatsu: A rival officer who commanded the Lion right and used Akuyaku's injury as an opening against Toronoko.
- Akodo Kayamayako: The medic. Rewarmed a frostbitten foot with gentle fire, carefully controlled.
- Ikoma Akuyaku: Came back on a stretcher with his feet frostbitten and a map of the conditions. Reported the Unicorn undaunted, their cavalry late or lost in the storm, their strength about equal, and the cold lethal. Confined to camp, and left his tent on those feet anyway to watch the battle.
- Kitsu Somalia: Led the mountain-born archers over the Frozen River, where the ice broke and cost her a soldier and four in the water. Had her archers drop bows for swords when the cohorts closed.
- Matsu Sakura: Took the Central Hill and drove the Unicorn off it — against a thinned garrison, because their units had been pulled to answer the flanking force.
- Abandoned Mines: Workings on no map the Lion army carried, reached by a path above the Frozen River. The snow on the approach had been disturbed and then swept flat. Recent use, deliberately concealed.

## Setsuna

**This is the moment the mines enter the record, and they enter it as a secret somebody was keeping.**

[[Matsu Morozane]] came on a path to workings that were on no map, and the snow over that path had been walked and then swept. Not weather — work. Somebody had been up there in the days before two armies met below, and had taken the trouble not to be followed. Neither the [[Lion Clan|Lion]] nor the [[Unicorn Clan|Unicorn]] knew what was on that hill. Somebody did.

That sits against [[Shoshuro Amane]] at the parley. A [[Scorpion Clan|Scorpion]] who knows what the hill is worth, who wants the fighting stopped before either army wanders up it, who will not be read, and who leaves quietly when refused — and a covered path on the same hill in the same week. She is the reading. The memory does not prove she made those tracks, and the memory is not evidence anyone will accept anyway.

What she can use is the shape of it, and the shape is that the Scorpion's claim to the [[Old Diamond Mines|mines]] is older than the battle. Their case is not that they won the ground. It is that they already knew, and were already using it, and said nothing while two clans bled at the foot of the hill. That is a strong claim in a deed and an unspeakable one in a courtroom, which is why [[Shoshuro Aishi]] will never put it that way.

And the whole flanking manoeuvre went up along the old mine paths. Those workings were the road. No account of this battle records that, and it is checkable — a road leaves marks that outlast a war.
