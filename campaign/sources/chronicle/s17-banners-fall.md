## Narrative

!note **Only the last twenty minutes of this session were recorded.** What survives begins with the banner already being reached for and ends on [[Kitsu Somalia]] naming what she heard. The night's events after [[Crystal Pass]], the commune with the bound spirit that was promised at the end of the previous session, and most of the day's fighting have no source at all — the Archivist's own account of session 17 is drawn from the same twenty minutes and adds nothing.

!lede [[Shinjo Kamo]] brought his entire remaining force out for one all-out push. The [[Unicorn Clan|Unicorn]] came in just before dawn with fire arrows and screaming arrows — a reveille rather than an assault — and the [[Lion Clan|Lion]] formed up and went out to meet them, and what followed was grinding, punishing and expensive on both sides, and moved nobody. [[Akodo Toronoko]] was locked in a brawl with [[Shinjo Kamo]] in the middle of it.

[[Matsu Sakura]]'s order was not to kill him. It was to take his banner, which was fixed to his saddle.

[[Matsu Morozane]] took it with a piece of theatre and no honour spent at all. He had his soldiers ring the horse with spear points and lob something heavy and log-sized at the man on it, sent [[Shiguro Chinmoku]] up to give the animal a second thing to think about, came off his own horse, got a hand on the reins of [[Shinjo Kamo]]'s and hauled — and the general of the [[Unicorn Clan|Unicorn]] came down into the snow like a sack of potatoes, grumbling, and was dragged clear of his own mount. Then the banner came off the saddle and went up over [[Matsu Morozane]]'s head where [[Matsu Sakura]] could see it.

That was the Lion's strategic objective and it landed. The standard the whole [[Unicorn Clan|Unicorn]] line took its bearing from was in a Matsu's fist in front of both armies, their panic was already high and their discipline no longer enough to hold it down, and it was very near enough to break them outright.

[[Akodo Toronoko]] was back on her feet and walking at [[Shinjo Kamo]] to finish what she had started when the horns went. Riders came across the field under the chrysanthemum — heralds of [[The Emperor]] — and everyone on both sides turned to look at them.

Somewhere in the fighting [[Akodo Toronoko]]'s own husband was very nearly eviscerated, and it was her hand that did it. [[Ikoma Akuyaku]] lived. Nobody afterwards, [[Akodo Toronoko]] least of all, pretended the hand had been anyone else's.

And [[Kitsu Somalia]] was in the snow, stabbed four times over, having come into the fight on a wave of snow of her own making. She got herself through it — severely wounded in her fire ring now, and by her own reckoning it has become easier to list the rings she is not severely wounded in, and somehow she was still not bleeding. [[Kitsu Yue]] reached her and dragged her out, and the heralds arrived at exactly that moment and broke [[Kitsu Yue]]'s concentration, and [[Kitsu Somalia]] surfaced and went under again.

An emissary of the [[Imperial Houses]] was on the field to deliver judgement on the whole matter, which meant the question was no longer which army could hold the ground.

Then [[Kitsu Somalia]] came round and said the thing that outweighed the banner. Whoever had put four wounds in her had spoken, and she knew the voice, and it was a woman's — which surprised her, because she had always assumed the camp's healer was a man and had never thought to check. *I would recognize the voice, because it's not the first time she's patched me up.*

The spy was one of the Lion's own healers. And [[Kitsu Somalia]] is the only person alive who can put a name to her.

## Learned

- Matsu Morozane: Took Shinjo Kamo's banner off his saddle in front of both armies — soldiers ringing the horse with spears, the lion at its head, and Morozane on the reins hauling the Unicorn general into the snow. It was the Lion's strategic objective and it nearly routed the Unicorn on the spot.
- Shinjo Kamo: The Unicorn general. Committed his last reserve to one all-out push, fought Akodo Toronoko through it, and was thrown off his own horse and stripped of his standard. He came off the Snow Plain alive twice over, since Matsu Sakura had already refused to have him assassinated.
- Akodo Toronoko: Fought Shinjo Kamo to a standstill and was walking back at him when the heralds arrived. Somewhere in the same fighting she came within an inch of eviscerating her own husband.
- Kitsu Somalia: Stabbed four times and left in the snow, and lived — severely wounded in a second ring. Her attacker spoke, and she recognised the voice as a woman's and as one of the Lion's own healers, who had treated her before. She is the only person who can name the spy, and she was unconscious more than awake.
- Kitsu Yue: Got Somalia out of the snow. The Emperor's heralds arrived mid-working and broke her concentration.
- Ikoma Akuyaku: Nearly gutted by his own wife in the confusion, and survived it.
- The Emperor: Sent heralds under the chrysanthemum onto the field while both armies were still engaged, with an emissary of the Imperial Houses behind them to deliver judgement.

## Setsuna

**The [[Snow Plain]] was never decided. It was interrupted.** Two armies locked into each other, an Imperial emissary walking onto the field, and judgement arriving over the top of both of them. Nobody won that ground. Somebody was later *awarded* it, and the distance between those two verbs is the whole of the dispute she has been sent to weigh.

That reframes the deed. If the [[Old Diamond Mines|mines]] passed by Imperial judgement rather than by conquest, the question is not who held the hill three hundred years ago but what the Empire was told when it ruled — and the Empire was told by the same clans whose accounts she has already found silent about the mines existing at all. A ruling made in ignorance of a property's value is a very different document from one made with knowledge of it.

Two things to carry out of the memory, and one thing she should be honest about not having.

The [[Lion Clan|Lion]]'s spy was a healer in their own camp, a woman whose voice [[Kitsu Somalia]] knew, who had put four wounds in her and left her in the snow. [[Kitsu Somalia]] is the only one who can name her, and she was barely conscious. Whether she ever did name her is not in this memory.

And her own ancestor took an enemy general's banner off his saddle in front of two armies, with a lion, a ring of spearmen and a thrown log. It is the sort of thing a family remembers and a historian does not — and it is worth knowing whether the [[Ikoma Hall of Scribes|Hall of Scribes]] has it, because if the Lion account omits even their own trophies then the omissions are not selective, they are total.

What she does not have is most of this day. The memory she was given runs out, and what is on the other side of that gap — the night after the [[Crystal Pass]] was lost, the bound spirit in the black talisman that was never asked a single question, the hours of fighting before the banner — is simply not there to be examined.
