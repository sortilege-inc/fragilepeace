Emerald Diplomacy and Ancestral Echoes

Session 9·2025-05-06

The Lion must have an enemy that is not us. The Dragon kept sleeping, the Phoenix kept busy, the Crane kept occupied. The Crab must not triumph over the Shadowlands, because then they would turn elsewhere. And the Unicorn kept on the outside.

At the shrine Kitsu Taigen had been watching the ancestral realm misbehave around his daughter and had worked out why. Kitsuko Ayoko is adopted, from a family in a nearby prefecture, and is not of samurai stock; she is just short of her genpuku; and she has golden eyes, which is what made him suspect Kitsu blood in the first place. She was tested once, by the shrine maidens, because she wanted to be one — and rejected. They said the kami did not speak to her, or not loudly enough. It is a sore subject. She became a librarian instead.

Shiba Midori told them both what was actually happening: the talent does sometimes come up outside the great lineages, rarely, and the girl is manifesting for the first time. Rejoice. Your daughter has been chosen by the kami. Get her formally tested at the temples. Start a dream journal. It would appear that their estimation was in error, said her father, without heat.

The dreams are recurring and they are not hers. She is looking through somebody else's eyes at a mountain pass, a grassy field, and a hill that everything seemed to be fought over — with purple and burgundy banners on it, and, she is fairly sure, the Lion on the field. She has had it the last several nights and had put it down to too many days in the archives.

Kitsu Taigen knew what that meant and what it would cost him to say. If it is Kitsu Yue who is speaking to Ayoko, then — and here he stopped, and said it anyway — I and my line owe her a karmic debt, for I am descended from Matsu Sakura, one of the generals of Snow Field — who would in all likelihood have been killed there without Kitsu Yue, and who therefore might never have had descendants at all.

On the way out of the temple they passed a Scorpion: a veiled woman with a fan in one hand and a scroll in the other, whose eyes stayed on Bayushi Monban a moment too long. Doji Setsuna read curiosity in it — do I know you, and why would I know you here? Shiba Midori read something more useful: a woman entirely at home, radiating that of course she was supposed to be in this castle. The mon on the corner of her mask was Soshi. She bowed and offered to speak later of what brought them both so far north, pleasantly, with no edge on it anywhere — which told Bayushi Monban something on its own, because everybody knows who Bayushi Monban is, and she plainly did not.

Then Kitsu Takeko gave them the case at Kitsu Keep, and it is not the case the party expected.

The Unicorn Clan and the Scorpion Clan are disputing old diamond mines in the region. The Scorpion are not claiming to own them. They are claiming that the proceeds are owed to them under an ancient agreement, made in exchange for assistance they say they rendered at the Battle of the Snow Plain three centuries ago. The Unicorn answer that the Snow Plain is their patrimony and they owe the Scorpion nothing. The Lion Clan's own records of that battle contain no Scorpion anywhere in them, and no such agreement is deposited in their halls as a formal treaty would be. What decides it is documentation and the history behind it.

I will of course open all of our archives to you. Ask and it will be granted to you. The envoys coming with Kitsuki Kaage are Ide Tsubame for the Unicorn and Shosuro Aishi for the Scorpion — and that second name landed on Bayushi Monban like a stone. Shosuro Aishi is one of his own lord Shosuro Hametsu's senior negotiators. His lord has a stake in the case he is here to help adjudicate, and his oath of vassalage tells him exactly what he will be expected to support. He kept still. Shiba Midori noticed his feet.

Kitsu Takeko also drew a line the party should keep: if your investigation does come for the Unicorn, know that we do not oppose it. Our dispute with the Unicorn is of a more current nature, and is entirely unrelated to whatever happened or did not happen three centuries ago.

Then the swords came back, polished, oiled, and repaired — every notch and chip taken out of them. Returning a guest's weapons inside one's own walls is a polite fiction that says the guest is trusted not to use them. Shiba Midori, watching rather than listening, saw fire spirits moving around the daimyō like weather; she is said to be a prodigy, she has the golden Kitsu eyes, and she has the habit of a shugenja listening to something in the room that nobody else can hear. Doji Setsuna shifted to a personal register at the end and gave her a poem, rolled and tied, for later. It was accepted gladly. Milady Setsuna, I look forward to another opportunity to have you join us for tea.

The archives did not cooperate.

In the Ikoma Hall of Scribes — one of three great libraries on these grounds, beside the Military Library and the Kitsu Archives — Ikoma Ichigo had already begun looking into the Snow Plain in anticipation of being asked. The problem is simple and it is not fixable. The official scribe never recorded the battle. What exists is a compilation Ichigo has assembled out of other people's witness accounts, some Lion and some Unicorn, which is not how he would prefer to do it. Ikoma Ichigo's trouble is that the compilation is all there is. We do not have a formal account of the battle, which is to my shame, as one of my own ancestors was supposed to have been there. That they left us no permanent account is a terrible loss.

Doji Setsuna asked for the names of everyone whose testimony went into the compilation and where each came from, and he was only too glad to list his sources with their authentications.

Then he performed it. All work in the hall stopped and the room gathered, because the man is a master: a one-man drama, tears running down his face, the Unicorn stealing land the Lion had guarded for centuries, provocation after provocation finally answered. No Scorpion appear anywhere in it. The Unicorn take the victory. And when he played Matsu Sakura losing her temper at her own defeat, a fight broke out in the audience — students on their feet, somebody shouting there was treachery afoot, there is no way my revered ancestor would have been defeated by that.

What all the accounts do agree on is thinner than the drama: several days of bitter fighting in winter that came out roughly a stalemate until something stopped it.

Bayushi Monban came away certain the Scorpion had been there, on no evidence at all beyond how hard the room took it — it smells like something my lord would do. Shiba Midori made a point of memorising the faces of whoever protested loudest, to find later.

They also pulled the betrothal that started the war, and it is fresh — white paper, ink barely dry, only just rolled and deposited. Shinjō Altansarnai is the Unicorn Champion, and she was to marry Ikoma Anakazu — marrying into the Ikoma, which would have meant standing down from her office. It carries the Imperial seal, so the Chrysanthemum Throne was consulted and approved it. It was negotiated by Ide Tsubame on one side and an Ikoma on the other.

The Unicorn broke it. What Doji Setsuna could establish about why is that the concessions were thought far too great for what was being bought, and that the lady was reluctant on her own account as well. Bayushi Monban's objection was blunter: the Lion do this sort of bullying with their diplomats routinely, and they would never have tried it on the Crab or the Crane. They tried it on the Unicorn because the Unicorn have been back three hundred years and are still treated as though they had just arrived.

So Doji Setsuna went and stood in front of a map of Rokugan and worked out what it was for.

The Lion's northern borders are held by the Phoenix Clan and the Unicorn Clan. Their champion is already engaged to Isawa Kaede. A second marriage into the Unicorn would have closed the other one. They have just concluded a treaty with the Crane, conceding a large piece of the Osari Plains to get it. A major clan cannot declare war on a minor clan, which is why the Dragon Clan handed the Dragonfly Clan the mountain gate, which closes most of the rest. With all four borders quiet, the broken betrothal is not a grievance. It is a door. The Lion can go north and west into Unicorn lands and be entirely within their rights while doing it.

The same read, turned around, gave her the Crane's own position, which is worse than she likes. The lands have not recovered from the tsunami of a few years ago that took out the rice; agricultural surplus is the Crane's usual lever and it is gone. The Mantis have stopped discouraging piracy, so shipping and trade are down. Toshi Ranbo has been a net win and an expensive one. There is friction with the Crab over the Yasuki. And the Scorpion hold a long border with the Crane, so the last thing the Crane can afford is a Scorpion–Lion understanding.

She wrote all of it down in her notebook and said none of it out loud.

Around Kitsuko Ayoko the room kept moving. Papers stirred with no wind on them. During the recital the battlefield map came off the table, crossed the floor, and very nearly hit her on the head. Scrolls came down out of the shelves. Shiba Midori sat her down, calmed her, and told her plainly that her ancestor was trying to reach her — ancestors do not bother with subtlety when they want your attention, do they — and got back the only question that matters to a girl who has been told once already that she is not the sort of person spirits talk to: but why me? And then: the other shrine guardians did not see it. How do you know?

One of the scrolls that came down was open enough to read a name and a chop beside it — Kitsumitsuru, Shiba Midori's own great-grandmother on her mother's side — a Kitsu who married a Falcon, whose son married a Shiba. The scroll is an academic study of the several battles in which the rare Kitsu working appears, the one that calls ancestor spirits down into a fight, which only a handful have ever been able to do. It carries a warning with it. If you intend to call your ancestors, you must be prepared to behave as they would expect. The ancestors will not attend a summons from an honourless samurai.

Doji Setsuna got a lead of her own out of Ikoma Ichigo: that personal accounts of people who were at the battle sit in the Kitsu Archives, and that he believes a Matsu Setsuna was one of them. So she went there, with Ryu and So and a scribe, and set her parameters — not the high-ranking accounts everyone has already read, but the low ones, and specifically Unicorn families who ended up holding the territory in question afterwards. A staff officer on the losing side has to account for the field to his own superiors, and has no reason at all to leave the Scorpion out of it.

Elsewhere, a red-masked figure slipped out of the hall as the recital ended. Bayushi Monban excused himself, was escorted as far as the archives to see Doji Setsuna settled, and went after her.

She took him out onto the Path of Splinters, because a garden path is a better place for a quiet conversation, and gave him a name. Soshi Aisha, here at the behest of the Imperial Advisor.

What followed was the clearest statement of Bayushi Kachiko's policy that anybody in this party has heard.

The Advisor was against the betrothal, because it is not good for the Scorpion if the Lion have few enemies. The Empire must never be wholly at peace, or every other clan will turn on the Scorpion; pressure that cannot vent bursts the pot. So: the Lion must have an enemy, and it must not be the Scorpion. The Dragon are to be left to contemplate their own navels. The Phoenix are to be kept busy. The Crane are to be kept occupied — until the day the last one of them dies. The Crab must not be allowed to triumph over the Shadowlands, because a victorious Crab would turn its strength elsewhere. And the Unicorn are to be kept on the outside.

Further: they have been provoking internal war between the Lion families for generations and are disappointed that the Matsu have not yet risen against Akodo Toturi. They intend a Scorpion to win the Emerald Championship and consider it all but done. The Lion have already been manoeuvred to where they have no allies and not the Emperor's ear — my Lady Kachiko has already accomplished this. Practically. And on the throne itself: the Emperor is an old man. His sons are pliable. What is good for the Scorpion is good for the Empire. Meditate on that.

She also offered, without being asked, to solve his evidentiary problem. The Scorpion claim needs proof the Unicorn promised them something. They won. Therefore obviously we helped them. Bayushi Monban observed that the word he had heard in the library that morning was treachery. Always a dish served by the Scorpion, is it not? — I will work on making the appropriate documentation available to you.

She knows his history and used it. He once refused a Scorpion who asked him for help, and called in a third party to have that Scorpion arrested. No wonder your lord hates you. Serve the clan here and he might find himself in favour, in power, and not chained to a puffed-up Crane. He answered that he is an Emerald Magistrate and his integrity cannot be questioned, and later, when she pushed, the thing that is very nearly a challenge: lowest Bayushi, highest Shosuro. There is a difference.

He put a proposal of his own on the table instead. Not one betrothal but two, running both ways — a Lion to a Unicorn and a Unicorn to a Lion, with no humiliating drop in station for anybody, and Winter Court coming. Would the Emperor not be pleased if four of the clans worked together to stabilise his northern border? Her lady, she allowed, would be quite satisfied with an arrangement that made the Unicorn happy at the Lion's expense — but if the Lion are at peace with the Unicorn, the Lion's enemy becomes the Lion, and after that it becomes the Scorpion. She did not think it would fly. She did not tell him not to try.

He gave up something to keep the channel: that they were attacked on the road by people who looked like shinobi, which she found very interesting indeed. He asked whether her lady knows the Doji he protects — peripherally — and offered tea, meditation, a ride, anything that might fill in the blanks. He asked to have missives carried to his Champion and to his own daimyō when her messengers next go out, and she agreed. And he offered, in as many words, to find the Lion an enemy, or at least a distraction.

They also talked about why the Lion are like this. Toshi Ranbo was taken from them. Akodo Toturi's predecessor promised it to his Matsu fiancée as a wedding gift and died trying to take it — shot in the face with an arrow by the Crane Champion, not killed in any duel. That fiancée is alive, and she is Akodo Toturi's principal rival for the leadership of the clan. The Emperor stopped the fighting and left the status quo standing, and the recent Lion–Crane treaty did not so much as mention the city. Three years ago. Akodo Toturi is not yet secure in his own championship.

And Bayushi Monban explained, to a Scorpion, where he actually stands: after the incident with the Emperor I am the bug that serves well enough to Matsu Tsuko. She knows my loyalty to the Empire comes before my duty to my clan. Hence my problems with my lord. — I will always choose the Empire. Always. — Then you should always choose the Scorpion. What is good for the Scorpion is good for the Empire, don't you know.

They went back to the archives to keep digging, with a preliminary report owed and the envoys already on the road.

Setsuna

The case is legible now, and it is not the property dispute she was briefed on. The Scorpion want a cut, not a deed, and they want it on the strength of an agreement nobody can produce, arising from a battle that was never officially written down at all. The scribe who should have recorded it did not, and the man whose job it is to know tells her that failure was his own ancestor's and calls it his shame. Either the Scorpion were not there and the claim is air, or they were there and the Lion never wrote them down — and the Lion are holding the archive, hosting the negotiation, and fighting a war on the other side of it.

Her line of attack is the losing side's staff work and the low-ranking accounts nobody has bothered to read, and specifically the Unicorn families who ended up administering the ground afterwards. She also has a name: a Matsu Setsuna left a personal account, and it should be in the Kitsu Archives.

The strategic picture is the thing she should be frightened of, and she worked it out herself and told nobody. The Lion have quieted three borders by treaty and marriage and were one wedding away from the fourth. The betrothal was not courtship; it was the last door being closed. The Unicorn broke it because the price was humiliating, which hands the Lion a grievance the Emperor's own seal makes respectable. There is now nowhere for Lion aggression to go except north and west, and every legal obstacle has been cleared in advance.

And the Crane cannot afford any of it. No rice since the tsunami, no shipping while the Mantis look the other way, Toshi Ranbo won at a price, the Crab unfriendly over the Yasuki, and a long Scorpion border. She is adjudicating a dispute between two clans while her own is the least able in the Empire to survive the outcome.

She now knows what the Scorpion want, and she does not know that she knows it. Bayushi Monban heard Bayushi Kachiko's entire doctrine laid out on a garden path — every clan assigned a condition, the Lion families deliberately set against each other for generations, the Emperor described as an old man with pliable sons, and an offer to forge the evidence in her own case. Whether any of that reaches her depends on him. He spent the conversation telling a Scorpion that he chooses the Empire over his clan, and also asking her to carry his letters, and also offering to arrange her a meeting with the woman he protects.

Two things she cannot reach with paper. A spirit in the Ikoma Hall of Scribes is trying to show a child from the Snow Plain what happened at the Snow Plain, and being ignored because a shrine once told that child the kami do not speak to her. And Kitsuko Ayoko is dreaming the battle from the inside, through eyes that are not hers, on the nights before the one witness anyone can still question is due to be asked.

Kitsu Takeko has now given her cavalry, an open archive, a promise about her husband, and an invitation to tea, and has asked for nothing at all. That balance will not stay where it is.
