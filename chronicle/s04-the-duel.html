The Duel

Session 4·2025-04-01

A duel that both lords have declined to sanction can settle nothing. It is fought anyway, because the alternative is that the grievance keeps.

The Emperor knows that Matsu troops have crossed into Unicorn land and has said nothing. Until he speaks, or until the two clans agree between themselves, the war continues by default — and a war that continues by default is one nobody has to answer for. Shinjo Mono carried that news to the Ide at Otosan Uchi with his Emerald Magistrate's badge newly around his neck, then cut straight for Loyalty Castle to get back to the front.

The Lion Clan intercepted him on the road. They were ready to take him prisoner; the badge changed that to an escort, which is a different word for the same journey. At Loyalty Castle he found Doji Setsuna already there, and the two of them spoke while Lion observers watched from a distance and did not pretend otherwise.

The courtyard had been busy before either of them arrived. Bayushi Monban had insulted the daimyō's kitchen and been set upon by Lion bushi for it. A monk had stepped in with the Dragon teaching — neither shall I — and offered to take a blow for every blow they landed, which so confounded the arithmetic of the beating that Bayushi Monban was able to walk out of it during the commotion of Shinjo Mono's arrival. He kept the bruises. He also kept the belief that the insult had been offered to him first.

That evening Matsu Koda challenged Kakita Kazumi, with a warning not to set cowardly terms. Kakita Kazumi set them anyway, and set them carefully: until one man taps out, any weapon or armour a man owns, no fatal blow — their lords had sanctioned nothing, and a corpse would have made the duel a matter for magistrates rather than for the two of them. Matsu Koda accepted. Then Kakita Kazumi arrived just short of the hour at which he would have forfeited, deliberately dishevelled, and put on Topaz Champion armour in front of the crowd with a Topaz Champion katana at his hip. It is a way of fighting the first exchange before the first exchange.

It did not work. Kakita Kazumi opened well — a hit, and a yoroi-doshi, the armour-piercing knife his grandfather gave him, readied for the gap — but Matsu Koda answered with Striking as Water, which takes a man's resistance down and leaves the next blow to do more work than it should. Koda missed once and corrected it. He pressed and kept pressing. Kakita Kazumi tried a showy feint and overreached on it, and the finishing blow put him on the ground and rolled him onto his back with a blade at his throat. He yielded. Doji Setsuna and Shinjo Mono watched from among three Lion minders and a number of the bushi who had beaten Bayushi Monban the day before.

Matsu Koda declared his own grievance settled and named four more still owed. Doji Setsuna took that up on the spot, appealing to the custom that a duel resolved settles the matter for the whole of the offended party, not for one man of it. Kakita Kazumi added the other half — that a Lion had insulted Shiba Midori's ancestors in this castle, and that the Ruby Champion among others could attest to it. Matsu Koda reddened, bowed to both of them, and said he had not been told the circumstances under which his brother was wounded and regretted humbling a man without cause. If it makes you feel all the better, Kakita Kazumi said, I have suffered worse for less.

At dinner the party was seated near the daimyō's table, which is a statement about them made in furniture. Shiba Midori took the measure of two Kitsu shrine maidens at the next table and found them idle but spiritually clean; asked to treat Bayushi Monban's injuries they declined, on the grounds that bruises earned by offending their lord were deserved — and noted, unprompted, that Kakita Kazumi's were not. Kakita Kazumi paid for the seating by performing, and gave the courtiers the Phoenix Lands and the ninja in the rain, and left them wanting the blue flame ninjas.

Then a plate came to Bayushi Monban with a conspicuous cut of meat on it and a note under it: a love poem, signed Yushi Sama, scented with cherry blossom, promising to wait beneath the Cherry Tree. He matched the scent to the earlier correspondence and understood, some days late, that the first gift had not been an insult at all — which meant the kitchen he had insulted had done nothing to him. It cost him honour and it gave him back his contempt for courtesy, which is the sort of trade the Empire makes constantly. He went to the tree at night to end the business, citing his betrothal. Kakita Kazumi followed and watched from cover in case it was a trap. It was not a trap. It was a catamite in a woman's kimono, powdered and speaking in falsetto, who called Bayushi Monban Chan and remembered a bed shared a fortnight ago — with his elder brother. On learning which brother he had, the admirer fled. Kakita Kazumi withdrew without being seen.

The letter of passage reached Shinjo Mono in the morning, clearing the party onto the Lion Roads toward the Castle of the Swift Sword: Crossroads Fortress the first day, past Rugashi the second, the castle on the third. They travelled in ordinary clothes rather than armour, which through Lion Lands in wartime is a courtesy and also a calculation. At every postal station the papers came out and Lion Clan officials read Shinjo Mono more carefully than they read the papers. Columns went north past them. Kakita Kazumi foraged what would poison a man and prepared it; Shiba Midori tried and failed to close Bayushi Monban's wounds; Bayushi Monban spent a night getting a formal letter's seals right against exemplars. Shiba Midori asked the shrine maidens what the kami were like where they were going and was told: well-tended, responsive, and mostly fire, with water and earth beneath. The road was good and nothing happened on it. The investigation would begin when they arrived.

Setsuna

She got two things at Loyalty Castle and they cost her nothing, which is worth noting because most of what she wants will not be cheap.

The first was the custom. Matsu Koda had settled with Kakita Kazumi and reserved four more grievances; she closed all four by naming the rule that a duel answers for the whole offended party. It held — Koda bowed to her as well as to Kakita Kazumi. The Lion at this castle will now have a harder time reopening the matter piecemeal, and she has established in front of Lion witnesses that she argues from custom rather than from clan.

The second was a refusal, which is still information. She approached a well-connected courtier for a letter supporting Shinjo Harunobu's release — pressed as her own suit and deliberately kept separate from the general exchange of hostages — and was told to wait: such requests belong after hostilities, and her husband is safest where he is until matters resolve. That is the standing answer and she should expect it again from anyone below the rank that can overrule it. The same courtier passed on a rumour that the Emerald Championship may be delayed because the clan champions are committed to the war. She answered that Shinjo Harunobu would suffocate without the sky, and kept the conversation warm.

She did not learn what happened under the Cherry Tree, and Bayushi Monban has not told her.
