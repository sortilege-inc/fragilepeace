Ikoma Ichigo

Lion

What is known

S9The Lion historian at the Hall of Scribes. The official scribe never recorded the Snow Plain at all; what Ichigo has is a compilation from other witnesses, Lion and Unicorn, and the failure was his own ancestor's, which he names as his shame. His recital gives the Unicorn the victory and contains no Scorpion. What the accounts agree on is a bitter several-day winter battle that came out roughly a stalemate until something stopped it. He will list every source and its authentication on request.
S19Ikoma Hideri's descendant, and says so — her lost record is his family's shame, and he gave up hope of her vindication long ago. Could not find the medic in his own clan's genealogy. Read Setsuna's poem correctly and told her the phrasing needed polish.
S21Will testify to what the documents attest and refuses to speculate about what they omit. Holds that acknowledging the Empire's intervention rather than naming a victor would be a balm. Confirmed that the treaty is silent on who won the battle — the Emperor awarded the Snow Plain to the Unicorn and its income to the Lion for a term, and acknowledged only that a dispute existed. Produced supply documents pointing at a third party he cannot name.
S24The descendant of the historian who died recording the Snow Plain, and the man Midori went to find to authenticate the field records.
S25Authenticated the Imperial-Sealed Official Treaty and confirmed it validates the Unicorn claim. Found Monban's secret treaty properly formed but never sealed or entered by the Imperial House, which by his account leaves it worthless until it is. Observed that one of the two secret documents appears to be new ink on old parchment, and would not say the two were written at the same time. Wept over Ikoma Hideri's recovered history, identified her chop on sight, and returned with library samples that proved the match beyond doubt.
S27Will present the recovered true history of the Snow Plain at the next winter court, and knows Emerald Magistrates had a hand in it.

Each line is what she took from the session it cites, and nothing else. The Archivist's own account of this person is not reproduced here — it blends what she saw with what she did not.
