Rice Paddies

The Rice Paddies, a crucial battleground near the encampment of the Lion Clan, are defined by their treacherous, boggy terrain, thick mud, and a fresh layer of snow that blankets the landscape. This snow-covered expanse creates a challenging environment for movement and combat, with visibility severely hampered by heavy snowfall. The paddies are flanked by hills, adding to their strategic significance, and have been fortified by the Unicorn Clan, who have established extensive defenses, including watchtowers and sharpened stakes, indicating their long-standing presence and preparation for conflict.

The importance of the Rice Paddies was highlighted during a war council led by Ikoma Akuyaku, who marked the location on a map to emphasize the need for careful planning and intelligence gathering regarding Unicorn Clan activities. He suggested a cautious approach, proposing an eastern route through a nearby Peasant Village and into the mountains for scouting, although this would take over a day. The council was aware of the Unicorn Clan's limited cavalry presence, as reported by scouts, and expressed concerns about potential traps laid within the paddies. Ultimately, they resolved to advance toward the Rice Paddies while planning for Ikoma Akuyaku to infiltrate the Unicorn Clan camp during the forthcoming engagement.

As the Lion Clan forces, commanded by Akodo Kagekatsu and Matsu Morozane, advanced through the snow-laden paddies, they faced numerous obstacles that hindered their progress. Upon reaching the paddies, they encountered a Unicorn Clan force intent on intercepting them to keep the battle away from the village, which was believed to be supplying the Unicorn Clan forces. Akodo Kagekatsu ordered a charge to seize control of the village position, and the Lion Clan soldiers managed to push back the Unicorn Clan forces, forcing them to retreat toward the village. However, this engagement resulted in a devastating ambush by the Unicorn Clan, leading to significant casualties among the Lion Clan forces, underscoring the perilous nature of the Rice Paddies as a site of conflict.
