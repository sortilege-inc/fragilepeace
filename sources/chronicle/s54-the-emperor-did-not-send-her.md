## Narrative

!lede The treaty was sealed by an Imperial Herald who then explained, standing over the
urn, that the envoy they had buried for it had never been sent.

[[Bayushi Monban]] came out of the west on foot. The wind kami set him down at the edge of
the forest and would not lift him again; he had been two days without water and longer
without food, and what he was wearing had been bought off a stall weeks ago and had not
survived the desert. He had one sword. He had no [[Emerald Magistrates|Emerald
Magistrate]]'s badge — it is at a river shrine, traded by his fox for the water that kept
him alive.

Four [[Lion Clan|Lion]] with yari met him at the picket. He announced himself as an
Emerald Magistrate, which was not believable, and [[Kakita Kazumi]] — standing close
enough to help — looked him up and down and said he had never seen the man before in his
life, and suggested the cells. He then followed at a distance, whistling, for the whole
of what came next.

What saved him was a name. He asked to be taken to the Lady [[Matsu Tsuko]], and when she
looked at the scruffy rōnin in front of her and said she knew no such person, he gave her
back a memory instead of an argument: that she had once called him the Scorpion who serves
well enough, with a blade at his throat; that she and her betrothed had declined cherry
wine, because she would not willingly drink with a Scorpion without Imperial command; and
that the Emperor had been pleased with how quickly the two of them came back from the
bandits. Seven horses and seven riders, dispatched messily and effectively.

*You have an interesting memory*, she said. Then she had him thrown in the river, and told
him to present himself again once he had composed himself, and she would consider it.

[[Doji Setsuna]] and [[Shiba Midori]] were at breakfast when he was frog-marched past.
[[Doji Setsuna]] noted it, said she would be along shortly, and finished her tea. At the
riverbank, from behind a fan, she asked whether he was well, established that negotiations
were concluding successfully, and then asked the question she had actually come to ask.
*Did you take my horse?* It came back, he observed. If he ever touched [[Kogarashi]]
again, she told him, she would have him beaten until his legs did not stand. Then, from
the other side of the camp: *it is good to have you back.* She sent [[Ryu]] and [[So]] along
afterwards with clothes.

He learned about the duel from [[Shiba Midori]] and then from [[Kakita Kazumi]], who was
precise about it: [[Akodo Kage]] had called him a liar, he had demanded satisfaction, and
the [[Lion Clan|Lion]] had put [[Akodo Akihito]] forward to answer it. He had been the
only bushi the magistrates had, because the other one had gone west into the desert alone
without telling anybody. [[Bayushi Monban]] pressed for the name of the Lion who had done
it and was refused — *I am not telling you because you are going to go off and cause
trouble with the leaders of the Lion Clan.*

The formal signing was that morning: the terms already agreed, the arithmetic already
done, and nothing left but two Champions putting their chops to linen in a tent pitched
between the two armies. [[Akodo Akihito]] was there with a fresh bandage over his eye and
a repaired kabuto. The chops went on.

Then horns, and green banners with the [[Miya Family|Miya]] mon coming over the horizon
with an honour guard twice the size of the ceremony it was arriving at.

The Imperial Herald is [[Miya Satoshi]] now — the son, not the father, the office handed
over at the last Winter Court. He rode up, waited to be received, and delivered the
Emperor's word: by order of his majesty [[Emperor Hantei Hantei|Hantei XXXVIII]], the Lion
and the Unicorn are commanded to cease hostilities immediately and settle the disagreement
between them.

[[Akodo Toturi]] answered that the Emperor had already sent word, and that they had
complied. The Herald was, for a moment, confused.

[[Doji Setsuna]] asked leave to speak and said she was there on behalf of the late
[[Miya Misato]], who had been sent with precisely that missive, and whose work she had
seen through to completion.

*Miya Misato is a low-level functionary at [[Otosan Uchi]]. She would never have been
given this order.*

She had the reconstructed writ brought out. The Herald read it and allowed that the form
was proper, mostly, and that the seal was genuine — and observed that its owner was dead.
She told him plainly how it had been salvaged: an attack in the night, the original
carried into a fire pit by the man who took it, one fragment pulled out, and the wording
rebuilt from the memories of the four magistrates who would testify to it.

He affixed his own seal to the treaty. It is in keeping with the Emperor's desires.

And then the ground moved. A rumble out of the west, and a plume standing up on the
horizon in the unmistakable shape of a mountain opening — the mountain [[Bayushi Monban]]
had been standing on a few days earlier. Lion soldiers made the sign against evil spirits
and asked one another whether the kami had just declared the war unfinished.
[[Kakita Kazumi]] observed to nobody in particular that a volcano had gone off in the west,
that [[Bayushi Monban]] had just come back from the west, and that [[Bayushi Monban]] had
left with a fire spirit. The Herald declined to be interested: the vagaries of the elements
bear no relevance to the momentous decisions made here.

With the Champions occupied entertaining him, the magistrates had an hour to themselves
for the first time in months. [[Bayushi Monban]] spent it with his niece — a young
[[Lion Clan|Lion]] who fought at Ponto Pass, cousin to [[Akodo Akihito]], and no relation
to him by blood at all. She calls him uncle because they have stood against the Shadowlands
together, which is also why he calls [[Akodo Akihito]] his brother in first enemy blood.
She had watched her cousin break the [[Unicorn Clan|Unicorn]] flanking attempt and take
their commander in single combat. He borrowed a katana off a Lion of lower standing to
replace the one he no longer has, promised to speak well of her to her daimyō, and began
arranging for her to be attached to somebody going to Winter Court.

Then, with only the magistrates in the room, he told them where he had been. His katana
was seized in [[Unicorn Clan|Unicorn]] country and is on its way to [[Shosuro Hametsu]]
— *the katana is your honour* — and he had been carrying an [[Ifrit]] inside his body,
which is why he vanished, and why he could not send word. He freed it in the desert on the
terms he had given it. It was pleased. It killed the five men who had staked him out on a
rock to cut it out of him, and then it went home.

[[Doji Setsuna]] and [[Kakita Kazumi]] looked at him, and then at the smoke on the horizon.

The remains had been gathered and burned and put in an urn while he was away. She carried
them to the Herald herself, with [[Bayushi Monban]] attending, and gave an account of
[[Miya Misato]] as she had known her: brave, disciplined in pursuit of her duty against
every obstacle, attacked repeatedly in the [[City Of The Rich Frog]] and again on the road,
and standing through all of it but the last.

The Herald was somber, and then he laid out the timeline. Word of the
[[Lion Clan|Lion]]–[[Unicorn Clan|Unicorn]] conflict reached the capital less than a week
ago, from the Unicorn ambassador. [[Miya Misato]] left months before that. She could not
have been dispatched, and the better question is whether she was dispatched at all.

[[Doji Setsuna]] asked who holds the Imperial seal. The Emperor. The Herald, on request,
when his office requires it. And, he allowed, the Imperial advisor has similar access.

She did not name anybody. She observed that it was interesting, and asked who stands to
gain from the Lion and the Unicorn at each other's throats, and said she would of course
never make an accusation in any direction, and that she would never suspect the Emperor's
chief advisor of anything that could not also be laid at the Empress's door.

It was [[Bayushi Monban]] who made sure the failure was somebody else's. The magistrates
had voiced their suspicions at the time, he said, and been overruled:
[[Governor Miya Tetsuya]] gave the envoy's security to the Lion and asked the magistrates
only to accompany her, and they went because he asked and because he is of the Imperial
family. He said it, by his own account, so that none of the blame could be laid at their
feet. The Herald did not accept the framing and said so — by their own testimony they had
been asked, not commanded.

It is true in outline. It is also true that [[Doji Setsuna]] was very nearly the only one
at that table who had argued for a proper Imperial escort, and that the man spreading the
failure across all of them had not been in the city when it was decided. She let it stand.

Then he declined to share the credit. He had not supported the venture, his chop is not on
the treaty, and he would not claim what he had argued against — having just claimed, on
everyone's behalf, that none of them had a choice.

The Herald's ruling is the shape of the thing going forward. The official account will be
that [[Miya Misato]] took the matter under her own authority when she heard of the
conflict, without orders and not in a right mind. He suspects the orders were forged or
dispatched without the Emperor's knowledge. The magistrates are not to be held responsible
for acting on what they took to be legitimate; the Lion who volunteered to stand as her
yojimbo would be the one to answer for the failure, and he does not intend to take him up
on it. *You three are responsible for this peace. You three cut through the lines of clan.
You three did the duty and upheld the peace of the Emperor.*

*We shall be watching your career with interest.*

[[Doji Setsuna]] asked to be formally released from her binding to [[Miya Misato]] and was
granted it on the grounds that [[Miya Misato]] had never had the authority to bind her in
the first place. She keeps the title and the standing that came with it. She raised the
[[Phoenix Clan|Phoenix]] question before he could leave without hearing it — a woman
divorced for a marriage that never happened, unproven, and therefore a matter for fact-
finding at Winter Court — and the Herald thanked her for the dimension. [[Shiba Midori]]
undertook to write to her own clan so their witnesses are ready. And [[Doji Setsuna]] asked
whether a letter of respect to the Emperor might travel back with his couriers. It might.

The rest is scheduling. The Lion–Unicorn settlement is postponed to Winter Court, where
other negotiators may be fielded and another arbiter may be chosen. The magistrates are
already invited. And the Emerald Championship has been put off until next summer — the
Emperor will not have his court made the venue for something that acrimonious — with the
Ruby Champion continuing in the role until it can be convened.

[[Shinjo Harunobu]] is one of the five held until the matter closes, so a complicated
arrangement was made instead: released into the custody of a guarantor heading south, on
recognisance, as part of an envoy going to reinforce the [[The Wall|Kaiu Wall]]. Which
means he will be at Winter Court.

And a courier had a letter for [[Bayushi Monban]] that had been chasing him for weeks.
[[Hida Chizuzu]] is lost on the other side of the Wall. His warning reached her father a
week after the man had already sent her, and Lord Hida would like him to come and explain
how he knew.

## Setsuna

**The writ was never real, and it does not matter, because it worked.** Twenty sessions of
service, a dead sixteen-year-old, a treaty sealed between two armies — and the office that
was supposed to have ordered all of it had never heard of any of it. [[Miya Satoshi]] read
the document, found the form proper and the seal genuine, and set his own beside it. The
peace stands. What she has learned is that it was standing on nothing the whole time and
held anyway.

She should sit with what that says about her instrument rather than about her luck. The
thing that made the treaty binding was never the writ. It was ten honour apiece staked in
front of witnesses, and two commanders who could not afford to be seen breaking it, and a
Herald who arrived afterwards and preferred a finished peace to an awkward question. She
built a structure that did not need its own authority to be genuine, and she did it without
knowing that was what she was doing.

**The name she did not say is the name she has had since session forty-seven.** A ghost
that could not lie said the Lord [[Bayushi Family|Bayushi]] commands there be no peace with
the [[Unicorn Clan|Unicorn]]. Tonight an Imperial Herald volunteered, unprompted, that the
Imperial advisor has access to the seal. She asked the question and let him assemble it
himself, which is the only way that accusation can ever be made — by somebody else, out of
facts she handed over one at a time. It is also the second time she has watched
[[Bayushi Kachiko]]'s method described to her and recognised it: entirely within the law,
entirely against its spirit.

She lied once and should count it honestly. She told the Herald that the two commanders had
offered seppuku and that she had bound them to the work instead; what happened was that she
stood in front of two armies and told them to do their jobs or open their bellies. It cost
nobody anything and it made her sound merciful, which is the kind of small vanity that is
hardest to notice in oneself.

**The second falsehood in that tent was not hers, and that is the one to watch.**
[[Bayushi Monban]] told the Herald the magistrates had been overruled about the escort,
which spreads a failure across three magistrates and a governor and off him in particular.
[[Doji Setsuna]] was the one who had argued for a proper Imperial escort at the time. She
said nothing while he said it, and the shelter he built covered her too. Letting a useful
untruth stand is not the same as telling one, and she has spent fifty-four sessions
knowing it is not the same as innocence either. What is new is that this time somebody else
did the building, and she has no idea what it will cost when he asks for something.

She got the release and she kept the title. The binding is void because
[[Miya Misato]] never had the standing to make it, which is the cleanest possible exit and
also the coldest thing anybody has said about that girl since she died. The status award
stays. She is an acknowledged advisor to nobody now, which is a strange and useful place
to stand.

And the [[Phoenix Clan|Phoenix]] question is now on an Imperial record. She raised
[[Asako Ikari]] herself, unprompted, to the one man who could carry it to the capital,
and framed it as unproven and therefore requiring discovery — which is exactly the frame
that keeps it alive without letting anyone rule on it early. [[Shiba Midori]] is preparing
witnesses. The woman herself is still missing, and every table she is not standing in front
of finds her more convenient.

Everything now runs to Winter Court: [[Toshi Ranbo]], the divorce, the Phoenix claim,
[[Asako Ikari]], the Lion–Unicorn settlement, her husband, and — because it has been moved
to next summer and will be argued about all winter — the Emerald Championship. She has
spent the whole campaign moving things there. It is nearly here, and she will arrive at it
with a Herald watching her career with interest, which is a compliment and a leash.
