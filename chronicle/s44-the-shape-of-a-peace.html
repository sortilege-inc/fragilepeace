The Shape of a Peace

Session 44·2026-04-27

The war is not about the marriage and it is not about the border. It is about an insult, and an insult in Rokugan is answered with a blade — which is what an army is, when the parties are large enough.

The column went west with the magistrates in the middle of it: Lion soldiers immediately around them, and the whole of that inside a larger box of Unicorn riders. It was plain enough who was setting the pace. Shiba Midori had Bayushi Monban on his feet again. So they spent the march arguing about what the Emperor had actually told them to do.

Doji Setsuna put the first item plainly: the Lion are on the wrong side of the border and have to come off it. Bayushi Monban agreed, and wanted both sides cooled, because more fighting is the one outcome the Emperor has ruled out.

Then Bayushi Monban made the observation that reframed everything. There are no Lion outposts behind them. Nothing was garrisoned on the way up. What the Lion have been doing is burning and desecrating — no ground taken, nothing held. That is not a conquest. It is a punitive expedition, and it means they never wanted the land.

Doji Setsuna weighed the fault on both sides. The Unicorn broke a betrothal the Emperor had sanctioned. The Lion answered it themselves instead of bringing it to him. Then she framed the legal position she meant to rule on: the Emperor commanded peace, and a command to make peace necessarily overrules any standing objection to the thing that broke it, because peace means the fighting stops. Whatever satisfaction the Lion have taken so far is all the satisfaction they get. Bayushi Monban agreed. Both clans are disobeying the Emperor, and both will be told to stand down.

He also proposed to deliver it rudely: the Lion go home and stop behaving like bullies, and the Unicorn send someone to the Imperial Court to learn how this is done properly. Doji Setsuna saw the use in that. Coming from a low-status Scorpion it is a different message entirely — the Crane stay courteous while the Scorpion carries the insult.

She asked Shiba Midori whether the spirit was still held inside Bayushi Monban, and noted it as something they could threaten a party with if it came to that. Bayushi Monban admitted he had been thinking the same thing. She put it away rather than picking it up. Let's not do it now.

Kakita Kazumi went off to work Matsu Maro instead — the Lion general being nearer to hand than the Moto one further west — and got the betrothal's actual terms out of her.

Shinjo Altansarnai was to stand down as Unicorn Champion, take the name Ikoma, and become Lord Ikoma's wife. The Unicorn were to cede border territory, Hisomori Village among it, settling claims that have been argued over for centuries. Matsu Maro called it amicable and said it would have ended the border war for good. She also said she had never understood why Shinjo Altansarnai agreed to it, since the Champion stood to lose a great deal of standing by it. And she passed on a rumour: Shinjo Altansarnai has a lover, not a husband, and a daughter.

Kakita Kazumi pushed on the honour of it and got the answer that matters. From a Lion view the refusal was not a snub, it was a direct insult — and in Rokugan a direct insult is answered with a duel. Bayushi Monban asked what clans do when the parties are armies, and the answer is the thing happening outside the tent. Matsu Maro called Kakita Kazumi very perceptive and confirmed it: this is about Lion honour, not about the marriage and not about Lord Ikoma's feelings. Find another way to restore the honour and the Lion can be talked down.

Asked whether the contract carried a penalty for breach, she scratched her head and did not know. Doji Setsuna guessed that writing one in at that level would itself have been an insult — who would insert a clause anticipating that a Champion might renege? Nobody argued.

Doji Setsuna then asked why Shinjo Altansarnai had held herself back. Either she is a free spirit who baulked at the last, or a better offer appeared. She went through the field and found nothing: Akodo Toturi married, the Hida, Yoritomo and Scorpion Champions married, Doji Hotaru and the Phoenix Champion the wrong sex for it, the Dragon Champion's identity not even known. No clan-level match was available. The only way up from where she stands is an Imperial marriage, which would move her out of the bushi ranks entirely.

So she asked Matsu Maro outright whether an Imperial suitor existed. Matsu Maro was caught flat-footed and allowed that one could be supposed. Doji Setsuna noted, to herself, that a claim like that would be very hard for anyone to disprove.

From there the strategies stacked up. First, a poison pill: rule that the betrothal must be honoured, and attach reparations for the punitive expedition payable by the Lion only if the marriage goes through — set high enough that they cannot carry it. Then the Lion waive the reparations, the Unicorn waive the marriage, and everybody walks away covered. Bayushi Monban pointed out that this turns a military problem into a political one.

Second, Kakita Kazumi observed that they were escorting a member of the Imperial family. Miya Misato objected in the manner of a sixteen-year-old being volunteered. She is not betrothed, and she would be a defensible substitute for Shinjo Altansarnai — slightly lower in personal standing but kuge rather than bushi, which about evens it. Doji Setsuna was frank about using it as the position everyone else has to argue against, to make Miya Misato help find something better, and reassured her that a marriage can be conducted perfectly well from two different cities. Kakita Kazumi noted that the Ikoma are famous philanderers and offered his services as a physician for anything that came of it — well out of Lion hearing.

They pulled at what the parties actually want. Miya Misato observed that all of it reduces to standing. Bayushi Monban wondered whether Lord Ikoma simply wanted an end to casualties. Speaking for Ikoma Tadayoshi, who was not there to speak for himself, Kakita Kazumi put the Lion position at its hardest: the Lion will not tire of this before the Unicorn do. Which closes off every plan that waits for one side to get exhausted.

The ground itself has history. The Lion lands were cut in two eight hundred years ago when the Unicorn came back from wherever they had been. The contested country is a breadbasket — river-watered, rich, with shrines to the Fortunes of Wealth and Agriculture on it — and the Lion administered it for the whole eight centuries the Unicorn were away. They have never stopped resenting the loss of it. Asked whether the border could simply be settled without a marriage, Doji Setsuna said the Unicorn could concede it and would not, and pointed out something odd: Lord Ikoma was not the one who agreed those concessions, and the Unicorn giving up the river bank is strange on its own terms.

Then she named the party she thought was behind it. The Scorpion. They profit from the Lion and the Unicorn tearing at each other; Soshi Aisha had already let slip that Bayushi Kachiko opposed the betrothal; and a diminished Lion suits them. Bayushi Monban agreed, reluctantly, on the grounds that he has watched his own clan trade away glory and honour for the pleasure of setting an adversary back.

Kakita Kazumi asked how Akodo Akihito stood to the Lion Champion, and the answer moved everything: he is Akodo Toturi's nephew.

So Doji Setsuna built the proposal they left with. Let Shinjo Altansarnai's daughter marry Akodo Akihito. It puts Shinjo blood into the Lion succession at something close to the original tier, it answers the insult, and it does not force the Unicorn Champion into a marriage she has already refused — and by report the daughter is of an age for it and was offended on her mother's behalf anyway. A second match was floated on top: the Unicorn prisoner Akodo Akihito is holding could be married to him as well, folding a hostage release into the treaty. Ruthless, and structurally sound. Doji Setsuna observed that ending a Lion–Unicorn war by marrying pairs out of each side is exactly how it ought to be done — the rancour buried in the match rather than fought out.

The road ahead of them ran to Akodo Akihito's castle, which makes it possible. A river crossing came up audible in the distance — the Drowned Merchant River country near the contested border. Bayushi Monban worried aloud about going over another waterfall. Shiba Midori said she could carry them across.

And Doji Setsuna noted the difficulty in it. Akodo Akihito outranks her. There is no leverage here, only the pitch.

Setsuna

The whole war is an insult, and insults have a settlement procedure. Matsu Maro said it outright: not the marriage, not Lord Ikoma's feelings — honour. In Rokugan a direct insult is answered with a duel, and when the parties are clans the duel is a war. That reframes the problem completely. She has been trying to arbitrate a broken contract. She is actually being asked to find a substitute for a duel — some public act that discharges the offence — and there is more than one of those.

Bayushi Monban's observation is the best evidence anyone has produced. No garrisons, no outposts, nothing held. An army that meant to take the country would be sitting on it. This one burned and moved on, which proves the Lion do not want the land and confirms what Matsu Maro then admitted. It also means the Unicorn have a real grievance of their own — burned land and desecrated dead — and she should stop treating this as a case with one injured party.

Her ruling is sound and she should say it in exactly those words. A command to make peace overrules any objection to what broke it, because peace is the cessation of hostilities. It costs the Lion their claim without calling them wrong, and it costs the Unicorn nothing they still hold. It is also the floor: it stops the armies but settles nothing, which is why the marriage question still has to be answered.

The daughter is the right answer. It puts Shinjo blood into the Lion succession through the Champion's own nephew, discharges the insult in public, and does not require Shinjo Altansarnai to accept a thing she has already refused once. It also avoids the Miya Misato substitution, which would work and which she should be honest about not wanting to use — she talked the girl through it and volunteered her as a bargaining position in the same breath.

Nobody has asked Shinjo Altansarnai anything. Every plan on the table turns on her or on her daughter, and the party has heard her second-hand from a Lion general who admits she never understood the woman. The daughter's supposed willingness is a rumour repeated by an enemy. That is a thin footing for a treaty, and she is riding toward Battlemaiden Castle where the Champion herself is.

She has also handed herself a weapon she cannot control. The Imperial suitor is a claim nobody could easily disprove — which is precisely why using it would be a lie told with an Emperor's name on it, in a document she signs. She noticed how hard it would be to disprove before she noticed anything else about it.

And she has named the Scorpion out loud. Bayushi Kachiko opposed the betrothal, the Scorpion gain from both clans bleeding, and her own yojimbo — a Scorpion — agreed with the reading. She was right to put it aside until the fighting stops. She should be equally clear that setting it aside is not the same as deciding it does not matter, and that the man who agreed with her is bound by an edict from a Scorpion daimyō she has just billed fifty koku.

The immediate problem is smaller and harder. Akodo Akihito outranks her, she has nothing to compel him with, and the only thing she is bringing to his castle is that the proposal is a good one and it would look well in a song.
