Secrets of the City

Session 28·2025-11-18

A Unicorn shugenja broke her grandmother's jade cup as a child and something rose out of it and asked her why she had been so careless with its home. She has been arguing ever since that what the Unicorn do is a partnership.

The City Of The Rich Frog is divided and the division is the whole of its usefulness. The Dragon hold the north bank, the Lion the south, the Unicorn the west — and in the middle of the water there is an island, and on the island is the palace of Governor Miya Tetsuya, because a place where three clans meet has to have an Imperial in it. Anything forbidden on one bank is a bridge away from being permitted.

The magistrates were still at the teahouse, and still not magistrates. The question of whether to present themselves to the governor had a real answer on both sides: it is what one is supposed to do, and Bayushi Monban is kuge and could have the whole party inside the palace on the strength of it. They are also not on a mission, and nobody would ask questions if they simply passed through. What decided it was not the governor. If we present ourselves to the governor, someone else — possibly connected to the families who are doing the thing we are trying to investigate — hears about it. And beneath that, the harder problem: the Unicorn practise a form of shugenja magic that the rest of the Empire does not understand, and the Emerald Magistracy is the law that does not understand it. To a Unicorn, a magistrate asking about meishōdō is a tool of the oppressors. First part would be getting anyone like that to trust us and not think we're there to execute them and their families. So: stay travellers, work upward, and call on the governor when there is something to say.

Only one person in the room already knew they are magistrates. Shiba Midori had shown Kitsuki Wataru's son her badge, on the reasoning that she was standing there discussing his father, a senior Emerald Magistrate, and he was going to work it out anyway. Which cuts the other way too, as somebody pointed out: a person clever enough to connect those dots is clever enough to wonder whether they are Emerald Magistrates at all, rather than mahōtsukai wearing an Emerald Magistrate's shape.

Three pairs at that table and three different hunts, and Kakita Kazumi had walked into the middle of all of it. The Dragon magistrate wants the trade in illicit meishōdō artifacts stopped because the things are dangerous, and wants to know why they keep surfacing in Dragon lands in the hands of people who should not have them. The Lion pair are under orders from their daimyō to find Hana no Ame and gently persuade her to write somebody else: the next book has a Lion samurai in love with an ancestor spirit, the daimyō finds it crude and tasteless, and would like the character to be anyone at all who is not a Lion. It was noted, in passing, that the Akodo were once famously not fastidious about such things, and that the daimyō is evidently trying to demonstrate a new leaf. The Matsu carrying out the errand is of the view that she could be fighting in a war. And the third pair — the Unicorn — are looking for the author too, and nobody had spoken to them properly yet.

The party's reason for caring was arrived at out loud and it is worth writing down. It is not the book. Helping the Lion with this puts the Lion in their debt, and law enforcement owed favours this close to the Lion border can cross lines it might otherwise not. And favours help with people the Lion are holding. With a husband the Lion are holding.

Kakita Kazumi went at the Lion's hired local, who had been doing the strong silent type all evening and is a Kaeru — Kaeru Akiara. Akiara's whole manner is a wall, and Kazumi played the out-of-town young man wanting the lay of the place, which was not honest and worked anyway. What he got: watch out for the Kaeru, and if they tell somebody to go or not go somewhere, listen — most of the city is kept safe by our presence. The best teahouse in the city is the one he is sitting in. There are boat races upriver, not announced far in advance. The Unicorn race horses on the far bank and are worth watching at it. And, unlike Slowtide Harbor, there are no opium dens here.

Is there anywhere I should absolutely avoid? — The docks after dark. And then the part underneath it: the Kaeru are not policing the docks at night, and it is deliberate. They are waiting for whoever is working down there to make a mistake they can catch, and giving them more room than they normally would.

Kakita Kazumi offered to help. He was looked up and down. You? You're a silk-swaddled boy. We appreciate the offer, but we've got this in hand. He tried the weight of his own reputation — the incident at the Crane embassy a few seasons back — and got nothing at all: the capital's a long way away. Takes a while for stories to get out this far, and we generally don't care.

Doji Setsuna went in as something else entirely: a rich, incurious woman from out of town who wants to see the exotic and has money to spend on it. She played it well enough that Ide Iwena's guard came down outright, and then asked, guilelessly, where a person might acquire such a thing. The answer was the Unicorn District across the river — the docks there have seedy people near them who know what is where — and specifically a shop kept by an old man who deals in curiosities. Ask him for the relics from far away. Ide Iwena gave directions, and gave her own name so it could be used as an introduction, and mentioned that she has not been to see him herself yet, so I hope you make a good impression.

Then the caution, which was not about the law. There are rules against messing with such items. The locals, especially on that side of the river, take a dim view of people buying and selling and trading items of such spiritual importance. They are simply not supposed to be on any market. Doji Setsuna, still playing: well, if I am not to buy it, how can I give it to my husband? — Whether one is supposed to do something has little to do with whether one can. She looked scandalised, correctly. She gestured at Bayushi Monban: he makes sure that I'm safe. They looked at him, and then at her. Now I understand a great deal more about why you are so bold about these things.

And Ide Iwena mentioned, in passing, that Hana no Ame had gone upriver to the City of Courtliness Behind Violence — Toshi Ranbo — having had no luck finding whatever she was researching here.

Shiba Midori spent the evening on the other Unicorn. Iuchi Minoru's manner is older, severe, and continuously running her fingers over the rim of her teacup, somewhere between a nervous habit and reverence. She had been watching Doji Setsuna's performance with visible disbelief that anyone could be that vacant.

Shiba Midori nearly made a bad mistake — she considered telling Iuchi Minoru an invented story about a missing Phoenix shugenja, and was reminded in time that inventing stories in front of someone who may be able to hear a lie is a way of handing them the weapon. She went at it honestly instead, and it landed. Oh, a great grasp of the details of spiritual matters. Are you also possessed of the gift of the shugenja? — Now that would be telling, wouldn't it. Please consider me a gifted amateur. And then: you are not one of us, but you talk like one.

Spirits are partners, not our servants. Would you not agree? — I have found few spirits that are quite servant in their attitude. — Yet so many of the gifted in Rokugan assume the spirits are theirs to command rather than partners to be worked with. That can be quite a dangerous tack to take. — Indeed. Particularly with spirits one does not know well, for they lack the restraint people might show.

Shiba Midori was thinking, while she said it, about a charge of ancestral spirits across the Snow Plain, and about how little idea this woman has of how right she is.

Bayushi Monban came at Iuchi Minoru directly and it was the one approach in the room that failed. He chose courtesy over command, which was correct — she has a streak of grandeur in her and does not care to be spoken down to — and still got nowhere. I am not sure I understand your meaning. He had spent both his opportunities on not giving offence, which is the only reason it was not worse.

What he got instead was Iuchi Minoru's warning, and it is the most valuable thing said that evening. She looked back at Shiba Midori and said that certainly nobody would traffic in sacred relics that contain — that serve as the home and the vessel — and here she stroked the side of her teacup — for a being of great power. Then she poured steaming tea into it. After all, such a bowl would be such a vessel. Extremely dangerous.

The tea set is a century or two old and has been in service that long. She was not speaking carelessly, and she was not speaking about hypotheticals.

Then Kakita Kazumi bought himself a disguise for a handful of zeni. He had a servant cut his sake half with water — it would ruin the flavour, the servant said, and pocketed the coin, and did it, and did not let the others see — and proceeded to get visibly and loudly drunk on it. Sober, he pushed Kaeru Akiara again: come on, you can tell me. I know how to take care of myself. I can handle anything that happens at the docks. The performance was aimed at a specific conclusion — that this young fool would either be told or would go down there anyway and cause trouble — and it reached it.

Very well, pretty boy. You're welcome to get yourself fucked up. And then, crassly and cheerfully: the Kaeru are leaving the docks alone at night deliberately, to catch them out doing something stupid. Maybe you'd be willing to be the bait. The sting.

I have been known to do many stupid things. I assure you, I am doing stupid things.

He also took Akiara's measure of the Lion woman while he was at it: green, young, and of lower status than himself. Everything in her posture says so.

Doji Setsuna went back to Ide Iwena and changed the subject to the war, still playing the enthusiast — isn't this exciting — and got the official line delivered flat. The Lion chase our soldiers across the field, thinking to pin us down and force us to fight them their way. They are fools, and the endless plains of our lands swallow them whole. They may chase all they like; they will only die tired.

And under it, nothing. The longer she talked the clearer it became that the war is distant in this woman's mind and the answer was a thing she had ready. What is actually driving Ide Iwena is concern for a friend she believes is in danger — not her companion, someone else — and her search for Hana no Ame is not an errand. It is personal, and it is loyalty.

Shiba Midori arrived at the same place from the other side, and further: this woman knows the author. Which puts the Toshi Ranbo story in a different light. Bayushi Monban might have believed it. Shiba Midori did not, and knows a liar on sight, and reads the endangered friend and the missing author as the same person.

Then she asked Iuchi Minoru for her own story, gesturing at Ide Iwena's cup — what she was mentioning earlier, that is familiar to you too, isn't it.

My grandmother had a set much like this. Green-coloured jade, or the best jade anyway, and the fired work of a ceramic master. One day I was playing with it and I slipped and dropped one of her teacups. No matter how well made a cup is, it is only clay. It shattered, and when it did, I saw something rise up out of it. It frightened me. I could see it moving — a person, almost. It chastised me. It said: why were you so careless with my home? And I did not understand then, as I do now, that everything around us has a spirit. It is only a matter of whether the spirit in question is awake and aware of itself.

Shiba Midori tested it while she listened, and the story holds. The point it hangs on is whether she genuinely saw the spirit, and if she did she is a spirit speaker — which she has now all but said aloud without ever saying it. And the second conclusion follows from the first: a Unicorn-trained shugenja knows how these talismans are made. If she cannot make them herself, she has certainly handled them.

The quarrel she is known for came out in her own words. An Isawa once put it to her that her magic requires caging that which should never be bound. I told him that the spirits who enter the bindings I have made for them are my allies and my friends — rather more so than the servants he orders around. It came within reach of a magical duel between shugenja, and was stopped only because his superior told him to stand down.

Bayushi Monban tried once more, with the Crab as his excuse and academic curiosity as his cover. I suppose the Scorpion are prepared to reveal the secrets of their many wards? — which Kakita Kazumi answered from across the table by shouting that if Bayushi Monban said one word about those, his mother would cut his tantō off.

Outsiders — few of them have the perspective you are espousing. Fewer still have the interest, much less the enthusiasm. He asked whether they might speak privately sometime about the philosophy of the thing, and how a polite disagreement comes within reach of a duel. I can humour you in at least a general sense, but we have a purpose here that overrides casual conversation. And: it is very interesting, Scorpion, that you have an interest in pursuing the same. Not very common of your kind.

My clan's reputation is a little more practical, but I have had the pleasure of learning at the feet of many masters, and my eyes are well accustomed to seeing different points of view and not passing judgment. Atop the Wall you see many different aspects of evil. With the Crab you also find family, and duty, and inclusion, and I wish the same for the rest of the Empire.

Then I wish you more of that camaraderie as you return to the Empire's most dangerous border. May the Kami go with you. He asked where he might call on her, and was told.

The service was taken up, and the highest-status guests are the first expected to leave. Ikoma Kotone to Shiba Midori: I look forward to serving you again, my lady Shiba.

Outside the door Kakita Kazumi straightened his clothes, adjusted his belt, cleared his throat, and stopped being drunk. People have been going missing near the docks. The local security has been letting it happen, in order to catch whoever is doing it. He intends to go down there alone tonight, because those looking to kidnap someone tend to look for less rather than more — and the docks in question are in the Unicorn District, which is also where the curiosity shop is.

Doji Setsuna would not normally vote for anything this reckless. She voted for it on timing: they have not presented themselves to Governor Miya Tetsuya yet, and the plan was to do that in the morning. If this is going to be done at all, tonight is the only night they are still nobody.

Setsuna

Going in without the badge was the right call and the reason is sharper than she put it. It is not that a magistrate frightens criminals. It is that to a Unicorn shugenja the Emerald Magistracy is the institution that does not understand what they do and has the authority to act on that misunderstanding. Every question she asks with the badge on is a question asked by someone who might execute the answer. She got more out of Ide Iwena in one conversation as a rich fool than the office would have bought in a week, and Shiba Midori got further still by admitting ignorance and asking honestly.

The most important thing she learned tonight is that Ide Iwena is not hunting the author. She is protecting a friend she thinks is in danger, and the war she is nominally here about barely registers. That reframes everything. The Toshi Ranbo story is not a lie planted by someone watching the party — it is a woman sending strangers away from a person she is frightened for, within minutes of being asked. Which means Ide Iwena knows where Hana no Ame is, and thinks somebody means her harm, and that somebody is not the Lion daimyō, who only wants a character rewritten.

So the question is no longer where the author is. It is what she found out while researching how meishōdō talismans work.

Iuchi Minoru said the thing and nobody quite heard it. A sacred relic is the home and the vessel of a being of great power. She said it stroking a teacup from a set that is two hundred years old and still in daily service, and then she poured tea into it, and called it extremely dangerous. That was not a lecture about the black market. That was a woman telling a room full of strangers what is in the cup they are drinking from. And it is precisely the thing Matsu Morozane cut off a Unicorn shugenja's neck at the Snow Plain and handed to a Kitsu to question. That thread has now come at her twice.

The Crane should be careful about which side of that argument she ends up on. Iuchi Minoru calls the bound spirits her allies and friends; an Isawa called it caging and nearly fought her over it; and Shiba Midori, who has better reason than most to hate the practice, listened and did not argue. The honest position is that neither of them knows, and that the Empire has been legislating it for centuries without asking.

The plan tonight is bad and she voted for it for a good reason. Kakita Kazumi alone on a dock after dark, deliberately unpoliced, where people have been disappearing — that is not a sting, that is the shape of the thing they are investigating. What makes it defensible is the clock: tomorrow they call on Governor Miya Tetsuya and stop being nobody, and after that this is impossible. If it is going to happen it happens tonight.

She should still note who authored it. Kakita Kazumi engineered the offer — watered his own sake, played the drunk, and pushed until the Kaeru suggested exactly the thing he wanted suggested. So the bait is his own idea wearing somebody else's mouth, which is better than being recruited into it, and worse in a different way: nobody is looking after him tonight because he arranged for nobody to be.

One line to keep. The Kaeru are not bothering with the docks after dark on purpose, so we can catch them out doing something stupid. A security force that lets people vanish in order to make a case later is either patient or complicit, and there is no way to tell which from outside. Kaeru Akiara serves Governor Miya Tetsuya, not any clan. That is worth remembering before she declares herself in his palace in the morning.
