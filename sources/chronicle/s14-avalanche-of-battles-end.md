## Narrative

!lede [[Akodo Kagekatsu]]'s flank went in and took the [[Central Hill]]. His unit put [[Utaku Mangiano]] on the ground the moment he left himself open, and the panic of it ran back through the Unicorn line. The [[Lion Clan]] fortified what they had taken and settled into attrition — hold the hill, bleed them, wait.

[[Akodo Toronoko]] made that work. Her archers laid a curtain that pinned the Unicorn where they stood, and when their spearmen came on she split her volleys by angle — the back rank shooting high over the front — and stopped the advance in the open snow, where neither army had anywhere to be.

Atop the hill [[Kitsu Somalia]] gathered her cohorts, pointed them at the Unicorn commander, and called Fire From Within. The cohort went up. Their commander spent his turn rallying men who were on fire.

[[Matsu Morozane]] put out a Rallying Cry with [[Shiguro Chinmoku]] at his back. A Unicorn officer came at him and he played stupid until the officer was close enough, and then let the lion go — and the horse bolted and took its rider out of the fight and a good deal of order with it.

Then [[Kitsu Yui]] settled the day's second leader. She is [[Kitsu Somalia]]'s bodyguard, and when a duel was thrown at her commander she took it herself and beat [[Utaku Mangiano]] with it. Two of the three Unicorn leaders were down. [[Kitsu Somalia]] looked at that and decided to keep grinding rather than gamble.

The Unicorn had one more thing. Their shugenja put a working over the field that drove the Lion into mass panic, and then called [[Matsu Morozane]] out — *come out and fight, coward* — and burned him down. He went into the snow with the fire still on him, in front of his own troops, which is the worst place for a commander to fall and the reason a shugenja picks him. [[Kitsu Somalia]] came off the hill at a run, put Extinguish through him, killed the flames and held him together. He lived. He was not going to be standing up again that day.

Then the hill came down. An avalanche off the [[Central Hill]] split the battlefield in half and buried the flank the Unicorn were standing on, and that was the end of the engagement — not a decision, a burial.

When it was quiet, all three Unicorn leaders were dead, their army was broken, the Lion had taken losses and still held the [[Central Hill]], and [[Matsu Morozane]] was alive by a margin. A tactical victory, hard bought.

## Learned

- Kitsu Yui: Kitsu Somalia's bodyguard. When a duel was aimed at her commander she answered it in her place and killed Utaku Mangiano with it. This is the Kitsu whose intervention Kitsu Taigen's line still owes a karmic debt for — and Setsuna has now seen her fight.
- Utaku Mangiano: A Unicorn leader. Knocked down by Kagekatsu's flank when he opened himself up, and finished by Kitsu Yui in a duel meant for Somalia.
- Akodo Kagekatsu: Took the Central Hill with a flank attack. Whatever he is in a tent, he delivered in the field.
- Akodo Toronoko: Pinned the Unicorn with a curtain of arrows and broke a spearman advance by splitting her volleys high and low.
- Kitsu Somalia: Called Fire From Within onto the Unicorn commander's cohort from the hill, then ran down off it to Extinguish the flames on Morozane and hold him alive.
- Matsu Morozane: Rallied his troops, drew a mounted officer close by playing ignorant and loosed the lion at his horse, then was called out by the Unicorn shugenja and burned down in front of his own men.
- Shiguro Chinmoku: Covered Morozane's back, then broke an officer's horse and scattered the order around it.

## Setsuna

The Lion held the field. All three Unicorn commanders dead, the Unicorn army broken, the [[Central Hill]] in [[Lion Clan|Lion]] hands at the end of it.

[[Ikoma Ichigo]] told her, in the [[Ikoma Hall of Scribes|Hall of Scribes]], that the [[Snow Plain]] was a Unicorn victory and a Lion defeat.

Those cannot both be the whole truth, and the memory is not finished — there are days of this left, and an army that holds a hill on one evening can lose everything by the next. The historian's summary is no longer the settled fact of the battle. Somewhere between this hill and the ending the Lion lost what they were standing on, and **how they lost it is the thing the record has been quiet about twice now.**

The ending itself is worth as much. The engagement did not conclude, it was buried. An avalanche came off the [[Central Hill]] and cut the field in two. That matters for a property dispute in a way it did not matter to anyone fighting there — snow moves ground, and ground that moved three hundred years ago is not where the old survey says it is. Whatever boundary the [[Old Diamond Mines|mines]] were described by before that winter, the hill it was drawn on came down.
