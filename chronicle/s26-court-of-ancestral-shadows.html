Court of Ancestral Shadows

Session 26·2025-10-20

Setsuna asked to be excused from the séance. Kaage refused her. Then Kitsu Takeko opened the void and two of the dead walked into the arbitration.

Kitsuki Kaage put the obvious question to the room: how does anyone testify to a battle three centuries gone? The answer he got was ancestral vision, and he did not like it. When Kitsuko Ayoko called up her own ancestor — who carried the guilt of a victory won unfairly, and said that the guilt was why the histories had been allowed to rot — and described a scroll her ancestor gave her that put the same vision into the whole assembly, Kitsuki Kaage called it fanciful lore.

The witnesses held. They had all seen the same battle from different places in it, and they named Shinjo Altansarnai and Matsu Sakura as the fixed points they could all check each other against.

Doji Setsuna gave the court Matsu Morozane's account: poisonings, weather, the unexpected — and the Lion winning anyway, against the odds, right up until the Empire stopped the battle. Ide Subane pressed her on whether the Lion advantage was decisive, and she would not overstate it. The Lion had an edge but not a decisive one. Ide Subane took that and ran the other way with it: an edge means the Lion could have won without any Scorpion help at all, so dismiss the claim. Shosuro Aishi took the same testimony and ran the opposite way — Matsu Morozane's own account puts a Scorpion at the first clash and at the treaty signing both, which is presence, and presence is what the claim needs.

Kakita Kazumi argued that the Lion and the Unicorn were matched in valour, that the Scorpion tried to tilt it and failed, and that what actually ended the battle was Imperial authority. Shiba Midori told the court about the shugenja general and the infiltrator who tried to kill her — sabotage that hurt the Lion, and pointed at Scorpion collusion with the Unicorn. Shosuro Aishi answered that the infiltrator wore no clan colours. Ide Subane answered that the Unicorn are entitled to their own counterintelligence.

Bayushi Monban laid out the irregularities in order. Point one: the pincer manoeuvre failed, almost as if the Unicorn knew it was happening. A third of the Lion host poisoned, which bought the Imperial Houses the time they needed. And the histories erased by Ikoma Akuyaku, a scout bound to the Unicorn.

Shiba Midori cut across all of it with the argument nobody wanted. Whatever the Scorpion did or did not do, the court should not be in the business of paying out for dishonourable methods, because of what that teaches everyone watching. The room went politely quiet.

Then Kitsuko Ayoko was called back, shaken, and gave the session its verdict before the magistrate could. Her ancestor had made a mistake, out of fear — the fear of having very nearly won the battle. And Kitsu Yui was the true hero of the Snow Plain, and neither Unicorn valour nor Scorpion treachery could put her honour in shadow.

Bayushi Monban closed for the Scorpion: that they have kept the Empire's histories for centuries, that an agreement obliges regardless of whether it was honourable, that the Lion disobeyed during the battle, and that the timing of events made the Unicorn victors — and so the mines are theirs to give.

With the floor open, Bayushi Monban proposed calling the ancestors themselves. Doji Setsuna asked to be excused from the séance. Kitsuki Kaage refused her. Ide Subane warned that ghosts can be worked on like anyone else; the Scorpion agreed to it under conditions.

Kitsu Takeko came forward. I am going to do my very best to see that these ancestors attend this matter. She performed the rite with Shiba Midori supporting her, opened the void, and two of the dead came into the room: Shosuro Aishi and Ikoma Akuyaku.

Setsuna

She asked to be excused and was refused, and that refusal is the most important thing that happened to her today. She is about to be in a room where the dead are compelled to speak, carrying Matsu Morozane's memories in her own head, with a grandmother — Matsu Tomoe — who has already told her she is walking with her and watching. Whatever Doji Setsuna wanted to avoid by leaving, she is now going to be present for.

Her testimony was honest and it cost her the argument. Asked whether the Lion advantage was decisive she said an edge, not a decisive one — which is true, and which Ide Subane immediately used to argue the Scorpion contributed nothing that mattered. Note that this is the second time this week that telling the truth precisely has strengthened the Unicorn case. That is not a reason to stop. It is a reason to make the Imperial argument the one she is actually running, since it is the only one where accuracy helps her.

And her own account was used by the other side. Shosuro Aishi took Matsu Morozane's testimony — Setsuna's ancestor, Setsuna's mouth — and made it the Scorpion's proof of presence at both the clash and the signing. Anything she says about that battle from now on is evidence for whoever finds it useful. She should assume it and prepare for it rather than resent it.

Shiba Midori made the argument that wins. Not who was there — what the Empire teaches by paying out. A court that rewards poisoned wells and forged ledgers instructs every clan watching. The room had no answer, and a room with no answer is where a magistrate rules. That is the ground the Imperial-custody finding should be argued from, and Kitsuko Ayoko's testimony now sits under it: the honour that deserves recognition at the Snow Plain is Kitsu Yui's, and she belongs to nobody's claim.
