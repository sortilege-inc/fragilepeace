Historical Intrigue and Dispute

Session 18·2025-08-26

The one historian who survived the Snow Plain and meant to write it down properly was assassinated before he finished. Everything anyone knows about that battle is what was left after somebody made sure of him.

Three days until Kitsuki Kaage arrives at the Castle of the Swift Sword with the Scorpion and Unicorn ambassadors behind him, to adjudicate the Scorpion's claim to the Snow Plain diamond mines — a claim the Unicorn deny outright. Three days, and an archive.

The first thing the archive gave them was a murder. Ikoma Hideri came off the Snow Plain alive and set out to write the battle down properly, and was assassinated before he finished it. Everything the Empire knows about that field is what survived him, which is to say it is what somebody was content to leave.

Doji Setsuna went looking for Matsu Morozane and found his records thin. What is in them is not the fighting. It is weather, and supply, and the ways the army failed to feed itself — a general who came out of that winter writing about logistics rather than valour. That pointed the whole party at the quartermasters' books.

Kakita Kazumi laid their ancestors' accounts side by side and the discrepancies stood up on their own. Akodo Toronoko's narrative boasts, and downplays the supply failures, and does not mention Ikoma Hideri at all — and her writing changes markedly after this battle, which is the sort of thing a reader notices and a clerk does not. Matsu Sakura's own record admits the Lion failures honestly enough, and still leaves out both Ikoma Hideri and the medic suspected of spying. Kakita Kazumi went hunting that medic through the archive century by century and found nothing at all. No record. Not a line.

Kitsu Somalia is missing too — an ancestor remembered for calling the Lion dead onto a battlefield, absent from the accounts of the battle she called them onto.

And Kitsu Yui's record ends in verse: I gave my name and I lost my honor in the snow… Come spring, the world changes again.

Somewhere in the Kitsu Archives Bayushi Monban saw what all this was doing to Doji Setsuna and walked her outside into the summer night until she could breathe.

At dawn they ate together in a garden, and Shiba Midori took the chance to sound out Kitsuko Ayoko on whether she could be taught to summon ancestors. Doji Setsuna, tired and off her guard, said something that landed as an insult to the girl's standing, and it was heard as one.

They compared absences and found one more. Shosuro Amane is in nobody's personal record, though she was present at the pivotal moments of the battle. The party's theory: Lion authors cut the Scorpion out to avoid recording that Scorpion interference had touched a Lion defeat.

So they set their course. Quartermasters' books, to prove the supply failures were sabotage. Bayushi Monban drank with the servants, which is his way of working, and then went privately to a Scorpion historian to see whether the proceedings could be slowed while the missing histories were run down. And they agreed a position to hold in front of Kitsuki Kaage: that the Scorpion claim rests on a copy of a treaty and nothing else, and should fail unless something better is put on the table.

Setsuna

She has now read the archive of a battle she has been inside, and the two do not match in a way she can list.

The medic first. Kitsu Somalia came round on that field and said the person who tried to kill him was a woman whose voice he knew, because she had patched him up before. The medic in that camp was Akodo Kayamayako, who rewarmed Ikoma Akuyaku's frostbitten feet with gentle fire and dressed Matsu Morozane's burns. And Kakita Kazumi has searched the whole archive for that medic and found nothing — no record, in any century. A person can be left out of a battle account. Being left out of the archive entirely takes somebody doing it.

Then the pattern of what is missing. Ikoma Hideri, killed before he could write. Kitsu Somalia, who called the dead onto the field, absent. Shosuro Amane, present at every hinge, absent. The medic, erased. What survives is Akodo Toronoko boasting, and Matsu Sakura admitting failures that make the Lion look unlucky rather than betrayed. These are not four separate accidents.

And her own ancestor kept the right books. Matsu Morozane wrote about weather and supply and how an army starves. He was there for the duels and the banner and he wrote about the carts. That reads like a man who worked out what actually beat them, which is exactly where the party is now digging.

The quartermasters' books are the correct target, and the reason to give Kitsuki Kaage is not honour but arithmetic: stores are counted. A poisoning leaves a hole in a ledger that no historian's silence can close, and a ledger is the one kind of evidence in this castle that nobody thought to edit.

One caution about herself. She insulted Kitsuko Ayoko's standing in a garden at dawn because she was tired and full of somebody else's memories. Ayoko is not samurai-born, Shiba Midori is trying to open a door for her, and the girl is dreaming the same battle. That was a bad moment to be careless in.
