## Narrative

!lede [[Daidoji Shin]] intercepted them leaving the teahouse. *You are new to the city, I take it. We do not often see great clan samurai in such interesting mixed company.* [[Doji Setsuna]] placed him instantly and has probably served him tea: he is one of Daidoji Uji's agents, he works rogue shugenja, and he has a particular reputation for arresting shugenja who are doing nothing like maho but are nonetheless a danger to the peace. He has sworn to find whichever shugenja stirred the spirits that raised the tsunami and the misery in the [[Crane Lands|Crane lands]], and he suspects a [[Mantis Clan|Mantis]].

He is also, by the estimate of every Emerald Magistrate present, a rank amateur with a neon sign over his head. Officially he is the [[Crane Clan|Crane]]'s liaison out here; unofficially this is an excellent posting for getting an obnoxious man out of the way, and he appears to enjoy the freedom it gives him. He is very good at one thing: pinning a person down socially, because he can talk a man to death.

*Doji Setsuna, of course. Are you going to bury me in paperwork again?* — *That remains to be seen. Right now I am just enjoying the sights and doing a little shopping.* He congratulated her on the Emerald appointment and assured her he would be joining her shortly, *especially once I crack the matter of the illicit magical items being distributed throughout the city.* On method he was contemptuous of the other approach: *Kitsuki spend too much time digging in the dirt and looking for footprints when you can simply talk to the right people.* And on the party: *you are not the right people, but I appreciate that you might know who, or might be able to find them on your own.* He ignored [[Kakita Kazumi]] entirely, which was deliberate — they share a lord, and Shin has no interest in provoking someone who could actually beat him.

What he gave them was real. Avoid the [[Dragon District]] — too many Kitsuki already sniffing along that trail. Nobody sees [[Governor Miya Tetsuya]] in the morning who is not already on the books, and was not two weeks ago. And on the trade itself: *obviously the trade in [[Meishodo Talismans|meishōdō talismans]] is in the [[Unicorn District]], but it is not that simple. The suppliers are there. I suspect the dealers are elsewhere.*

[[Kakita Kazumi]] introduced him with the observation that the only thing Shin is good at cracking is a bottle of sake. *That is rich, coming from the most mediocre samurai who ever lived.*

Then he worked the two women and won. [[Doji Setsuna]] outranks him, and used it to demand a full written survey of the city's commercial situation — which is to say, an all-nighter on something she has no intention of reading, because what she actually wants is him busy and elsewhere while they work. He spent the conversation baffling her with reasons why it could not be ready until late tomorrow, which was fine; she had set the frame and anything inside it was an acceptable loss. He came away having noticed her Imperial bloodline, which he would dearly love to possess, and left her dazed and compromised.

On [[Shiba Midori]] he turned the full smile, and was told [[Ikoma Kotone]]'s teahouse had already served her. *The only reason you would not take tea there is to take it at my house, of course.* [[Kakita Kazumi]] intervened — *keep your blade stowed, Shin, she is already promised* — and then, when that did not land, added that her mentor would hardly approve of him chasing one of the Elemental Masters' students. Half of [[Otosan Uchi]], he noted, knows what happened the last time she and a certain ronin master met; it is rumoured the two of them caused an earthquake there. He was careful not to specify whether he meant that or the last time she met her betrothed.

[[Doji Setsuna]] broke it herself, deliberately, with an outburst she chose: *have you not read the novels of [[Hana no Ame]]? Sometimes what a fine lady needs are the rough hands of a sailor.* It cleared everything she was carrying, and it cost her. *I did not know you read such scandalous things. It so totally suits you — you are so respectable on the outside.* And then, without quite saying it: *such a shame they had to turn you into a peace cow with the [[Unicorn Clan|Unicorn]], of all people.*

Wordplay is her passion and it is his, which is why she heard it instantly and did not miss a syllable of it. She gave him nothing back, and the nothing was a full answer. He worked her a second time, reached for the plot of the ghost-romance novel, and watched her face when he said the word ghost — and the recognition went the wrong way for him. *Oh. You were not playing.*

[[Bayushi Monban]] stepped in front of her. *We have business in the Unicorn District. Shall we be moving along? All this squawking is giving me a headache.* — *I apologise that the sound of civilised conversation does not please your ears, my lord. Perhaps you should go back to the Wall, where the [[Crab Clan|Crab]] can appreciate your level of sophistication.* [[Bayushi Monban]] squatted down and looked into his face: *perhaps you should return to the teahouse. This might get a little too rough for your feathers.*

He laughed, wished them a pleasant stay, and offered his home and his teahouse if they ever needed to work out which way was up. Behind his back, his bodyguard Mori Kasami mouthed *I am sorry. I do not know what possessed him.*

They caught the last ferry of the night across to the [[Unicorn District]] at dusk, with the shops closing. The [[Curio Shop]] sits off the wharf's main thoroughfare, windows under thick dark drapes with a sliver left uncovered, a curtain hanging in the doorway, the inside crammed to the ceiling. [[The Shopkeeper]] has a long white beard and the manner of every curio dealer in every samurai drama. *Come in, come in. It is late and I am closing soon, but I can certainly help you find whatever you are looking for. And to whom do my old eyes have the privilege of beholding such radiant finery?*

They had also picked up a tail — moving like a samurai, wearing two swords, in no clan colours, following badly and not from the teahouse. [[Kakita Kazumi]] went into an alley and up onto a roof.

The stock is mostly upper-peasant to lower-samurai curiosities, gaijin material and Rokugani side by side, with a few pieces plainly too expensive for the room. One of them is a very good old tea set — a style fashionable a few generations ago, with that subtle sophistication, and of much the same age as the one at [[Ikoma Kotone]]'s teahouse, by a different maker. Nothing in the shop pinged [[Bayushi Monban]]'s sense for the first enemy, which it would have.

[[The Shopkeeper]] offered the ladies a silver chain with a locket of unmistakably foreign design, for looking their best at their next court appearance. Nothing supernatural about it.

[[Doji Setsuna]] asked for something else. She described [[Shinjo Harunobu]] at length — a boy raised with siblings and horses out west, married not long ago, now on the front lines — without once naming his family, and let the shape of it say [[Shinjo Family|Shinjo]] for her. She wanted something to give him the inner peace that warfare so often demands. *I hear he told me of things he saw growing up — specific artifacts one could engage with spiritually, in a way that is quite uncommon.*

His eyes narrowed. *Many objects have a resonance. They are not like what you are talking about. You are wanting something spiritually active.* — *Yes. Something that would bring him back to those days when he was coming of age on the great plains.*

Something pinged, and his entire demeanour changed, and then he went into salesman mode and stayed there: saddles, saddle charms, ornaments for stirrups and for a horse's mane. Plains curios, all of them perfectly legal, none of them described as spiritually anything. He was selling her mementos and deniability at the same time.

[[Shiba Midori]] passed [[Doji Setsuna]] a silent verdict on the room — half the stock probably should not be in this shop, and the other half is questionable — and then found the way through. Not the proper word. A child's word for the things, half-mistranslated across dialects: *small friends*.

[[Doji Setsuna]] read him properly and what she found was not a criminal. It was a cornered man: *I have a shop full of magistrates and I want none of this.* He suspects what passes through his hands, and has been careful never to confirm it, because confirming it would put him in far deeper. So she apologised for the confusion, said plainly that she was not here representing her office, and offered him a writ indemnifying him with the local magistrate for anything already done in error.

*I am sure I do not know what you are talking about. But if I did, I would remember that information is valuable — and the sort of information I might have, if I knew what you were talking about, would be worth rather more than simple indemnification for things I am sure I have no knowledge of whatsoever.*

So [[Shiba Midori]] stopped being harmless. She performed the outraged noblewoman — a knave, a ruffian, a man who should be ashamed of himself — accused him of setting the tail on them, and unmasked into it, sobbing and beating him with her fan. She chose to enrage rather than daze him on the reasoning that she carries a blessed lineage and he would know better than to act on it, and that [[Bayushi Monban]] was one step from the door.

He broke and blurted the thing out: *the Kaeru. The police. The one that followed — the one I certainly did not call for.* The tail is a [[Kaeru Ronin|Kaeru]] officer, and he was following the Emerald Magistrates, which is the local police attempting to shadow Imperial law in its own city.

*Look, I had nothing to do with the missing persons involved in this. And I assure you, I know nothing about the personages who may or may not have bought certain items in this shop — for whom I have the receipts.*

[[Doji Setsuna]] put it to him in one sentence. *I am fairly sure, as you mention, that the police are indeed coming. Do you want those receipts in your hands when they arrive, or would you prefer they were in ours?* He produced the paperwork. *It would not do for the local authorities to find this now, would it.*

The sale carries [[Hana no Ame]]'s own chop — the one she uses in that identity. The item was a talisman said to hold a wild spirit out of the [[Burning Sands]]; or that was what she was after, *some sort of meishōdō artifact, which I do not deal in* — and [[Doji Setsuna]] read that last part as half a lie. He is the middleman. He bought from a [[Fox Clan Samurai]] whose name he does not remember but can describe, who claimed to be from across the [[Burning Sands]] with magical goods. *Not quite meishōdō. I did not believe him. My customers certainly did, and that is all that matters.*

She kept him solvent on the way out: still interested in the original request, and willing to pay a finder's fee for an introduction so that the two parties could deal directly and he need not be in the middle of it. He countered that he might know where the person in question is staying, for a consideration — and sold her an ornate saddle tassel at an outrageous price with the address folded into the box. She charged it to the Empire as an investigation expense. [[Shiba Midori]]'s assessment: they have just acquired a perfectly good informant on local illegal activity, of which he has only tangential knowledge, no doubt.

And the last thing he gave them was the thing that matters. Some of those papers are the handling instructions for the talisman, which the buyer never paid for and left behind. He assumes she will be back for them once she works out she can do nothing without them. He has not seen her since. He will certainly let them know if either the seller or the buyer comes in — *to do otherwise would be a very costly and almost certainly fatal mistake.*

[[Bayushi Monban]] asked him, on the way out, whether he had lied. *Not to your knowledge.* And then: *I have always been scrupulously honest with good customers.*

Outside, [[Bayushi Monban]] crossed the street in one movement and arrived a sword's length from the officer, who dropped straight into the stance for a rising draw. *You are not from around here, and you are poking your nose into matters that do not concern you. It is after dark on [[The Docks|these docks]], and a decent samurai should be thinking about where he will take his rest, and leave the keeping of the city to those who know it better.* — *Are you telling me my business, officer?* He shifted, uncomfortable, and tried again: he is only saying that for his own safety a man should not involve himself with matters and people who might do him harm. *You carry yourself like a great clan samurai who runs every room he walks into. But you do not live on these streets and you do not know these people, and if you bumble into business that does not concern you, you may disrupt delicate plans already in place.*

[[Bayushi Monban]] showed his badge. *All business within the Empire is my business.* The man's eyes went wide with the arithmetic of his own pay grade. [[Kakita Kazumi]] came off the roof behind him in a tumble and tapped him on the shoulder; the sword came half out again, and went back when he saw the second great clan samurai. *Monban, we did rather want to keep ourselves on the down-low. Could you please put that away.*

Then they had him between them. *You will keep this quiet, won't you, officer.* — *I would so hate to have to find an appropriate ditch. Or some nice weights.* He began to say he would inform his superior of their presence and caught himself halfway: *wait — you do not want me to do that.* [[Bayushi Monban]]: *we are very aware of protocol. We were planning to make our presence known as soon as the opportune moment arrived. Do not worry. We are not intending to stir up trouble — merely to silence it.* He bowed toward [[Kakita Kazumi]], said he was sure he had simply lost their trail, and left in the only direction available to him.

[[The Shopkeeper]] was throwing the blanket over his door when he turned and found a [[Scorpion Clan|Scorpion]] standing behind him, and was frightened in a way he had not been all evening. *I do not need to die. I will keep my mouth shut.* — *You answered my companion's questions truthfully. You have nothing to fear.* And then the useful part: he might want to make friends with certain strangers, because whatever he is doing here is evidently known. Be careful. Call on the Scorpion if help is needed.

[[Doji Setsuna]] came back to end that conversation — the man has agreed to help them, it would be a shame to hurt him, though she understands the drive to ensure his motivation. [[Bayushi Monban]] had no intention of hurting him. *He was honest. He is the middleman. He is not the person we want.*

They found dumplings and read the papers properly.

The instructions are hard going — faded, complicated, and written in something that is barely Rokugani. What they describe is a wholly different art from anything [[Shiba Midori]] has been trained in. And the operative fact is this: a talisman that has failed to contain its spirit cannot be used again. The spells are for *resealing* — one must subdue the spirit and bind it into a fresh vessel, assuming one can get it out of whatever it currently occupies. If [[Hana no Ame]]'s talisman has been used, the spirit may already be loose, and the papers do not say what becomes of it. Only that another bottle will be needed.

The saddle tassel is just pretty. The earrings are just earrings.

[[Shiba Midori]] said what she thought of the method and did not soften it. *This is not working with a spirit. This is beating the spirit into submission, shoving it into a box, and making it do what you want when you want.* The [[Phoenix Clan|Phoenix]] rites are cooperative and the spirit is willing; the spirit's consent is nowhere mentioned in this document at all. Pressed honestly on whether she actually knows it is against the spirit's will — there might be a rite that calls a spirit which understands what it is agreeing to — she conceded the point: *I think we would need a [[Unicorn Clan|Unicorn]] meishōdō practitioner to trust us, and to like us a great deal more than they do now, for me to get that kind of answer.* Which is also why she knows there is a quarrel between the Elemental Council and the Unicorn over how much of their art to tolerate, and that the Elemental Master of Earth — the man who replaced the one she duelled — has been travelling and negotiating with them.

Her plan to ask the local kami what had been let loose in this city ran into something worse than politics. If people here are doing this, and the spirits object, then whoever is doing it will have made certain there are no free spirits left nearby to carry word of it. There may not be a single ordinary kami at the centre of the city. Which is a problem for every spell she has.

The receipts finished the picture. The buyer's is deliberately empty — no address, no details, nothing but the artist's chop, as though the discretion had been paid for. The seller's carries a seal, a chop and an address, with a note to the effect that if the shop has room for more of the same, he may be found here.

Tomorrow is [[Tongbo Temple]] and [[Governor Miya Tetsuya]]. Tonight they are going to [[The Docks|the docks]] anyway, having been warned off them by three separate people.

## Setsuna

**The author is a buyer, and she is a buyer who cannot work the thing she bought.** [[Hana no Ame]]'s chop is on a receipt for a talisman holding a wild spirit out of the [[Burning Sands]] — and she walked out without the handling instructions, unpaid for, still sitting in a shop. She can open it. She cannot close it. She cannot move what is inside into anything else. And the only copy of the papers that would tell her how is now in [[Doji Setsuna]]'s hands.

That is not a clue. That is a deadline. Either she has already used it and something is loose in this city, or she has not and she will come back for the papers — and the shopkeeper has agreed to send word when she does. Both branches lead to the same place, and the second one is the one to prepare for.

**Three hunts collapse into one purchase.** The [[Lion Clan|Lion]] want a story about a samurai and an ancestor spirit rewritten. The [[Dragon Clan|Dragon]] want the meishōdō traffic stopped. [[Daidoji Shin]] wants whoever roused the spirits that drowned part of the [[Crane Lands|Crane lands]]. All three are chasing one woman's shopping, and only [[Ide Iwena]] — who is not hunting her at all, but protecting her — seems to understand that the danger runs toward her rather than from her.

Her novel about a [[Lion Clan|Lion]] samurai in love with an ancestor spirit reads very differently now. It may not be invention.

**The shopkeeper's fear is the most useful thing in the shop and it is not about her.** He offered, unprompted and before anyone had asked, that he had nothing to do with the missing persons. An Emerald Magistrate's indemnity was not enough for him, because the people he is actually frightened of do not answer to Emerald Magistrates. And a [[Kaeru Ronin|Kaeru]] officer was shadowing Imperial magistrates through his district while telling [[Bayushi Monban]] that outsiders *may disrupt delicate plans already in place*. Somebody in this city is running an operation, and the police are either part of it or waiting on it, and there is no way to tell which from the street.

**[[Shiba Midori]] found the thing that should frighten everybody and nobody picked it up.** There may not be a free kami left in the centre of this city — not because of the spiritual weather, but because whoever is doing this has made sure no spirit remains loose to carry word of it. A quiet where there should be voices is not an absence. It is evidence, and it is evidence of scale.

Two things done well. She took the paperwork before the police could, which is the difference between evidence and a story about evidence. And she paid an absurd price for a worthless tassel so that the man had a clean sale to point at — he survives having talked to them, which means he can be talked to again, which is worth more than the tassel and more than the receipt.

Two things to watch in herself. She has now asked a stranger in a shop, on the record, for something to ease [[Shinjo Harunobu]]'s spiritual burden. In a city where a [[Kaeru Ronin|Kaeru]] officer follows her from a teahouse, a [[Crane Clan|Crane]] agent knows her by sight, and somebody had a [[Toshi Ranbo]] story ready within hours of their arrival, a want stated aloud is a handle, and this is the second time this week she has handed one over.

And [[Daidoji Shin]] called her a peace cow to her face, in front of her own party, wrapped in enough wordplay to be deniable. She heard it, gave him nothing, and was right to. But he also spent that conversation cataloguing her — her Imperial bloodline, and a haunting she would not have chosen to advertise — and he is an ambitious mediocrity who wants her office and believes he is close to earning it. He is not dangerous because he is clever. He is dangerous because he will talk, to anyone, at length, and he has just learned two things about her worth talking about.
