Kitsu Takeko

Lion

What is known

S8The Kitsu daimyō at the Castle of the Swift Sword — young, small, red-haired, a year or two Setsuna's senior. She heard the ambush out and spent cavalry on it inside a minute: roads closed, four mon-less samurai to be taken alive, Akodo Atsushi in command. She refused to let Setsuna ride along. She refused to let her leave the tea to make herself presentable. And when the answer about her family broke into pieces, she crossed the room, held her, wiped her face, said it would not be spoken of, and promised to inquire after Shinjō Harunobu. Setsuna walked out with no strife and no honour lost. She had already sent healers to Monban on hearing he was hurt.
S9Gave Setsuna the case, the envoys' names, unconditional access to the archives, and the party's swords back polished and repaired — which inside a castle's walls means they are trusted not to use them. Golden-eyed, a shugenja, said to be a prodigy, and recently raised to the daimyōship. Midori sees fire spirits moving around her. She drew one line for the party: the Lion do not oppose a finding for the Unicorn, because the current war has nothing to do with what did or did not happen three centuries ago. Setsuna gave her a poem and was invited back to tea.
S19Lord of the Castle of the Swift Sword, and Akodo Sakuon's cousin. Agreed to write for Shinjō Harunobu's custody to be moved here. She has now given Setsuna cavalry, an archive, a promise and a prisoner transfer.
S26Performed the rite that compelled both ancestors to attend, with Midori assisting, and threw salts into the braziers to make them visible. She can make the dead seen but not heard except by shugenja, and translates for the court.
S27Granted Ayoko to Midori outright despite a badly delivered petition, and is more moved by wit and charm than by correctness. Received a sketched portrait of the mahōtsukai who escaped at Slowtide Harbor and reacted with horror and recognition — she knows who that is.
S48Made a heartfelt plea to Akodo Toturi on behalf of a worried wife, and it is the reason Setsuna's husband is free.

Each line is what she took from the session it cites, and nothing else. The Archivist's own account of this person is not reproduced here — it blends what she saw with what she did not.
