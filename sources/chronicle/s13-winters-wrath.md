## Narrative

!lede Snow fell all night and put one flat white sheet over the whole field. The [[Unicorn Clan|Unicorn]] know what is under it and the [[Lion Clan|Lion]] do not — but the same fall buried every marker the Unicorn had set themselves, so neither army could now read the ground it was standing on.

[[Ikoma Akuyaku]] came into the [[General’s Tent]] on a stretcher, interrupting the morning council, with both feet frozen, having walked twenty-odd miles through snow on a broken foot, trailing blood, his feet bound in strips of his own upper clothing with purple toes sticking out of the ends. [[Akodo Toronoko]] — his wife, and a commander of the Lion left — looked at him, looked at the toes, sighed, and asked the general's leave to fetch balms. *Your courage in making it back to the camp is commendable. Have you anything to report?*

He had the map, and he had this. The [[Unicorn Clan|Unicorn]] want the fight, which he noted is unlike them. They have allies. Their cavalry is either still coming or snowed in somewhere, and the numbers he saw were nothing like what he expected — equal to the Lion or a little more, nothing insurmountable. They are confident in their positioning because they know the terrain, and after this snowfall even they may not be sure any more: whatever signs they left themselves are under it. He nearly could not find his own route back. And the cold is not, in his judgement, a natural cold — he heard no chanting, but he said it anyway.

[[Matsu Sakura]] asked whether that was all he had learned, for all the damage. It was.

[[Akodo Toronoko]] came back with sage boiled in water and a paste of aloe and cayenne ground in a mortar, and put it on the frostbite herself, and it did not work. What worked was slower and took four people. [[Kitsu Somalia]] put Path to Inner Peace through him for the pain, which is not a technique for feet that have gone black. [[Akodo Toronoko]] directed triage and took two off the difficulty of everything the medics did afterwards. [[Akodo Kayamayako]], the chief medic, asked for a shugenja who could produce gentle fire and not the other kind — *if you unleash the full power of a fire kami on this, I will have to amputate* — and [[Kitsu Somalia]] called fire and held Extinguish over it the whole time while they turned his foot above the flame like something on a spit.

He kept the feet. He can feel his toes and they do not move, and a knife drawn along the sole of that foot gets nothing back. Permanent nerve damage. He will not move at speed again, and the medic said the quiet part: he may not be fit for the field, and certainly not as a scout, which is the only thing he is for. Confined to camp for the duration — because if he freezes it a second time he will not feel it happening, and then the foot comes off. *This is payback for you being an idiot*, [[Akodo Toronoko]] told him, *going off alone and not taking any kind of backup with you.*

[[Akodo Kagekatsu]] was waiting round the corner. *You cannot even keep track of your own husband and keep him out of trouble.* Then, with his arms folded: her troops got stuck in the mud, her husband got his foot stuck in the snow and came back with no useful intelligence whatsoever about intentions or deployments, and he was beginning to think the man's reputation for spying was vastly overstated — *as is your command skill.* [[Akodo Toronoko]] gave him back that her husband had still been more useful than a man whose only apparent function was heating the air, and asked what he had done the previous day. Commanded the reserves, as ordered. And then [[Akodo Kagekatsu]] said the thing worth writing down. *You will pay for the shame that our family suffered at your hands. Your comeuppance is coming.*

The plan was already made. No cavalry into that country; the [[Lion Clan|Lion]] horse stayed in reserve under [[Matsu Soko]]. [[Matsu Morozane]] asked for and got ashigaru recruited out of the foothills of the [[Spine of the World]] — men who know mountains — and took them as spearmen; [[Kitsu Somalia]] took samurai archers and left her shugenja behind for speed. The army's maps put some old mines somewhere up that way, and old mine paths were part of why the route was thought passable at all. Everyone not going into the mountains went to the front, because [[Matsu Sakura]] meant to take the [[Central Hill|hill in the middle of the field]] in the teeth of the weather, and the Unicorn would certainly come out to hold it. [[Akodo Toronoko]] asked for the left, which is open ground and good for bows. [[Akodo Kagekatsu]] got the right.

At the [[Frozen River]] the ice gave way under [[Kitsu Somalia]]'s archers. One man lost, four measures of panic through the rest, and a couple more who were pulled out and turned to popsicles and had to be left where they lay. It was a great deal less than it could have been. [[Matsu Morozane]]'s spearmen crossed without a scratch.

Higher up, [[Matsu Morozane]] found the thing this whole memory turns on. A disused mountain path running to what look like [[Abandoned Mines|abandoned workings]] — and that path and those workings were not on the topographical information the Lion army carried. The snow on the approach had been disturbed, and whoever disturbed it had covered their tracks. No traffic on the road itself, but at least one adit looked to have been entered, and possibly more. He cut a scout detachment out of his own strength to go up, look, and report — *our general needs to know the answer* — and kept moving, because a third of the army was off the field as long as they stood there.

Below, [[Akodo Toronoko]] opened at range on the fortified hill and most of her arrows clattered off it while the Unicorn jeered. What the volley did do was pin the enemy leader where he stood. Her second was Righteous Example, ranged, on a spent Void point, and that one went through the fortification and took attrition and panic with it, *as they realised just who was in command of that other force* — and dropped the difficulty for every other Lion leader on the field. Then a Unicorn cohort came at her in a running charge across open ground.

[[Ikoma Akuyaku]] got out of his bed. A quiet exit to the edge of the camp bought with strife, and a vantage from which he could watch his wife's archers work the hill, count two Unicorn units in reserve, and see the whole shape of it.

The flanking force came down off the mountain by the mine paths with [[Kitsu Somalia]] leading and the kami with her — and into the trees, where they discovered they were not alone. Unicorn samurai were in the forest waiting, several cohorts of them, set to charge. Somebody had got word of the ambush and put a force in front of it.

[[Kitsu Somalia]] had one volley before they closed and she spent it on the cohort with the man dancing on horseback juggling fire, who they had both seen before. The forest took most of it. Then three cohorts hit [[Matsu Morozane]]'s spearmen at once, and a fourth commander put fire into them on top, and by the end of it his unit was cut in half and still standing in place — holding the line, which is remarkable for ashigaru, and which the general's own narration allowed was partly because they had nowhere to run.

He reached for the Touchstone of Courage and it would not come. So he spent what he had on taking two measures of panic off them and then went to the front of them himself with [[Shiguro Chinmoku]] beside him. [[Kitsu Somalia]] had her archers put down the bows, draw swords, and cut at whatever was nearest, which was worth two more of the enemy. And [[Matsu Morozane]] got a Lightning Raid off — only because he is famously lucky, and the dice had to be thrown twice to find it — into the fire-dancer's cohort, and then a second blow behind it. Three panic and the worst casualties on that side of the field. If a way out exists it is through them.

Word came up from the plain that [[Matsu Sakura]] had taken the [[Central Hill]] and driven the [[Unicorn Clan|Unicorn]] off it, and nobody up on the ridge could say what that meant for them. [[Matsu Morozane]]'s reading was that the Unicorn had not been as strong as the Lion feared, since the flanking force was never needed. The other reading, which the field supports just as well, is that the Unicorn spent that strength on the trees instead.

## Learned

- Ikoma Akuyaku: Akodo Toronoko's husband. Walked back on a broken foot with both feet frozen and reported the Unicorn eager for a fight, allied to someone, short of the cavalry he expected, equal or slightly stronger in numbers, and confident of ground they may no longer be able to read. Said the cold did not feel natural. Kept his feet and took permanent nerve damage — he cannot feel the sole of that foot and the toes will not move. Confined to camp, and got out of bed to watch the battle anyway.
- Akodo Toronoko: Ikoma Akuyaku's wife, and commander of the Lion left. Her own frostbite treatment failed; her triage direction is what made the medics' work possible. Took open ground for her archers, pinned the enemy leader on the fortified hill with a volley that otherwise bounced, and broke through with Righteous Example on the second attempt. Gave Akodo Kagekatsu nothing when he came for her.
- Akodo Kagekatsu: Commanded the Lion right. Blamed Toronoko for her husband, for her troops and for the intelligence, and named the grievance behind it: a shame his family suffered at hers, for which he promises a reckoning.
- Akodo Kayamayako: The chief medic. Called for a shugenja who could make gentle fire rather than destructive fire, warning that the full power of a fire kami would cost Akuyaku the foot. Confined him to camp on the grounds that a man who cannot feel a freezing limb will not notice it freezing.
- Kitsu Somalia: Led samurai archers, leaving her shugenja behind for speed. Lost a man and took panic crossing the Frozen River, held Extinguish over the fire that saved Akuyaku's foot, found the way down off the mountain, and put her archers to swords when the trees emptied.
- Matsu Morozane: Took mountain-born ashigaru spearmen. Found the unmapped path and the covered tracks, and detached scouts out of his own strength to investigate. Lost half his unit to a four-sided attack, failed to rally them, and went to the front of what was left with his lion.
- Matsu Sakura: Ordered the hill in the middle of the field taken despite the snow, and took it.
- Matsu Soko: Held the Lion cavalry in reserve; no horses went into the mountains.
- Abandoned Mines: Workings above the Frozen River, reached by a disused path. Neither path nor workings appear on the Lion army's topographical information — though the army's maps did put old mines somewhere in that country, and the mine paths are what made the descent possible. The snow on the approach had been disturbed and then covered over, and at least one entrance had been used. Recent, and hidden on purpose.
- Frozen River: Crossed on the ice by the flanking force. It gave way under the archers.

## Setsuna

**This is where the workings enter the memory, and they enter it as somebody else's secret.**

The care is in the distinction. The [[Lion Clan|Lion]] were not ignorant that there were old mines in that country — their maps said so, and the old mine paths are a large part of why that mountain route was thought crossable at all. What was not on any map the army carried was the particular disused path [[Matsu Morozane]] found and the particular workings at the end of it. And the snow on that approach had been walked and then covered. That is not weather. Somebody was up there in the days before two armies met below, and took the trouble not to be followed, and went into at least one adit.

So the question Setsuna should carry forward is narrower and better than the one she was asking. Not *did anyone know the mines were there* — the Lion did, vaguely, the way an army knows a country has old workings in it. The question is who was using them that week, and why they cleaned up after themselves.

**And the ambush was known.** [[Matsu Morozane]] proposed the mountain flank out loud in a council held the morning after somebody was caught at [[Matsu Sakura]]'s tent and got away without showing a face. The [[Unicorn Clan|Unicorn]] were in the trees at the bottom of that mountain with several cohorts, numbers matched to what was coming down at them, in position and waiting. That is not a guess about terrain. Somebody carried that plan out of the Lion camp inside a day.

She should hold those two things apart and refuse to weld them, and she should be just as careful with the third. [[Shoshuro Amane]] rode to the parley two days ago with the [[Unicorn Clan|Unicorn]] honour guard, offered a peace nobody wanted, and could not be read by a shugenja looking straight at her. A covered path on the hill, a leak out of the Lion camp, and an unreadable [[Scorpion Clan|Scorpion]] on the field are three separate established facts and a very suggestive shape, and a shape is not evidence. The [[Scorpion Clan|Scorpion]]'s claim to the [[Old Diamond Mines]] is that they rendered assistance at the [[Snow Plain]]; what this memory gives her is a battle in which somebody rendered assistance to somebody, invisibly, twice, inside one week. What it does not give her is a name on it, and she must not spend the shape as though it were the name.

The rest is what the [[Lion Clan|Lion]] were doing to themselves. [[Akodo Kagekatsu]] told [[Akodo Toronoko]] that his family carries a shame suffered at hers and that her comeuppance is coming — in a war camp, with the two of them holding the opposite flanks of the same army. That is worth remembering when she reads what the Lion eventually wrote down about this battle, and who wrote it, and whose name is in it.
