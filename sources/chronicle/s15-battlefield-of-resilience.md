## Narrative

!lede [[Kitsu Yui]] got [[Matsu Morozane]] out of the snow and it cost her — she convulsed and went down in it herself, and [[Kitsu Somalia]], bent over the burns, did not see her fall. In the [[Healer’s Tent]] that night [[Akodo Kayamayako]] wrapped [[Matsu Morozane]]'s chest and arms and torso and told him to lie still, while the tent filled past its capacity and stopped being a tent and became a field hospital. In the morning he came out on a stick, bandaged from waist to shoulder. His lion came back too, limping, smelling of blood, and put its tongue on his face.

[[Matsu Sakura]] opened the council with the arithmetic. *We have three problems that must be dealt with.* The stores were poisoned and a third of the army was in its blankets. Morale had gone with them. And somebody had known about the flanking manoeuvre — there was a Unicorn spy inside the Lion's decisions.

[[Ikoma Akuyaku]] added Unicorn reinforcements coming up. [[Kitsu Yui]] added that the contaminated food was being thrown away faster than anyone wanted to say out loud. And a scouting party had come back east of the camp near the river — come back dead, with no tracks at the [[River Bend]]. The council turned that over and got as far as a spy, or a route nobody had mapped, and someone raised the possibility that the Unicorn were sitting in the [[Old Diamond Mines]].

The argument was food. [[Akodo Kagekatsu]] wanted the [[Peasant Village]] taken — it was feeding the Unicorn, taking it would starve them and fill the Lion's carts. [[Matsu Morozane]], on his stick, argued for holding what they had and looking at the village rather than lunging at it. They sanctioned [[Ikoma Akuyaku]] to go out again for intelligence on ruined feet, and [[Matsu Sakura]] sent him with the plainest order she gave anyone: *don't come back without answers. And if you don't come back, well, die well.*

Then they split the army — [[Akodo Kagekatsu]] to the village, [[Matsu Morozane]] and the rest to hold the [[Central Hill]] — and the blizzard came down on both halves at once and took their formations apart.

At the [[Rice Paddies]] the Unicorn were waiting in the snow. [[Akodo Kagekatsu]] called the charge to punch through to the village. [[Matsu Morozane]] took Void stance and met the flankers and pushed them back off the line. [[Kitsu Somalia]] put Fires From Within into two of their leaders at once and burned both. On the hill [[Akodo Toronoko]] took a Unicorn field leader in a duel through the blizzard, opened him with a Flowing Water Strike and finished it, and the Lion around her lifted and the Unicorn around him did not.

[[Matsu Morozane]] called out their commander. *I will stake my horse against your honour.* [[Takun Norio]] came at him with a naginata up, went off his horse onto a stake, and [[Shiguro Chinmoku]] was on him before he could get off it.

None of it mattered. The village was a trap and the cavalry rode into it. [[Akodo Kagekatsu]] was cut badly and stayed up; the cohort with him was destroyed. And on the third round at the [[Central Hill]] a Unicorn flanking force came up out of the snowbanks — out of the ground, where nobody had seen them get to — directly onto [[Matsu Sakura]], [[Kitsu Somalia]] and the whole officer corps.

## Learned

- Kitsu Yui: Pulled Morozane out of the snow and collapsed convulsing from the effort, unnoticed. She was on her feet again for the council, and it was she who put the scale of the food loss in front of the generals.
- Matsu Sakura: Named the three crises — poisoned stores with a third of the army down, broken morale, and a Unicorn spy who knew the flanking plan. Sent a man with ruined feet back out for intelligence with the order to die well if he could not return with answers.
- Akodo Kagekatsu: Pushed hardest to take the Peasant Village for its food, led the charge himself, rode into the ambush, and was gravely wounded holding on.
- Akodo Toronoko: Won a duel against a Unicorn field leader in the blizzard with a Flowing Water Strike, and the Lion line lifted off it.
- Takun Norio: A Unicorn commander. Took Morozane's challenge, charged with a naginata, went off his horse onto a stake and was killed by the lion.
- Unicorn Spy: Never found. Knew the Lion flanking plan in advance. A Lion scouting party was killed east of camp near the river and left no tracks at the River Bend; the council raised the Old Diamond Mines as a place the Unicorn or their insurgents could be sitting.
- Peasant Village: Suspected of supplying the Unicorn. Taking it was a trap.

## Setsuna

The two accounts reconcile now, and the reconciliation is worse than either.

The [[Lion Clan|Lion]] took the [[Central Hill]] and killed three Unicorn commanders and still lost the [[Snow Plain]] — not on the field, but to poisoned stores, a third of the army sick in its blankets, a village that was bait, and somebody who knew the flanking plan before it was run. [[Ikoma Ichigo]]'s Unicorn victory is true. It is just not a victory of arms, and his account does not say what kind of victory it was.

Three facts sit together and she should not pretend they do not. A scouting party died east of camp, by the river, leaving no tracks at all. A Unicorn flanking force came up out of the snowbanks onto the officer corps from ground nobody had them on. And the [[Lion Clan|Lion]]'s own flanking route went up along the old mine paths — the workings [[Matsu Morozane]] found with the snow over them swept flat.

Somebody was moving through that hill unseen. The Lion council's guess was Unicorn insurgents in the [[Old Diamond Mines]] and they never proved it. Her own reading is that the party who knew the hill was worth something was the [[Scorpion Clan|Scorpion]], and that a body which knows the tunnels can sell that knowledge to whichever army is willing to pay for a route — or use it themselves and let both armies blame each other.

She cannot prove any of that and should not say it aloud. What she can do is stop arguing about who won the battle. **The mines were in use during it, by someone, and both claimants were fighting downhill in ignorance.** That is the fact the deed dispute has been built on top of without knowing it was there.
