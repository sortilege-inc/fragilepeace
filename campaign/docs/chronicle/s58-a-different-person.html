A Different Person

Session 58·2026-09-28

A Dragon monk. Silent and thoughtful, gentle, and wise.

They came back out of Gaki-dō at sunrise and found themselves standing at the foot of the great willow, which is to say on Lion ground, where none of them is welcome. Nobody had seen them yet. Bayushi Monban scratched the fox behind the ears, told it to bite him, and swam the river invisible to the Dragon side, arriving on the far bank looking like a drowned rat.

At the governor's mansion, Tonbo Kuma and Shiba Midori made their report. Governor Miya Tetsuya greeted Tonbo Kuma by welcoming him back to the land of the living, and Tonbo Kuma — short of words for it — went to the standing go board between them and moved his piece to a more aggressive position before he said anything at all.

What he had to say was this. The great willow is a soft place: the world is thin there, and the tree is what defends it from what would otherwise come through. The bindings are renewed and the city has peace for a few generations, not forever, and the record must say so plainly or the next people to need it will not know it was ever a thing that needed doing. They have the diagrams. The rite is reproducible.

Then he made his one confession, eloquently, which amounted to admitting he had decided something on the governor's behalf without asking: that the city did not want a permanent two-way road into Gaki-dō running through it. Presumptuous, yes — but not incorrect.

The accounting after that was mostly about who had done the work. A fortune in jade had been spent, and it is not a small thing that it was spent. Shiba Midori named Bayushi Monban as the reason it was recovered at all — without reclaiming the jade, I do not think we would have been successful — and then, seeing that this visibly discomfited the governor, leaned on it a little harder out of pure mischief. Governor Miya Tetsuya allowed that the Scorpion had done nothing to discredit the office. Tonbo Kuma he commended outright, and then declined to release him: his duty is here, he is the local man for this sort of thing, and there is a go game to finish. That last is not a joke either way round.

The company is going south, to the Crab lands, to look for Bayushi Monban's betrothed, which Shiba Midori declined to explain in any detail in front of outsiders and which reduces, as Governor Miya Tetsuya observed, to a startlingly short answer for a very complicated thing. He laid out every way to get there — the River of Gold and the portage over the Spine of the World; down the Drowned Merchant River to the sea and along the Crane coast, weeks not months; the lake road past the castle of the Wasp if they wanted to avoid Scorpion lands entirely; or back through Unicorn country and around. How you intend to get there means everything. He offered a boat, a portage, horses, whichever they chose.

Elsewhere, Shinjō Harunobu was losing at go.

He has been a hostage at the Castle of the Swift Sword long enough to acquire five or six Lion pre-teens who follow him about and whom he calls his nephews, whom he arms with bokken and takes on three and four at a time — serious, but more casual than anything they are used to. He has also been trying to learn the board games the Lion prize, against hosts who took the view that if they were going to play him they were going to play to win. He was losing badly when Akodo Akihito arrived.

Under the treaty Doji Setsuna negotiated, the Lion daimyō has released all Unicorn hostages on recognizance until Winter Court. Shinjō Harunobu is still technically a hostage, and he is going to Winter Court this year, and until then he is free. His horse was waiting. His scimitar was returned at the gate. At no point in any of it was he treated as anything other than an honoured guest.

He spent the ride to the City Of The Rich Frog showing off — overshooting the column, circling back, letting them catch up and doing it again — because he has been cooped up. On the way past he made a whinnying sound with his mouth that put two Lion out of their saddles; their leader held on, then copied the sound himself to see it work, and filed it away. The captured Utaku steeds they are riding are one more thing nobody has decided what to do with yet. Akodo Akihito tried to keep pace and could not, which the eye he no longer has did not help. At the parting he promised to capture him again next time.

Then he rode a circuit of the city, came across Doji Setsuna window-shopping for silks, and — rather than call out — swung off the horse and swept her up, which was on the whole the most dignified undignified thing available.

On the Dragon side, Bayushi Monban went looking for the monk who taught him. He found Togashi Gembei tending a rock garden, and asked for a disguise.

The suggestion is not his own. Akodo Akihito gave it to him in a tent before the signing — unmask, change clothes, drop the name, and sail the enemy's own river straight through his front gate — and Bayushi Monban has carried it since. The River of Gold is much the fastest way to the Crab lands and it runs the entire length of Scorpion country, where there are people who would very much like to know he is back. He will not risk his companions for the sake of his own face.

Togashi Gembei listened to all of it without hurrying him, and set his price in the form of questions. Are the companions samurai? Are they afraid of danger? It seems we continuously run towards it. Then: the swords must be set aside, which is difficult for a samurai and which some monks never manage; and the head must be shaved, which does grow back. What it cost to say yes to the second was not vanity. Shaved, the demon wound shows — the scar goes all the way round his neck — and he was asked what it was. A gift from both the Lady of Decay and the Final Judge. One day he will tell the story.

There was no tattooist to be found, so the tattoos will wait for the Crab lands. What he has instead is a high collar bearing a Togashi school mark, worn over the scar. He sent his niece's katana back to her rather than carry it, which leaves him with a wakizashi, and the badge, and a name that is not his: Togashi Yuriki.

Between those two errands, the duel.

Doji Setsuna brought her husband to meet her yōjimbō. Shinjō Harunobu gestured at the string of ponies he had brought for the journey, established that this was the man who had been guarding his wife, said let us see what you are made of, and drew. Bayushi Monban answered by throwing a fist of air at him, and the street cleared.

It was a prepared duel in everything but warning. Bayushi Monban flicked a foot like a man tapping a ball and the air hit; the lacquered armour did nothing much but the impact landed and pushed him back a band. Shinjō Harunobu threw his wakizashi at him butt-first. Bayushi Monban picked it up, inspected it, and kicked again, harder, and blew him three range bands down the street. So Shinjō Harunobu took somebody else's horse, took a borrowed yari rather than his own tetsubō because he did not want to kill the man, rode the distance back down and put the butt of the spear into his chest at a gallop. It broke armour and it did not break him.

Both of them hit compromised in the same round. The lower honour goes first — fifty-five against seventy-five — so Bayushi Monban's finishing blow landed first, a fist of air, and Shinjō Harunobu rode it down from a debilitating gash to a flesh wound and stood there with his sword half drawn. Then he put it back. It is not any yōjimbō I have ever seen. But he seems to have kept you protected well enough. He returned the borrowed horse to wherever he had taken it from. First blood, and honour in it, and the two of them will be travelling south together.

The Kaeru rōnin arrived immediately after it was over, as they had at the beginning of all this, to ask the Unicorn lord whether this ruffian was troubling him.

They found him again that evening at the Dragon docks, three of them, standing between him and his boat. They wanted an apology. They observed, at length and to each other, that a certain foul wind had been thrashed on a bridge by Karahaya not long ago and evidently had not learned from it. He read them — they were deciding whether to throw him in the river — and answered by naming what they were and were not: twice he has protected this city from things they are incapable of fighting, twice he has had contempt for it, and the third time, if they know anything about the Scorpion, is unlucky. Then he reached for the fox to vanish, and the fox was not in his robe.

They put a geta between his ankles and a hand on his back and he went into the water. The boatman who fished him out had not seen a thing, and was certain nobody else had either, and advised him to be honest about it anyway if asked, rather than be grouped with the rōnin trash of the city. He arrived at Togashi Gembei's dripping. A bath was not necessary, but I suppose we can begin there. Before the razor he sat and wrote to the acting Emerald Champion about how rōnin in this city speak to Emerald Magistrates, and about what they are and are not capable of protecting.

Shiba Midori spent her afternoon on Akodo Akihito, who has been resisting treatment for days. She got him from severely wounded to lightly wounded, then took tea with him and worked the rest of it loose across the table, which is the only way it was going to come out. He has a fresh and stylish eye patch over the socket. The eye is not coming back; Kakita Kazumi took it in a duel neither of them is happy about, and Kakita Kazumi won.

What they talked about was marriage. Shiba Midori pushed, because she thinks Akodo Akihito is attracted to Shinjō Miharu and wants to know why the match was never made. She got a conflicted face rather than a clean answer. What she did get is that he holds Shinjō Miharu to be a magnificent woman, that he wishes she had been his colleague at the Topaz Championship, that Kakita Kazumi once called her horse-faced and considered her a match for himself and beneath Akodo Akihito — and that if a match were possible it would have happened already. Duty came out of his mouth for what may be the first time with a shade of regret in it: if the clan asks him to marry to make peace, he will. He also asked her, with no small talk to soften it, how her own betrothed was.

There is a matchmaking economy running underneath all of this. Shiba Midori is working on a match for her own cousin, who has reasons of their own for finding Kakita Kazumi acceptable, and asked that it be held in confidence. And there were three cranes sniggering in the corner of the tea house the whole time, paying close attention to a Phoenix and a Lion taking tea together, which Akodo Akihito had noticed long before she mentioned it.

Her own situation she stated plainly: the Phoenix have asked her to make herself into a more powerful shugenja, she has been kept from it by Emerald Magistrate duty, and she is a candidate before the Elemental Council rather than a member of anything. If she champions the Phoenix who were slighted, will they call her theirs, or will they see someone who traded a duty for a game?

The last tea was hers and Bayushi Monban's, in the Dragon district, because he is not permitted at the governor's residence and would not leave the quarter. He arrived with his head shaved and his collar on and told her he had met Doji Setsuna's husband and had a little conversation with weapons. She raised an eyebrow at that. He noted that he had not started it.

Then she pushed on the disguise, and her objection was not that it was a bad plan. It was that he had arranged it before any of them knew, that he is the only one taking a precaution in a party that is all going down the same river, and that he keeps trading things away before he has priced them. Do not sacrifice something before you know its value. He said that as long as they are together everything will be fine, and that if it means protecting her he will do it, and she put her very small hand on his much larger one and told him she has noticed he does this for others too, and that people like that pay more than anyone thinks. Brother and sister in first enemy blood. He promised to be careful, and then described what careful looks like — a Dragon monk: silent and thoughtful, gentle, and wise — and nobody at that table believed a word of it.

Doji Setsuna is not going south. She goes to Otosan Uchi with the Imperial Herald, and the party she has been travelling with for a year goes down the River of Gold without her.

The crossing is by boat down the Three Sides River to the foot of the Spine of the World, a day's travel, and then over the mountains past the Shosuro family castle, where travel papers are checked. Shiba Midori is the only one of them presenting a badge. Bayushi Monban is an Emerald Magistrate and is not presenting himself as one, which makes him part of a magistrate's entourage, which is exactly what an entourage looks like.

So she lied to the border, and he forged the paper she lied with. The story is a trade dispute at the Yasuki estates over the procurement and quality and price of artworks — and the aggrieved purchasers, in the paperwork, are a pair of Phoenix twins the party has met before, stuck with it purely for fun. Kakita Kazumi would not help forge it. The Scorpion took the documentation, took notes, took the name of everyone in the party, and asked Shiba Midori one question she had not prepared for: what became of Bayushi Monban, who was supposed to be travelling in her company? She said he had other duties and they hoped he would rejoin them — a solid member of the party — while the man himself stood in the room with a shaved head. The clerk's face told her she needs to work on her lying.

Then the clerk made a note, and mentioned that Shosuro Hametsu is in the city downriver, and would very much like to see their wayward samurai.

Setsuna

She negotiated her husband out of captivity and was not told when it happened. The terms are hers — every Unicorn hostage released on recognizance until Winter Court is a line she wrote — and the way she found out it had been executed was a very large man vaulting off a horse in a silk market. That is not a complaint worth making out loud. It is worth noticing that the treaty is now a thing that happens to people at a distance from her, which is what a treaty is supposed to become, and which is the first time one of hers has.

The company is dividing, and she is the one leaving it. For a year the answer to any question has been that they go together. Now Bayushi Monban has shaved his head and put on another man's name, Shiba Midori is carrying the only badge, Kakita Kazumi is keeping his Topaz armour packed, and Shinjō Harunobu is going with them — and she is going to Otosan Uchi with the Herald, which is where the record of all of this gets made. Both halves are her work. Only one of them is the half where anyone can be killed.

Her yōjimbō was tested in the street by her husband and passed in a way she will have to think about. Shinjō Harunobu drew on him unannounced and got air thrown at him, a thrown wakizashi returned, and finally a man on a stolen horse conceding at first blood — it is not any yōjimbō I have ever seen, but he seems to have kept you protected well enough. What her husband learned is that the Scorpion will not fight the way he was asked to. What she should learn is that the two men who have most reason to be jealous of each other's place beside her have instead decided they like each other, which is convenient, and which means neither of them will tell her anything about the other.

And the thing he is doing to himself is the thing to watch. Bayushi Monban is going into his own clan's lands as a Dragon monk because the alternative is his companions paying for his face. Shiba Midori put the objection better than anyone: not that the plan is wrong, but that he arranged it alone, before telling them, and that he keeps spending things before he has found out what they were worth. The head, the katana, the name. He has been doing this since the jade. Somebody should be counting for him, and the one person who does is going down the river with him and not with her.
