Battlefield of Resilience and Chaos

Session 15·2025-07-01

The general sent one of her two best commanders away to cool off, and in her absence the other one won the argument. The Lion marched on a village that was bait, and the record of the day is a duel nobody remembers and a defeat nobody wrote down.

Matsu Morozane surfaced twice in the night. The first time was in the snow, long enough to watch Kitsu Yue — who had just dragged him out of it — drop into a convulsive fit beside him, and to think that somebody ought to do something about that before he went under again. Kitsu Somalia was bent over his burns and did not see it. Nobody saw it but him.

The second time was in the Healer’s Tent, under more bandages than he had ever seen on a living man, with Akodo Kayamayako telling him to lie still. Large parts of his chest, arms and torso were burned through. The tent had stopped being a tent — soldiers past any capacity to treat them, temporary shelters going up to hold the overflow, and every healthy body that could be spared pressed into helping. He said he would give the cot up the moment he could stand. In the morning his lion woke him by licking his face, covered in dried blood that was mostly not its own, and limping from a kick.

He walked out on a stick, wrapped from waist to shoulder, into a morning cold enough that standing in it cost every officer something. And because the fire had gone into his fire ring, everything he would ever again attempt through fire had just become three degrees harder.

The general's council started late, because two Akodo had come close to tearing each other apart outside it and Matsu Sakura had sent Akodo Toronoko to the far side of the camp to cool off. She was not in the tent. That mattered more than anything else that was said in it.

My warriors, we have three problems that must be dealt with. The enemy had the initiative. The stores were poisoned and a third of the army was lying in cots. Morale had gone with them. And the enemy had known about the flanking manoeuvre before it was deployed — there is a spy among us. Then, looking hard at Ikoma Akuyaku: reinforcements had reached the Unicorn camp in the night.

Ikoma Akuyaku proposed a parley — send a small party to talk, delay them, and get a look at what had arrived. Matsu Sakura scowled. Would that not be interpreted as a sign of weakness? Kitsu Yue put the supply position in front of them instead: most of the food had been thrown out for fear of contamination, resupply would not come soon, and something had to be found. Akodo Kagekatsu walked in on that sentence with an answer. A peasant village, not far, certainly feeding the Unicorn. Solve two problems with one stroke. Starve the Unicorn. Feed ourselves. Take the village.

The arithmetic was thin in every direction. Two-thirds of the Lion still fit to fight, a quarter dead, a quarter down. The Unicorn had lost about a third of theirs in the mountains and had been reinforced by an unknown quantity. I would take half a force of Lion against two-thirds of a force of Unicorn, said Kitsu Yue. With unknown reinforcements, somebody answered.

Then the thing nobody had raised yet. Matsu Morozane's scout detachment — the one he had cut out of his own strength to go and look at the workings — never came back. They were found dead east of the camp, by the river, as though returning. Ikoma Akuyaku had been out at the River Bend trying to work out how anyone had reached the eastern side of the camp unseen, and had found no tracks at all and could not get close enough around the bend. Akodo Kagekatsu wanted to know why this was the first anyone had heard of it. Morale is already low enough.

The council chewed on it and produced two readings and no proof. Either the Unicorn had a force sitting in the Old Diamond Mines — it is a hole in the ground, and you can hide a lot of things in the ground — or the spy had known where those scouts would come back and been waiting for them. Kitsu Somalia argued for the spy: a full unit in those mountains would have been seen on the way up, because they had gone up looking for trouble and found it three times. What everyone agreed on was worse than either. The Unicorn were behaving as though they had freedom of movement across the entire plain.

Matsu Morozane argued to hold the Central Hill and meet them in the field, and argued it badly — a fire argument out of a man with a scrambled head, at a difficulty he never had a chance of clearing. It was heard, commended and set aside. Akodo Kagekatsu made the case that men cannot bite on an empty stomach, and made it well, and asked only for half the available force. Kitsu Yue said what should not have needed saying — that splitting the army left both halves weaker — and was shouted down. With Akodo Toronoko on the other side of the camp, nobody in that tent was equipped to take Akodo Kagekatsu apart, and he carried it.

Ikoma Akuyaku asked to go out again for the intelligence they were missing, on feet he could no longer feel, and sold it with a song and a dance — look, I can still move — staking five honour and five glory on coming back with something. Akodo Kagekatsu's contribution was if your wife will let you. Matsu Sakura agreed, and put it plainly. Don't come back without answers. And if you don't come back, well, die well.

So the army split. Akodo Kagekatsu took half the mobile yari samurai to the village, with Matsu Morozane beside him — the village looked to him like the surer bet — and Ikoma Akuyaku attached to slip away from it. Kitsu Somalia went to the hill, at Kitsu Yue's asking, because a shugenja on that position would make the Unicorn think twice. About half the surviving shugenja had eaten the bad food; the rest went with her. Then the blizzard closed and both halves of the army lost contact with the camp inside a few minutes.

At the village, the Rice Paddies were entangling, obscuring and confining all at once, and there was already a Unicorn force in the village, which came out to meet the Lion rather than let the fighting reach the houses. Akodo Kagekatsu called the charge. Matsu Morozane went at the flankers mounted, in Void stance, with Shattering Tide Style — a Badger kata that wants exactly the sort of ground nobody else could move through — and put them back on their heels and off their position, and the Lion advanced through the paddies. Ikoma Akuyaku made himself a parley flag out of nothing and hid it on his person, waited for the engagement, and tried to slip north-west along a trail, and failed to go unnoticed.

At the hill, Kitsu Somalia stayed beside the general and handed her cohort to Kitsu Yue so she could work as a shugenja rather than a commander. She reached into Fires From Within and took the two Unicorn outriders who were riding ahead of the line to bring the flanks around — a mounted warrior woman and a hard-riding swordsman — for five points of fire each, which armour does not stop. The swordsman looked back through the flames as though he knew exactly who had done it, and she smiled at him. Then a Wall of Fire down the left of the line, three rounds of it, splitting the Unicorn in two and shouted through to Kitsu Yue before it went up.

And Akodo Toronoko, back from being sent away and marching to reinforce the centre, found the enemy commander and called him out. Shinjō Gansari dismounted because she was not mounted. She staked ten honour and five glory, bid four measures of strife to take the initiative off him, and opened with a Flowing Water Strike through obscuring, entangling ground at a difficulty of five — spending Void to reach it and calling on her grandmother to place the blow. Your grandmother directs your blow toward your foe's exposed shoulder. It knocked a plate off him and left him bleeding. He answered with the same technique and left her bleeding too.

Two rounds later he was compromised, and she took the finishing blow with a katana in two hands at twice its deadliness, and he died badly. He offered her his hand as he went down. You have done well to fight me, and you are not an easy foe. May your ancestors smile upon you for this honourable duel. She took his head. He was one of the Unicorn's Five Pillars, and the Unicorn army took five measures of panic from losing him. Every Lion within reach of her came off that duel with five measures of their own strife gone, which is what a Renowned Warrior is for.

And then the village stopped being a supply problem and became what it had always been. The enemy sprang out of every crevice and corner of it — arrows, spears, men who had been waiting — and Akodo Kagekatsu's force had walked into the middle of it. Eighteen measures of attrition in one blow, doubled for the round, and the Lion cohort began to come apart.

The Unicorn commander rode at Matsu Morozane with a bow he did not bother to use. I should shoot you and put you out of your misery. He holstered it, took the naginata off the saddle, and came on. Matsu Morozane — on foot, burned to the shoulder, a hand he could not use and a leg he could barely stand on, looking, as the general put it, like a walking corpse — called him out anyway. Battle of the Mind took Fire and Earth off the man's options before a blade moved.

Takun Norio had the initiative and missed by one and rode straight past him. Matsu Morozane spent his turn getting out of the way and spending everything he had on being able to move at all next time, and turned and goaded him back. The second pass he put the yari into the horse. The horse reared, Takun Norio came off it, and Shigo no Chinmoku was on him before he hit the ground — a savage mauling against a prone man who could not defend against it, at twice deadliness, which nothing survives.

It changed nothing. The Unicorn had already seized the day. Matsu Morozane reached for the Touchstone of Courage and deliberately failed it to take the opportunities instead, pulling two measures of panic off his men so the withdrawal would be a withdrawal. They got off the field. They did not take the village, and the casualties were massive, and Akodo Kagekatsu was somewhere behind them on the ground with a wound that nearly finished him.

Back at the hill, in the third round, a Unicorn flanking force came up out of the snowbanks — out of ground nobody had them on — and rode straight for Matsu Sakura and her officers, three days into a battle for the Snow Plain that neither army could any longer read.

Setsuna

The two accounts reconcile now, and the reconciliation is worse than either.

The Lion took the Central Hill, killed one of the Unicorn's Five Pillars in single combat, and still lost this day — to poisoned stores, a third of the army in its blankets, a village that was bait, and somebody sitting inside the Lion's own decisions. Ikoma Ichigo's Unicorn victory is true. It is simply not a victory of arms, and his account does not say what kind of victory it was.

And the decision that lost the village was made in a tent one commander had been ordered out of. Two Akodo nearly came to blows that morning, Matsu Sakura sent Akodo Toronoko away to cool down, and Akodo Kagekatsu then carried a plan to split the army with nobody of equal weight in the room to argue it. Kitsu Yue tried and was shouted down. That is not treachery and it is not bad luck. It is a feud between two families deciding a battle, and it is the kind of thing a clan history is written specifically to leave out.

Then the part she cannot explain. Matsu Morozane's own scouts — sent up to look at the workings with the covered path — never reported, and were found dead east of the camp, by the river, as though on their way home, with no tracks near them at all. The council's two readings were a Unicorn force hidden in the Old Diamond Mines, or the spy waiting for men he knew were coming. Kitsu Somalia argued the spy. Nobody proved anything, and by nightfall a Unicorn flanking force had come up out of the snowbanks behind the whole Lion command from ground that had been empty.

Her own reading — and she should hold it as a reading and not say it in a courtroom — is that the party who knew what that hill was worth was the Scorpion, and that anyone who knows a set of tunnels can sell a route through them to whichever army will pay, or walk it themselves and leave two clans to blame each other.

What she can actually stand on is smaller and harder. Somebody was moving under and around that hill throughout the battle, and neither claimant could see them doing it. The deed dispute has been built on top of that without knowing it was there.
