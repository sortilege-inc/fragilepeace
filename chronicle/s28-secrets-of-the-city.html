Secrets of the City

Session 28·2025-11-18

A Unicorn shugenja broke his grandmother's jade cup as a child and something came out of it. He has been arguing ever since that what the Unicorn do is a partnership. The Phoenix call it caging.

The City Of The Rich Frog is divided and that division is the whole of its usefulness: the Lion hold the south bank, the Dragon and the Unicorn the rest, and a person who wants something that is illegal in one district need only cross a bridge. The magistrates took two leads. Where the illicit Unicorn spirit-charms are coming from, and where the author is who wrote the story that could ruin a Lion samurai.

The story matters more than it sounds. It has a Lion samurai in a romance with an ancestor spirit, and the Lion daimyō wants it dealt with. Meanwhile a Dragon magistrate — Kitsuki Wataru's son — is working the Meishōdō artifacts that have been turning up in Dragon lands. The two leads are the same lead from different ends.

They decided not to announce themselves. Shiba Midori put the question of whether to go to Governor Miya Tetsuya as Emerald Magistrates straight away, and the answer they settled on was no — a badge closes mouths in a city like this one.

So Doji Setsuna went in as a naïve traveller with money and curiosity, and got Ide Iwena talking about Meishōdō curios. Iwena sent her to a shop in the Unicorn District and told her the words to use — relics from far away — and warned her that the locals do not approve of trading things with spirits in them. Her caution ran deeper than clan politics; whatever her objection is, it is personal.

Shiba Midori fell into the real conversation of the evening with Iuchi Minoru, who holds that what the Unicorn do with spirits is a partnership and not a servitude. He knows exactly where that argument gets him: Isawa Kaede once accused his practice of caging spirits and it nearly went to a duel between shugenja. He told Shiba Midori how he came to it. As a child he broke his grandmother's jade tea cup, and in the moment it shattered, something rose out of it.

Bayushi Monban worked the same man for the black market and got a warning shaped like a metaphor: a sacred relic is a vessel with a being of great power inside it, and people who traffic in those do not always know what they are carrying.

One of the Unicorn told Doji Setsuna that Hana no Ame had gone upriver to Toshi Ranbo and was no longer in the city. Shiba Midori did not believe a word of it and said so — a lie planted to send them the wrong way.

Kakita Kazumi spent the evening building a disguise out of nothing. He paid a servant to cut his sake with water so he could be visibly drunk and quietly sober, and pocketed the change. He had also been talking to Kaeru Akiara, a rōnin hired by the Lion, about what happens at The Docks after dark — and Kaeru Akiara's proposal was to put Kakita Kazumi down there as bait and see what came for him.

They agreed to do it that night.

Setsuna

Going in without the badge was the right call and it should be held to. In a city split three ways across a river, an Emerald Magistrate's authority is an announcement, and every announcement in this place crosses a bridge faster than she can. She got more out of Ide Iwena in one conversation as a curious traveller than the office would have bought her in a week.

The two leads are one lead. Illicit Meishōdō surfacing in Dragon lands; a scandalous story about a Lion samurai and an ancestor spirit; a Unicorn practice that the Phoenix call caging. Everything in this city is about spirits being put into objects and moved across borders, and the story about the samurai may not be scandal at all. It may be a description.

She should also hold Iuchi Minoru's warning against her own recent history. A vessel with a being of great power in it, carried by people who do not know what they have — that is precisely the black talisman Matsu Morozane cut off a Unicorn shugenja's neck three hundred years ago and handed to a Kitsu to interrogate. That thread has come at her twice now.

The Toshi Ranbo lie is worth more than the truth it hides. Shiba Midori is right that it is a lie; the question is who bothered to plant it. Somebody in this city knew the party was looking for Hana no Ame within hours of them arriving and had an answer ready. They are being watched, and by someone who thinks the author is worth protecting.

One caution about tonight. Putting Kakita Kazumi on The Docks as bait is Kaeru Akiara's idea, and Kaeru Akiara is a rōnin in Lion pay whom they met this evening. It may be a good plan. It is also the plan to propose if what is wanted is one of them alone, in a known place, after dark.
