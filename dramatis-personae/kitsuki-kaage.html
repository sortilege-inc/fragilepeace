Dramatis Persona

Kitsuki Kaage

Dragon

What is known

S1The Emerald Magistrate who holds her orders. He sent her to the Castle of the Swift Sword to weigh a Scorpion–Unicorn dispute that turns on Lion records — which is to say he has sent her somewhere she cannot succeed without Lion cooperation.
S2Held at the Emerald Champion's castle by other business, which is why Setsuna was sent ahead without particulars. The case may turn on a request to alter a building on the historical register.
S18Arriving at the Castle of the Swift Sword in three days with the Scorpion and Unicorn ambassadors, to adjudicate the claim.
S22Sitting as Senior Emerald Magistrate. Laid three conflicting versions of Akuyaku's journal side by side and stated at least two are forgeries. Permitted sworn testimony subject to cross-examination. Took Monban apart in private for withholding the secret treaty document, then admitted it. "There's been quite enough delays arguing over evidence."
S23Withheld Monban's secret treaty from evidence pending verification and sent to the Lion library for records to check it against. Tabled Setsuna's dismissal motion for the same reason — nothing rules until the evidence is authenticated. Told the room to bring substantiated claims, not accusations.
S24Read out the letter to "the least of the Bayushi", set it aside as a historical artifact rather than evidence, and sent the field records to Lion experts for authentication.
S25Ruled that one of the two competing secret documents is forged, and that the proof is not good enough to say which. Warned Ide Subane what an accusation against an Emerald Magistrate would require. Relieved to have one authentic body of evidence.
S26Openly sceptical of ancestral vision as testimony — asked how anyone attests to a battle three centuries gone, and called the shared-vision scroll fanciful lore. Refused Setsuna's request to be excused from the séance.
S27Named Setsuna's argument the most persuasive before him in the ruling.

Each line is what she took from the session it cites, and nothing else. The Archivist's own account of this person is not reproduced here — it blends what she saw with what she did not.
