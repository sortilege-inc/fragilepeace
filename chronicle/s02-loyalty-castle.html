Loyalty Castle

Session 2·2025-03-18

Every Lion dojo keeps a copy of Shinsei's Tao, and every copy is unopened. The one in the mustering square at Loyalty Castle is sealed with wax, and the rumour that a missing chapter is hidden somewhere in these walls has been enough, for a century, to keep pilgrims arriving.

Loyalty Castle — Shiro Kodo — is a day's ride from Otosan Uchi across flat ground, close enough that the capital stands on the horizon and the Lion Clan Champion can answer an attack on it the same day. It is the ancestral seat of the Akodo and their largest staging ground, a place for cycling and drilling troops with the forges lit day and night, and it was never built to host guests. Its layout is deliberately labyrinthine so that no one can mistake it for a palace fit to hold the Emperor's Winter Court. Once a year it makes an exception and holds the grandest Go tournament in the Empire.

The guest quarters said the rest. Baths, a hearty plain meal with rice and meat in quantity and whatever sake was asked for, and a request not to leave the guest wing after dark — the night watch was on, and had not been told anyone would be in the guest quarters at all.

Akodo Ieyasu holds the castle and is the Akodo daimyō's karō; the daimyō's court is elsewhere, and not even his personal estate is on these grounds. Akodo Masami, the yoriki, handles guests, and handled these ones well. He dressed to about a seven to receive a lady of her standing, kept his manners unctuous and correct, and turned out to be very well read: the conversation went to pillow books and stayed pleasant. Doji Setsuna told him she had been sent by Kitsuki Kaage, who had meant to take the case himself and could not, and that she was trying to close the gap between an adequate job and a Kage-level one. She asked whether the fighting would obstruct the work.

What she got back was careful. The Lion do not call it a war — a war is a specific thing — they call it the incident. They know the incident involves one of the parties to the dispute she is being sent to judge, and they understand that they are being called on as the keepers of the histories involved. The Castle of the Swift Sword is close to the fighting but unlikely to be drawn into it, and she would not be an imposition so long as she did not draw down the resources committed to the war. He gave her the name of a cousin at the War College to ask for, and a set of Lion travel papers for herself and her people — a different document from Imperial papers, and worth more on this ground, closing as Lion passports always close: there will be consequences for interfering with those on Lion business. He confirmed the castle's swift messenger had already carried Bayushi Monban's letter out. He ended by paying her a genuine compliment, said he would be glad to correspond, and left her the means to do it.

A sentiment check read him as shrewd, which made him harder to read, not easier.

Bayushi Monban had the worst of the night. A missive reached him from his own contact among the Yasuki: Hida Chizuzu had gone over the Wall on a mission, her party was a week past due, no rescue had been dispatched, and the likeliest readings were captured, tainted or dead. The chop was correct and he could vouch for it. The hand was not. It was clear to the point of severity, the characters replicated exactly, with none of the flourishes the writer's hand should have had. He did not believe it, on the narrow grounds that a mahō-tsukai now has her name and they had fought mahō-tsukai on the water not long before. It was Doji Setsuna who asked the useful question — whether he had anything else in that hand, to set beside it. He did not. Everything he owns is back at the Emerald Champion's castle. There was nothing to compare.

Then he slept, and dreamed her: walls of black stone with mortar a dark reddish colour, a place that reminded him of somewhere terrible he could not name, and Hida Chizuzu straining against her bonds and hammering on a wooden door, demanding to be let out so she could fight her way clear. He woke on the gong with his strife halved and not rested. She is alive in it, and she is furious.

He decided to finish at the Castle of the Swift Sword before riding for the Crab lands, because that duty was owed first. He also offered to teach Ryu and So to write, on the grounds that an Emerald Magistrate is best served by servants who can take down what they hear.

He had already offered Doji Setsuna something else. He demonstrated Coil and Serpent Strike on Ryu — the blow that takes the hand off the hilt without taking the hand — and asked what she would do if it happened to her, and offered to teach her to fight left-handed. She answered that she is not ambidextrous but has learned to use all her limbs, and agreed to train so far as it did not displace her actual duties. When he and Kakita Kazumi fell to arguing across her about whose duty it was to defend her — a Doji's words are their responsibility, and their life is mine against if a Doji is getting into a sword fight, they have failed — she observed that the Doji in question was an adult samurai, was standing right there, and could conduct her own affairs.

Morning came in on a gong half an hour before dawn. They found an open training ground easily; the castle is built full of them. She proposed a spar first, to calibrate. Bayushi Monban took the Air stance and went up onto the banister with emerald snowflakes falling off his feet. She hit him anyway. He answered with one deliberate strike that did no damage at all — a fingertip to the nose, delivered so that she would notice there was something different about him — and then she hit him again, spending a Void point to keep the extra die, hard enough that he felt the skin move. He went straight down on his face and apologised. She bowed and told him to get up: she had understood what the exercise was for, and she appreciated the deference.

Afterwards she tested him the other way, with a narrow question of Imperial law about mercantile border disputes — not a trap, but the kind of thing a man fresh from his studies ought to be able to work out from principle. He reasoned at it from first principles and got it wrong, and took it badly; he still carries the book with the ribbons in it. His own summary of the pair of them was that in public they are a nightmare, and that she is the one who can talk to people.

Dressing was its own lesson. Half her baggage train is clothing, and she could have gone to a nine or a ten; she chose an eight and a half, dressing down so as not to embarrass a castle that could not match her. Bayushi Monban's best was about a six, and the castle heard him shouting damn it, Ryu through a door at a servant who is not permitted in the samurai's quarters in the first place.

Kitsuki Wataru went looking for the Togashi Nun who brought them back and found her doing warm-ups in the mustering square with two others. There is a shrine in the corner of that square, clean and properly appointed and entirely unattended, with a copy of the Tao on it under a wax seal — which is ordinary, because every Lion dojo keeps a copy of the Tao and no copy is ever opened. What is not ordinary is that there is nowhere in the design for worshippers to stand. When he approached the book a monk put out a hand and shook his head. He gave the name he has taken, Chosun, said he hoped to be received by the Togashi, and asked their names; they invited him into the morning chanting instead, told him to observe today and take part tomorrow, and recited the verses from memory. The monk who brought them back deferred to them — she is under their tutelage herself, and she is still recovering what she used to know. The more I spend time with you, and hearing people talk, hearing the birds, feeling the breeze, I am starting to come back.

The Lion have no monastery here, which is precisely why the Shinseist pilgrims come: anywhere else in the Empire a fortress this size would have one. The Brotherhood keeps an unspoken tradition that one of theirs is always at that shrine, and that monk is the pilgrim, treated as the local abbot and the man the castle goes to when there is a problem with the Brotherhood. At present there are two of them. The rumour that keeps them arriving is that a chapter of the Tao is hidden somewhere in these walls, never found, and that whoever finds it could carry it to the Emperor and hold a lever over the Brotherhood and the religion of the Empire. Nobody knows whether it is real. Some call it a peasant legend.

Kakita Kazumi was working kata when Kakita Sugimasa found him — I heard there was another Kakita in the house — and was recognised as the man who beat Riku in one stroke at the Topaz Championship, which she still does not believe and has not forgiven. Kakita Sugimasa warned him that a Lion student is prowling the castle with a brother to avenge, and that the student had already challenged Kakita Sugimasa by mistake, being unable to tell one white-haired samurai from another. Kakita Kazumi said he does not go looking for fights and does not need to.

Then Kakita Kazumi gestured across the square to where Doji Setsuna was training, and offered her as a better source of gossip. Kakita Sugimasa looked over, went the colour of his own hair, and said: not that one. Not ever. And then: may you be warned — she will get you killed. We have already got her. Their family is like that. You should be worried, cousin. He made excuses and found somewhere else to be.

He is at this castle as the Crane observer sent when a peace was signed, kept on to sit still and hear the glories of the Lion recited at him. The court's reading is that his father sent him to be rid of an unwanted relative. The Lion call him the plucked crane behind his back. His white hair is dyed.

Breakfast produced a bowl of soup with a set of testicles in it. Bayushi Monban set the bowl down, unmasked, pointed at it and roared a challenge at the room like a lion; seven Lion lined up to take him up on it. The courtiers hid behind their fans and said they had no idea who could have done such a thing and that it was funny. An Ikoma Bard told him the truth of it afterwards: it had nothing to do with his office and everything to do with his personality, because he has insulted relatives of theirs in the capital. They also told him they hoped he was no relation to the plucked crane, who has taken far worse; that he and Kakita Kazumi were the second and third Crane with the nerve to stand in this castle in living memory; and that they had gone so long without one they had forgotten how much fun it is to bait them.

Set at Doji Setsuna's own place was a note in an elegant, formal, entirely proper hand, regretting that the writer could not come down to the dining hall and asking, if the young lady had a moment, for the pleasure of her acquaintance. It was signed by Kitsu Saemi of the Kitsu and gave directions to the castle shrine. Doji Setsuna countered with an invitation to tea, which was accepted, and they took tea together.

The real work of the morning was gossip. Loyalty Castle is a backwater for news and she was the only new thing in it, which made her the seller in a market of motivated buyers. Her first approach did not land, and it cost her: one of the courtiers there knows her marriage status and now holds it. What she did get was the shape of the room — that the Ikoma Bard was holding back the one thing she wanted and knew that she wanted it. She went around him instead, to a Matsu logistician, who confirmed prisoners had been taken and pointed her, from behind a fan, straight back at the Ikoma: the historian of that battle might be related to someone at this table.

So she paid. She found the Ikoma Bard in a reading room over a go board and let herself be taught the finer points of a game she had only the rudiments of, which is the frame such trades are conducted in. What they wanted was not Crane gossip — they know what a Doji will say about the Crane — but anything she had on Scorpion interests. She gave them true information about the right people and let it be out of date, and lowered their guard while she did it.

What she bought: Shinjō Harunobu is alive and in custody. He was taken at the end of the Battle of the Hanto River, north of the border across the Drowned Merchant River in Unicorn country, and is numbered among the hostages. It is not normal Lion practice to take captives at all, but those they take are treated as honoured guests. He is most likely with the army besieging in the north, under Akodo Sakura — and her companions may know something of him, because they are known associates of his eldest son, Akodo Akihito. Her mission will bring her close enough to the front that a letter could be delivered, and her seal is authority enough to arrange it.

She asked one more question: do they also have his horse. It was not mentioned, which does not mean no — it may simply have been counted among the war goods. Unicorn horses are not spurned, and the Akodo in particular have developed an appreciation for what her husband's family breeds and would like some for themselves, as breeding stock. The stable hands have already remarked on the animal she came in on.

The session closed with Bayushi Monban being comprehensively beaten in the courtyard, the duels and Kakita Kazumi's formal challenge still to come, and nobody yet on the road.

Setsuna

She found out her husband was a prisoner by buying it. Not in passing, not from a friend — she worked a room of bored courtiers, failed her first approach, was routed around a bard who knew exactly what he was holding, went to a Matsu logistician to find out who held it, and then sat down over a go board and paid in Scorpion secrets she had deliberately let go stale. Nobody in the Crane, the Unicorn or the Imperial service had thought to tell her. She is on no list that gets told things, and what she learns she will have to purchase or be standing next to when it is said.

The purchase was not free and she should count what it cost. One of those courtiers now knows she is married, which in a castle full of people who trade in exactly that is a hold on her that she did not choose to give. She got the better half of the trade and she knows it. It is still the first debt of the campaign, and she incurred it in her first real day of work.

Two assets came out of this castle. Akodo Masami gave her Lion travel papers unprompted — the difference between moving through Lion lands and being moved through them — along with a name at the War College and an open invitation to correspond. And there is now a route to her husband: a letter from the Castle of the Swift Sword, a named commander in Akodo Sakura, and the thing the bard handed over without being asked, which is that Akodo Sakura's eldest son is Akodo Akihito, and her own companions already know him.

The problem is not getting him out. It is getting him out in a way he could survive. He has already been shamed by being taken. If his release comes because a wife pulled bureaucratic strings and wrote the right letter, that is not a rescue, it is a second humiliation with her name on it. Any plan that ends in a signature is the wrong plan.

One liability, and it is closer than the Lion. Her half-brother Kakita Sugimasa is at this castle collecting insults and waiting for one big enough to draw on, which would land on the Crane and on her. But the thing to sit with is not his temper. It is that he looked across a training square, recognised her, went white, and told Kakita Kazumi that she would get him killed — we have already got her, their family is like that. Somebody who shares her father is frightened of what she is for. She has not yet given anyone a reason.
