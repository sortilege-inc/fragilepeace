Witnessed, Not Ruled

Session 53·2026-08-17

My honour will be at stake with what I notarise, and something that is untrue by omission is not something I will be able to notarise.

Doji Setsuna stopped being the judge of this and offered to be its witness instead, and the war ended inside the hour.

Before she called the tent back to order she took chapter and verse off Kakita Kazumi. He had found the passage Akodo Akihito was leaning on — a witticism about a duel without armies being called a war, and about losing a duel by coming prepared for a duel when the other party came prepared for a war. Akihito was playing on it and not using it as it was written. Akodo's own teachings draw a hard line between a duel and a war, and there is a difference besides between riding to another clan's land to issue a challenge and being the clan whose land is ridden into. Eloquent, and post hoc.

She offered Kakita Kazumi the floor to answer it himself. He declined: the Lion do not favour him, having had their favourite son blinded in one eye by his hand, and his speaking would cost more than it bought. He gave her the passage and let her carry it.

She was forthright with him about what she actually wanted. She wanted the two clans to settle directly rather than be ruled on, because a ruling would take honour off whoever it went against, and she meant to make that plain to them.

So she gave the floor to Shiba Midori, who put the Unicorn objection in one sentence: it is rather self-serving to walk into somebody's house, challenge them, and then call their defending themselves consent.

Over the recess Shiba Midori took tea with the woman who had been sitting at the Unicorn's elbow all week and who now has a name. Iuchi Yukiko is one of the grandmothers of the Iuchi, here for Shinjō Altansarnai as Akodo Kage is here for his own Champion — high standing, no authority to bind, and present so that a daimyō's weight can be felt without a daimyō's word being given. She is meishōdō, and a shugenja, so she and Shiba Midori understand the world the same way and by opposite methods.

She asked one question and kept asking it. Shinjō Altansarnai sent Shiba Midori for the land, the prisoners and the horses; was what was happening in that tent getting any of them back? Then she said the rest of it plainly. The Lion are in a trap. Two Crane are plucking at their fur, and the arbiter looks like somebody who would rather be right than have a peace — and being right would not bring back her dead grandchildren. What the Unicorn could give that cost them least was an acknowledgement of a victory the Lion had already won. Marriages and bloodlines she advised keeping off the table entirely, or they would all still be sitting there at Winter Court.

The rumour Kakita Kazumi had planted was doing its own work meanwhile — that the Lion had opened the marriage negotiation while Ikoma Anakazu was still married — and it had reached the entourages of both Champions, which is everyone who matters.

Then Doji Setsuna made the move the whole session turns on. She told the Lion that if both parties would rather reach a bilateral agreement, she would put her name to it as a witness instead of handing down a decision — a representative of the Emperor attesting that these were the parties and these were the terms. Not a judge's ruling. And nobody would need to write to the Imperial legions about an unsanctioned war.

Akodo Akihito took out a fresh quill and a fresh piece of linen and started again, briskly, like a man relieved to be doing business instead of pleading. If he did not have to appeal to an arbiter, and could negotiate with a peer, that was a different matter entirely. He opened with the borders: back to where they were before, near enough, with any adjustment left for later.

The prisoners went next. Shiba Midori wanted them all home; the Lion wanted the question deferred and wanted paying. They met at five. Five of high standing from each side stay until the matter is closed at Winter Court — where those five would be expected anyway — and everyone below that goes home. Shinjō Miharu and Shinjō Harunobu are both of a standing to be among them.

Then the horses, which the Lion meant to keep. An Utaku attendant said the thing that decided it: they would not choose a Lion for a master, and they would not hold their quality without the training and the bond they are raised to. Doji Setsuna helped from the side, remarking to Kakita Kazumi on how fickle the animals are and how much Unicorn help she had needed to learn Kogarashi. Shiba Midori put money on the table rather than argue, opened low, and carried it.

Akodo Akihito then let slip what the Lion would want in exchange: some redress on the original marriage contract.

Doji Setsuna asked him for a private word. Was he certain he wanted anything about that marriage on a document she was going to notarise? Her honour is staked on what she attests, and a thing untrue by omission is a thing she cannot attest to — so either she would be adding the context herself, or he would be bringing her an agreement that did not contain the passage.

The Lion withdrew it and put something else in its place. The Unicorn would acknowledge that the Lion had conducted themselves throughout as righteous, honourable and correct by the traditions of Rokugani warfare.

That is the trade, and both halves of it are face. The Lion get a victory and their conduct affirmed, in writing, over the Emperor's witness. The Unicorn get their land, their people and their horses, and nothing is written down about a bride who would not come. Everything underneath — the betrothal itself, the fault for the war, the territorial concessions the match was to have brought, the historical grievances, and what is owed to Asako Ikari — is referred on to the Emperor's administration and to Winter Court.

Otoya was at her elbow for the drafting. She wrote it out fair, and made it handsome while she was at it.

Then she bound it. The delegates were made to stand for their clans, so that the three sitting at that table carried the terms back into them, and Doji Setsuna, Shiba Midori and Akodo Akihito each staked ten honour on the thing holding. In the doing of it she closed a trap of her own: the Lion were left with a choice between giving ground and owing her, and she took the debt. Two boons, owed by the Lion, banked and unspent.

The treaty was signed. The exchange of horses and prisoners begins within days.

Far to the west, Bayushi Monban finished what he had started in the troll cave badly. He was plucked off the roof and held in a fist while the troll woman asked him, in broken Rokugani, why he had burned the house of people who had fed him and given him somewhere to sleep. He got loose, drew them out into the open, and the Earthen Queen came up the mountain and had them thrown off it. Then the Lord of Fire arrived to inspect the palace he had been promised, found four of his own spirits unable to say whether it was suitable, and was walked away from the question by a man who knew exactly how burnt it was. His fire went down the mountain looking for other things to set alight.

He had gone into a house that took him in, burned everything in it, and had its owners killed. Honour is internal, and nobody had to see it.

Setsuna

The case was never meant to be delivered, and today it did the work it was built for. The bad-faith lever, the unlawful-duel counter, the divorce that may never have happened — every one of them raised the price of being ruled against, and she named that price to Akodo Kage out loud two sessions ago: settle gracefully, with the Lion the ones to begin it, or take a ruling that will be a great deal harsher. Offering to witness a bilateral agreement was the relief at the end of all that pressure, and the Lion reached for it inside the hour and paid the Unicorn everything they came for. Iuchi Yukiko told Shiba Midori the arbiter looked like somebody who would rather be right than have a peace. That was a fair reading of the performance and the wrong reading of the intent, and it cost nothing to leave standing — a Unicorn who believes the bench leans their way is a Unicorn who stays helpful.

The trade is clean and it is worth naming exactly. The Lion bought a written victory and an affirmation of their conduct, over an Imperial witness. They paid for it with the land, the prisoners and the horses — which is everything the Unicorn came for. Face is the cheapest currency at that table and she has now spent a great deal of somebody else's.

The lever was her own honour, and that is the part to keep. She did not threaten the Lion with a ruling. She told Akodo Akihito what she could and could not put her name to, and let him work out that a clause about the marriage would oblige her to write down why it was false. He withdrew it himself. Nothing was accused, nothing was proved, and the most dangerous fact in the case never had to be spoken aloud in that tent.

Ten honour apiece, from her and Shiba Midori and Akodo Akihito. That binds the three of them and, through them, three clans. It is also the largest personal stake she has ever put on another party's good behaviour, and two of the three are people she has spent this negotiation manoeuvring.

And the Lion owe her two boons. Unspent, unspecified, and taken from a clan that has just been handed its dignity back in front of witnesses. That is worth more than anything in the treaty and it is the only thing in this session that is hers personally rather than the Empire's.

The case was spent rather than surrendered, and the difference matters less than she would like. Asako Ikari is struck from the record along with the betrothal, the fault and the concessions, and all of it goes to Winter Court — which is where she said it should go before the delegations ever sat down, and where she has now also stacked Toshi Ranbo, the Phoenix claim, the divorce documentation and the woman herself. Intending a thing is not the same as it being harmless. That is a great deal of weight on one season, and Akodo Kage will be at it too, with his documentation and his patience.

Asako Ikari is still nobody's priority, and the agreement just made her officially somebody else's problem. She tried to die of this. She has still not been found.
