Docks of Deception Unfold

Session 31·2025-12-09

Kubota's grand-niece was taken because the magistrates executed a dock boss, the ransom is in his hold, and Monban cut his sails to stop him delivering it.

Two men came out of the dark at Shiba Midori and reached behind their backs for something. You have come to challenge me for my life. I will sell it at a high price. She was alone, at two-to-one, without a bodyguard, and she knew it — and went at the nearer one anyway, which is the sort of thing her delusions of grandeur are for.

Her cut landed on an arm interposed between her blade and a body, and took a chunk out of something that was not flesh. She's got a sword, said the one she had hit. And his partner: Always stolen. Remember, the boss needs another. Then we get them and get out of here.

The weapons were wooden — two apiece, identical, no edge on them at all. They were not there to kill her. They fought in water stance and stayed in it because it was working, using Striking as Water to strip her resistance a point at a time, and adapted faster than she could change her plan. She bruised. She did not bleed. And when they had her down and could not be defended against, she refused to surrender, and took a critical strike for it, and went under anyway.

Meanwhile Doji Setsuna was getting the measure of the man in the harbour. Bayushi Monban knows Captain Kubota: an older Mantis gentleman whose reputation is the ability to acquire foreign and illicit goods, confident in his position and brash enough to try something like this deep inside the Empire. And his first mate was at the Topaz Championship — let us just say I have a trump card with him. If we need leverage, we have it. If we need an admission of guilt, we should be able to get it. He offered to hold the ship on his rank alone if it came to that.

Doji Setsuna knew the shape of the problem from her mentor's casework. Kitsuki Wataru had written up a matter in the Crane lands judged by three magistrates she knows: a pair of Crane caught with a shipload of art of unusual provenance, who said the Mantis had lied to them. The Mantis pulled rank — he is a daimyō's equivalent and they were not — and told the court they had come to him specifically because he can get such things, and that he held none of it now. Which was true, because he had already delivered it. The magistrates ruled for the Mantis. The lesson is the one the Kaeru have been living with for weeks: rank will get a magistrate aboard, but if you do not catch him with the goods, you have to let him go.

Down at The Docks before dawn there was no Kakita Kazumi, and something else was wrong that Doji Setsuna noticed while playing the naive visitor — there should have been small boats tied up all along here, and there were hardly any.

Bayushi Monban went up. Five range bands of air on Ride the Clouds, a burst of emerald snowflakes, and some of his hair emerald now where it had not been. From up there: no Kakita Kazumi on the rooftops where he was last seen, no sign of Shiba Midori on the boards, and movement on the big ship six range bands out.

Doji Setsuna changed the priority and pulled rank to do it. She had been careful all night to defer to the Kaeru's operation — I am not going to intrude; let us get you over to the docks — and she stopped deferring: an Emerald Magistrate is missing on these docks, and that outranks a smuggling case. Kaeru Haya took it at once. An Emerald Magistrate is missing. Yes, that does matter quite a bit. She turned to her man Koji and told him to bring everybody out with lanterns and search the docks end to end.

Then Bayushi Monban, flying, caught a scrap of fabric on a bridge pylon in the moonlight — navy blue with an emerald green border, which is what Shiba Midori wears on purpose, being an Emerald Magistrate who sometimes declines to be subtle.

He unmasked and screamed — my big sister! — gave away exactly where he was, and lost three glory doing it. And Shiba Midori, surfacing, heard him. She reached for her daishō by reflex and it was not there.

He found her by the sound of his own voice coming back at him — in a fishing skiff tied against the dock a good distance from where her robe had torn, curled between the benches among the fish buckets and the oars and two fishing poles. Nobody rowing. Nobody aboard at all. He landed on the prow hard enough to rock the whole boat, scooped her up, and jumped back onto the dock.

Bruises, blood at her mouth, no bleeding anyone could find. Her daishō gone. Her purse gone — though she had been robbed of her money hours earlier. Not even a tantō left on her. Doji Setsuna's examination found nothing useful; Shiba Midori treated herself and worked out that the sharp pain through her right side is a real wound and the rest is exhaustion. A rib, probably. Ten fatigue, and everything she carries a weapon in taken off her.

And Kakita Kazumi was still gone. We have another magistrate unaccounted for, and given the situation we have to treat it as though they are in danger until proven otherwise.

Then Koji came running. Captain Haya — the ship is pulling away.

At dawn the big ship swung off the dock to point down-river under sail, with a small pirate banner flying and a very familiar captain on the tiller. Bayushi Monban crossed five range bands of open air and landed on the deck in front of him, badge out. Anchor this boat. You are not going anywhere.

We must catch the tide, and the tide is already taking us. If I put the anchor down I will damage my ship. Are you and the Emerald Magistracy going to repair my boat? — Yes.

A young man came up from below decks. No. We cannot be delayed any further. My daughter will pay the price if we are not back to Slowtide Harbor in time.

I have no intention of stopping. Besides, you have not even advised me of what I am remotely accused of, aside from being an honest merchant captain.

Bayushi Monban cut the front sail, and then the other — the only one actually unfurled. Can you not feel the wind, you fool? It is blowing against us. There is no point in putting the sail down now. And: I am a Fleet Admiral. I am a daimyō's equivalent. You do not tell me what to do. — I am an Emerald Magistrate. I do tell you what to do.

He gave in. They may not work, but we will put them down. Brace yourselves, boys — if the Emerald Magistrate breaks the ship, he is going to pay me with a new one, and I cannot wait until your lord hears about this, because believe me, he will.

The anchors went over and the lines snapped taut and the ship pitched hard toward the shore. From the bow: rocks, and cut the starboard line. They cut it. Then the other went of its own accord. Careful — we have lost your anchor. And the ship gathered way again with nothing left to stop it. What do you want me to do? I am fresh out of anchors. — This is the Drowned Merchant River, and I have no intention of being drowned in it.

Then he explained himself, which is the part that matters.

His nephew's daughter has been taken by one of the local bosses and is being held for ransom, and the ransom is in his hold. And we have you to thank for it, as I understand it — because one of the dock bosses, Yaguro, was tried and executed, and Doji Setsuna is one of the three magistrates who judged him. With him gone the other bosses are getting big for their britches. They took the man's wife, who is Tortoise, and then his daughter, reckoning the child would be leverage against a father who is himself a dock magistrate. They named a considerable sum.

I happen to like my nephew. I happen to like my grand-niece. She is an adorable little girl and I do not want to see her suffer. So I agreed to take the job, because I am the only ship fast enough to get it done — and it would be a considerable bit faster if someone were not cutting holes in my sails. He is making nothing on the run. And on the suggestion that he should have gone to the capital for help instead: have you ever had one of your relatives in the hands of a tong? How many pieces do you think I would get my grand-niece back in?

Doji Setsuna would have read him for a lie if she had been standing there; Bayushi Monban tried a sentiment check and got told he was not dealing with a practised liar.

On the missing crew he was unrepentant and, as far as he knows, honest: his men were told not to get into drunken fights ashore, they have forfeited their pay, that stupid Miya will not release them, and he can work the ship shorthanded. He does not appear to know that Kaeru Haya arranged it to strand him. He had also told the dockmaster plainly that he meant to leave today.

I maintain that you have still not told me what I am accused of, nor presented anything resembling evidence. — I would have, except that you sailed out of the port.

They agreed to finish the conversation another time, and Bayushi Monban wrote him papers to present himself at the capital after his business at Slowtide Harbor and wait there for missives, which Kubota received about as well as might be expected. And when are you next going to be in the capital, young man? It could be months. Years. Interrupting my business.

Then the old man gave him something worth more than the papers. A word of advice. I know you think yourself cloaked in Imperial glory with that badge, and you take every chance to mention that you know the Emperor. You will not impress the real powers that way. You need to become somebody in your own right — somebody known for more than showing his tantō.

And then the trade, because he had one. If Bayushi Monban would think about not mentioning the damage done to the ship, he would think about not telling the truth about the last time they met, when Bayushi Monban was unable to keep his lunch down in front of him and ruined a good pair of boots. The same people who talk about you showing your tantō to an old lady will find it highly amusing that you cannot keep your stomach inside itself when faced with one of the most powerful men in Rokugan. You might acquire an interesting reputation that way.

My dear Mantis. You will learn soon enough who has rope and who has pull. And, flying off: we will meet again. — Oh, I am sure. Learn some manners between now and then. Stop damaging other people's property.

The ship went on down-river. Doji Setsuna's recommendation was that the Kaeru escort them back to their quarters and everyone go to bed. In two or three weeks the bill for a fleet admiral's sails will reach Bayushi Monban's lord.

Setsuna

The girl was taken because of a ruling she made. Captain Kubota says it to her yojimbo's face: Yaguro was executed by three magistrates and she was one of them, and with him gone the surviving bosses started taking family off the people who could not stop them — a dock magistrate's Tortoise wife first, then his daughter. She removed the apex predator from an ecosystem and did not stay to see what filled the space.

That is not an argument against the ruling. Yaguro earned it. It is an argument for knowing what a verdict does after it is delivered, and for being told, and nobody told her. She learned it at dawn from an angry Mantis whose sails her own bodyguard had just cut.

And the ransom was in that hold. If Kubota is telling the truth — and he read as a man who is not a practised liar — then the party spent this morning delaying the delivery that buys a child back, cutting the rigging of the fastest ship on the river, and putting both his anchors on the bottom. If he is lying, it is an extremely good lie, made under pressure, with a nephew brought up from below decks to say the same thing. She should establish which, quickly, and she should not send Bayushi Monban to do it.

The collection is a quota and it is nearly filled. The boss needs another. Then we get them and get out of here. Wooden weapons with no edge, because a body that has been cut is worth less than a body that has not. Kaeru Haya jailed half a Mantis crew to strand a ship, and somebody has been replacing them off these docks ever since. The ship that sailed this morning was shorthanded and its captain said so cheerfully. Whatever is being loaded to make up the numbers is being loaded onto something else.

Shiba Midori was stripped of every blade she carried and left alive and breathing in a boat. That is not what robbery looks like. It is what inventory looks like — and the only reason she is not aboard something now is that the men who took her ran out of night.

Kakita Kazumi is the one to worry about. He was placed on these docks as bait, at Kaeru Akiara's suggestion, by a man who had already told the watch that magistrates would be interfering — and Kakita Kazumi engineered the offer himself, which makes it his own trap as much as anyone's. He was uncounted all last night and it kept him alive. He is uncounted now, and nobody is looking for him but them.

And the through-line has not moved: bodies taken and not killed, Opium and occult goods riding the same hulls, a Mantis ship that sat too long and has now sailed shorthanded, and a rite that needs a fresh vessel every time a spirit is moved.

Two smaller things worth holding. Captain Kubota gave Bayushi Monban the truest advice anyone has offered him: that leaning on the Emperor's name impresses nobody who matters, and he needs to be somebody in his own right rather than a man known for showing his tantō. He also threatened to make that reputation worse, which means the least of the Bayushi story has reached a fleet admiral on the far side of the Empire.

And she should mark what she did well, because it was the one clean decision of the night: she deferred to Kaeru Haya's operation right up to the moment a colleague went missing, and then took the whole watch off it without apology, and Kaeru Haya agreed instantly. Authority spent once, at the right moment, on the right thing.
