The Imperial Herald, and the man who told the [[Emerald Magistrates]] that the writ they
had spent a war carrying was never issued. He took the office from his father
[[Miya Yoto]] at the last Winter Court, and holds close to the opposite of his father's
opinions: where [[Miya Yoto]] spoke well of rōnin and of the lesser clans, the son does
not, and his disgust at the sight of an unwashed Scorpion claiming an Emerald Magistrate's
authority was not concealed. He is a traditionalist, he loves the work, and deference to
the new title is noticed and rewarded.

He arrived at the [[Lion Clan|Lion]] camp with an honour guard twice the size of the
ceremony it interrupted, to deliver a command the two clans had already obeyed. Told that
the peace had been brokered on the authority of the late [[Miya Misato]], he answered that
she is a low-level functionary at [[Otosan Uchi]] who would never have been given such an
order — and then laid out the timeline that proves it, since word of the war reached the
capital less than a week ago and she left months before.

He read the reconstructed writ, allowed that the form was proper and the seal genuine,
observed that its owner was dead, and set his own seal beside it anyway. His ruling is
that [[Miya Misato]] acted on her own authority and not in a right mind, that the orders
were forged or sent without the Emperor's knowledge, and that the magistrates are not to
answer for having believed them. He does not intend to collect the life the Lion yojimbo
offered.

He does not read a volcano as an omen. He does read a career, and he said so:
*we shall be watching yours with interest.*
